Hackers breach Austrian Foreign Ministry website, access diplomats' contact details

Hackers breached an Austrian Foreign Ministry website operated by an external provider, exposing diplomats’ names and official contact details. Austria has contained the incident, but the perpetrators, entry method, and any connection to Russia remain unknown.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Hackers breached an Austrian Foreign Ministry website operated by an external service provider between November 12 and 13.
  • 02The incident exposed names and official contact information for ministry employees in Vienna and Austrian diplomatic missions abroad.
  • 03Austria’s Foreign Ministry said the breach was contained and that no personal information belonging to members of the public was compromised.
  • 04Authorities have not identified the hackers, determined how they accessed the website, or established whether information was stolen.
  • 05Austria notified its cybersecurity and data protection authorities, and said there is no public evidence linking Russia to the latest incident.

Hackers breached a website of Austria’s Foreign Ministry between November 12 and 13, accessing the names and official contact details of employees in Vienna and at Austrian diplomatic missions abroad. The site was operated by an external service provider. The ministry said it contained the incident and that no personal information belonging to members of the public was compromised.

The perpetrators have not been identified, and authorities do not know how access was obtained or whether any information was stolen. Austria notified its cybersecurity and data protection authorities, while the ministry said it would not disclose technical details for security reasons. The incident coincided with a Microsoft service outage, but Austrian authorities have not established a connection, and there is no public evidence linking Russia to the breach despite wider diplomatic tensions.

  • The compromised website was operated by an external service provider, highlighting that the affected ministry information was hosted outside the ministry’s directly operated systems.
  • The exposed information was limited to employees’ names and official contact details at both the Vienna headquarters and diplomatic missions abroad, while the ministry reported no public personal data was compromised.
  • The breach occurred during a roughly 24-hour Microsoft service disruption, but Austrian authorities have not established any connection between the two events.
  • Although Austria has previously attributed a separate Foreign Ministry espionage operation to Russia, officials said there is no public evidence linking Russia to this incident.

Hackers broke into a website used by Austria’s Foreign Ministry and accessed the names and work contact details of staff in Vienna and at Austrian diplomatic offices abroad. The ministry says no information about members of the public was exposed and that it has contained the problem, but it does not yet know who was responsible or how they got in. The incident matters because diplomats and foreign ministry staff handle sensitive international work, and it comes during increased tensions between Austria and Russia, though there is no evidence Russia was behind this breach.

Netskope One Enterprise Browser, Now With Agentic Capabilities

Netskope has added native AI assistant and agent capabilities to its Enterprise Browser, governed by the same browser, DLP, web-security, and AI guardrail policies as users. The update also adds on-device screen-sharing, phishing, and AI-extension risk protections to enable managed enterprise AI use without separate deployment or cost for existing customers.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Netskope has added an agentic assistant to Netskope One Enterprise Browser that can read the current page and perform multi-step tasks such as searching, clicking, and typing while the user watches.
  • 02Administrators can select Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google Gemini models for the browser agent, or add custom and self-hosted models through Netskope Skylight AI Gateway.
  • 03The browser agent is subject to the same controls as the user, including restrictions on typing, pasting, screenshots, and web access through Netskope One Next Gen SWG with real-time data loss prevention and threat protection.
  • 04Netskope Skylight AI Guardrails can filter prompts and responses, block harmful or policy-violating content and sensitive corporate data, and provide protection against jailbreak and prompt-injection threats.
  • 05New browser features include Screen Share Guard, native phishing detection, and AI Extension Risk Analyzer, which scores Chrome Web Store extensions and can block those that exceed an organization’s risk tolerance.

Netskope has added agentic capabilities to Netskope One Enterprise Browser, alongside its existing secure-access controls for contractors, personal devices, and web applications. The new browser side-panel assistant can read the current page and perform tasks such as summarising contracts, drafting follow-ups, creating spreadsheet tables, and navigating multi-step workflows by searching, clicking, and typing. Administrators can select Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google Gemini models, or use custom and self-hosted models through Netskope Skylight AI Gateway.

The agent is governed by the same browser restrictions as the employee, including policies for typing, pasting, screenshots, and website access. Its web activity still passes through Netskope One Next Gen SWG with real-time data loss prevention and threat protection, while Netskope Skylight AI Guardrails can filter prompts and responses, block sensitive or policy-violating content, and address jailbreak and prompt-injection threats. Netskope also added Screen Share Guard, on-device phishing detection, and an AI Extension Risk Analyzer for Chrome Web Store extensions; the company says the new capabilities are automatically available to existing customers without additional deployment or cost.

  • The browser agent uses the company’s own licensed AI account and API key rather than an employee’s personal or shadow AI account, allowing administrators to choose and manage the underlying model.
  • Existing restrictions on actions such as copying, pasting, typing, taking screenshots, and visiting websites also constrain the agent, so it cannot bypass controls that apply to the employee.
  • Risk prevention is placed at several points: AI Guardrails can stop unsafe requests before processing, while on-device models can warn about sensitive content during screen sharing and flag phishing pages before a password is entered.
  • Netskope says a single AI rule can apply across the browser assistant, public web AI applications, and AI features in corporate SaaS applications because they share one policy engine and platform.
  • The article states that the new capabilities are automatically available to existing customers without a new deployment or additional cost.

Netskope has added an AI helper to its work browser that can read pages, summarize information, and carry out multi-step tasks such as clicking, searching, and typing. It is aimed at employees, contractors, and people using personal devices for work, who may otherwise use personal AI tools or browser add-ons that can access company information. The browser applies company rules to the AI helper too, helping prevent sensitive data from being copied, shared on screen, or sent to inappropriate places. This matters because AI tools can act using a worker’s own account, so a mistake or unsafe request could expose company data or cause actions to be taken before anyone can stop them.
