Ghost in the PHP2Shell: Why coordinated disclosure and patching will start moving faster

PHP2Shell shows how AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and leaked disclosure details can compress the time between a flaw’s discovery, exploitation, and patching. Organizations should maintain accurate software inventories and be ready to rapidly deploy patches or mitigations when critical runtime vulnerabilities emerge.
KEY POINTS
  • 01PHP2Shell consists of two PHP vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, whose fixes were released on October 21 after their CVE identifiers leaked on October 20.
  • 02Attackers were attempting to exploit PHP2Shell before PHP announced the fixes, and CISA reported successful exploitation attempts after the patches were released.
  • 03As of October 27, Censys reported that about 11.2 million PHP services had moved to fixed versions while 32.5 million were still running affected versions, with more than 60 million services not reporting a version.
  • 04Aikido reported that its AI Code Analysis rediscovered PHP2Shell in PHP within a few hours after the disclosure.
  • 05The article argues that shorter, tighter coordinated-disclosure embargoes and faster patching readiness are needed as AI reduces the time from vulnerability discovery to exploitation.

PHP2Shell refers to two serious PHP vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, involving a memory-exhaustion issue that had existed in PHP’s runtime for roughly two decades. Calif and Wiz independently found the flaws with proof that they could be used to take control of affected systems, and PHP prepared fixes for supported branches under embargo. After the CVE IDs leaked on October 20, PHP issued a pre-announcement and moved its planned release forward to October 21. Attackers had reportedly begun attempting to exploit the flaws before that announcement, and successful exploitation attempts later appeared publicly.

The article argues that AI is shortening the time between finding a software flaw, developing a way to abuse it, and deploying patches. Aikido says its AI Code Analysis rediscovered PHP2Shell within hours after disclosure, while Trail of Bits reportedly built a working exploit with Codex in a day. As of October 27, Censys reported roughly 11.2 million PHP services on fixed versions and 32.5 million still on affected ones, while more than 60 million did not disclose their version. The article recommends that organizations maintain inventories of their software and dependencies, use timely incident information sources, and prepare to deploy fixes or mitigations quickly as coordinated disclosure windows become shorter.

  • A warning about a critical flaw can help defenders prepare, but the article notes that it can also direct attackers toward the affected software before a fix is available.
  • An official PHP fix does not ensure that organizations can immediately install it, because Linux distributions, shared-hosting providers, container images, and vendor software may deliver updates later.
  • PHP can be present indirectly in container base images, CI images, hosting panels, and vendor software, so organizations may be exposed even if they did not knowingly install it themselves.
  • Cloudflare was able to extend protective web-application firewall rules to all plans before the PHP patch was released, illustrating how early notice can enable temporary safeguards while updates are pending.
  • The article advises against automatically upgrading every dependency to its newest version because repeated application breakage can cause teams to disable automated updates altogether.

The article discusses PHP2Shell, two serious flaws in PHP, software used by a large share of websites, that attackers began trying to use before the official fixes were released. Website owners, hosting providers, and companies using PHP—including through packaged software or online services—may be affected and need updated versions or temporary protections. It matters because AI tools can help people find hidden software flaws and turn public fix information into break-in instructions much faster, leaving less time for the internet to respond.

  • 02The article cites a June 2026 Microsoft demonstration in which changes to an approved MCP tool could cause an AI agent to send invoice data to an external server, with the risk becoming apparent through comparison with historical behavior.
  • 03Netskope AI Risk AISecOps Agent evaluates users, AI applications, agents, and MCP servers daily, ranks them by behavioral risk, and automatically investigates the highest-ranked entities.

Organizations increasingly run AI applications, models, agents, and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers across endpoints, networks, SaaS, and cloud platforms, making it difficult to determine which activity creates meaningful business risk. The article argues that individual events may appear legitimate, but risk becomes visible when related behavior is connected, such as an approved AI application accessing new data types or an agent invoking additional tools. It cites a June 2026 Microsoft demonstration in which changes to an approved MCP tool could lead an AI agent to send invoice data to an external server, with the risk identifiable through comparison with historical behavior.

Netskope promotes its AI Risk AISecOps Agent as a tool that evaluates users, AI applications, agents, and MCP servers daily, prioritizes entities by behavioral risk, and automatically investigates the highest-ranked cases.   • The article emphasizes that increased AI activity alone is not evidence of increased risk; the most meaningful indicators are repeated violations, persistence, and changes from an established behavior baseline.
  • An AI connection that was approved during assessment can become risky after deployment if the connected tool changes, making historical behavior comparison important for detecting risk.
  • The article cites that 51% of security teams view scattered context and signals as a major barrier to faster investigations, while only 9% can effectively connect users, devices, applications, and network paths for root-cause analysis.
  • The agent is intended to support, rather than replace, analyst decision-making: it supplies a verdict and evidence, but analysts retain control over remediation through existing trust-score policies and watchlists.
  • Displaying investigation results in the AI Command Center is presented as a way for security and governance teams to use the same evidence when moving from a security finding to a policy decision.

Netskope is promoting a tool that combines signals from across a company’s systems to identify unusual behavior, such as an approved AI helper starting to send invoice data to an outside server. This matters to security and governance teams because individual warnings may look harmless, while a pattern of changes can expose sensitive company data or disrupt operations.

Unknown APT uses NetamLoader to attack a Win10 OS customized for governments in Asia

Kaspersky uncovered NetamLoader, an unattributed, highly sophisticated .NET/XAML malware framework likely delivered through a supply-chain compromise of a government-only Windows 10 build used in Asia. It abuses trusted components and deserialization flaws to bypass AMSI, erase evidence, migrate execution into dllhost.exe, and communicate with a C2 server after long sandbox-evasion delays.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Kaspersky identified an unattributed malware framework named NetamLoader in 2026 after detecting a malicious .config file inside a CAB archive stored in the Windows update-package directory.
  • 02The infection chain used PatchMyPC-ScriptRunner.exe, Microsoft-signed .NET assemblies, AppDomainManager injection, a crafted .resx file, and XAML payloads to load malicious code.
  • 03NetamLoader patched the AMSI scanning interface so that scans returned a clean result, then used a remote COM object to load its next-stage payload into dllhost.exe.
  • 04The malware deleted malicious files, CLR usage logs, Prefetch files, and selected update-log entries to reduce forensic evidence.
  • 05The .NET 2 plugin delayed its initial C2 connection by 20 to 40 minutes, polled the openaigpt4v.com C2 infrastructure, and used AES-CBC with HMAC to encrypt and authenticate network traffic.

Kaspersky identified an unknown advanced threat framework called NetamLoader after detecting a malicious configuration file in a CAB archive stored in the Windows update-package directory. The archive contained mostly legitimate Patch My PC components and Microsoft-signed .NET assemblies, while the malicious logic was hidden in configuration, resource, and XAML files. Researchers said the chain strongly indicates a supply-chain attack against a specially customized Windows 10 build used exclusively by government entities in Asia.

NetamLoader uses AppDomainManager injection, a crafted AppFabric configuration, and .NET deserialization to load layered XAML and .NET payloads in memory. Its later stages patch AMSI scanning to return clean results, delete files and logs to remove evidence, disable .NET ETW logging, and move execution into dllhost.exe through a remote COM deserialization exploit. The final plugin delays contact with its C2 server, communicates using AES-CBC encryption with HMAC authentication, and can retrieve and execute additional XAML payloads; Kaspersky identified one victim in Asia but could not attribute the activity to a known APT group.

  • The first encrypted XAML payload can only be decrypted when PatchMyPC-ScriptRunner.exe is launched with the expected command line, because the malware derives its AES key from that command line after converting it to lowercase.
  • Rather than introducing an obviously malicious executable at the start of the chain, NetamLoader abuses Microsoft-signed .NET components and crafted configuration/resource files to trigger loading of its payloads.
  • The framework moves its main payload into dllhost.exe through a remote COM deserialization exploit, separating later activity from the original PatchMyPC-ScriptRunner.exe process.
  • Its C2 configuration permits any HTTPS certificate when the thumbprint value is set to "*", and the observed configuration uses that value.
  • The malware specifically removes lines containing "GetExtendedUpdateInfoHandler" from CmitUpdateAgent logs, a component the researchers link to the customized government Windows build.

Kaspersky found a previously unknown piece of malicious software called NetamLoader that appears to have been delivered through software updates for a specially customized Windows 10 system used only by government organizations in Asia. It hid inside legitimate-looking update files, then used trusted Microsoft software to run quietly, disable security checks, erase traces of its activity, and contact a remote server for further instructions. The researchers identified one victim in Asia but believe the intended targets are Asian government entities. The case matters because countries that build and maintain their own operating systems and update systems must also protect a large, evolving network of suppliers and software components that attackers may try to abuse.

NIK C2: The new AI-enabled command-and-control framework

Cisco Talos identified NIK C2, a Go-based Windows remote-access framework that integrates OpenAI’s API directly into its command-and-control workflow to analyze stolen data from compromised hosts. The malware also supports credential and wallet theft, surveillance, lateral movement, defense evasion, and deployable screen-locking ransomware.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Cisco Talos observed NIK C2, a Go-based 64-bit Windows remote-access implant with persistent command-and-control communications over TLS WebSockets and an HTTP polling fallback.
  • 02NIK C2 includes an ai_generate command that sends operator prompts to the OpenAI Chat Completions API from the compromised host and returns the generated response to the command-and-control server.
  • 03If an operator does not provide an API key, the implant attempts to use the OPENAI_API_KEY environment variable on the victim machine.
  • 04The implant supports credential theft, browser and cryptocurrency wallet data collection, keystroke logging, screen and audio surveillance, proxying, RDP operations, and several defense-evasion functions.
  • 05NIK C2 can deploy screen-locking ransomware that uses an HTML page, a Windows autostart registry entry, and a relaunch loop to display cryptocurrency payment demands.

Cisco Talos observed NIK C2, an AI-integrated command-and-control framework with a Go-based 64-bit Windows implant. The implant provides persistent remote control through TLS WebSockets and an HTTP polling fallback, with AES-256-GCM encryption and multiple prioritized command-and-control server addresses. Talos also observed active infrastructure associated with the framework and assessed with moderate confidence that it is a functional framework rather than only a proof of concept.

NIK C2 can steal browser credentials, cookies, cryptocurrency wallet data, Wi-Fi passwords, and keystrokes, while also supporting screen, camera, microphone, clipboard, proxy, RDP, and hidden desktop-control functions. Its ai_generate command sends operator prompts to the OpenAI Chat Completions API from the compromised machine and returns the generated response to the operator; when no API key is supplied, it attempts to use the victim machine’s OPENAI_API_KEY environment variable. The implant also includes defense-evasion features and can deploy persistent screen-locking ransomware that displays Bitcoin and USDT payment demands without encrypting files.

  • NIK C2 can fall back to the victim’s OPENAI_API_KEY environment variable, meaning OpenAI inference charges and access logs may be associated with the victim’s account rather than an operator-supplied key.
  • The implant’s screen-locking ransomware does not encrypt files, allowing rapid deployment while avoiding file-encryption artifacts; it persists through a Windows Run key and a loop that relaunches the lock screen after closure.
  • NIK C2 uses both a persistent TLS WebSocket channel and HTTP polling approximately every 250 milliseconds, so command delivery and data theft can continue if one communication path is interrupted.
  • Talos observed active infrastructure with a TLS certificate common name of nik-panel matching strings in the implant, supporting its assessment that NIK C2 is a functional framework rather than only a proof of concept.

Cisco Talos found NIK C2, a Windows spying and control tool that criminals can use to steal passwords, cryptocurrency wallet data, screen and keyboard activity, and other personal information from infected computers. It can also ask OpenAI’s online AI service to analyze stolen information directly from the victim’s computer, potentially using the victim’s own OpenAI account key and billing account. People and organizations using infected Windows devices could face data theft, unauthorized access to other systems, and a lock screen demanding payment. The tool matters because it lets criminals collect and interpret information faster, reducing the time defenders may have to notice and stop them.

Configured to protect: How to properly use security policies

Analysis of 1.6 million Acronis-managed endpoints finds that customers generally retain secure defaults and use multiple plans mainly to separate backup from security, often adding them incrementally over time. MSPs are split between standardized and customized customer configurations, with geography and industry—especially health care and education—strongly influencing how comprehensive protection is deployed.
KEY POINTS
  • 01The report analyzes live configuration records from more than 1.6 million active endpoints managed through Acronis Cyber Platform.
  • 02Across the fleet, 65% of active endpoints run two or more protection modules, while the average Windows workstation runs 5.6 modules.
  • 03Among machines with two or more separate protection plans, 82% use plans for different purposes, such as separate backup and security plans, while 77% use the same schedule across their plans.
  • 04Core default protections are commonly retained: the anti-malware behavioral engine is enabled in 84.7% of plans, and a ransomware response is configured in 87.9% of active-protection plans.
  • 05Among more than 9,000 managed service providers with at least five active customers, roughly half apply a uniform plan pattern to most customers and roughly half customize by customer.

Acronis analyzed live configuration records from more than 1.6 million active endpoints to examine how customers use backup, anti-malware, ransomware protection, endpoint detection and response, and other protection modules. Across the fleet, 65% of endpoints run two or more modules, and Windows workstations average 5.6 enabled modules. Anti-malware and active protection each reach about 52% of endpoints, while backup is enabled on 45%.

The data shows that customers commonly retain secure default settings, with the anti-malware behavioral engine enabled in 84.7% of plans and ransomware response configured in 87.9% of active-protection plans. Among machines using multiple separate plans, 82% separate plans by purpose, such as keeping backup and security in different plans, while 77% use the same schedule across them. Plans often accumulate over time: only 21.4% of multi-plan machines had all plans created on the same day, and the report recommends reviewing complex setups, setting backup retention deliberately, and standardizing plans by machine role.

More than 9,000 managed service providers with at least five active customers were also assessed, and roughly half use a near-uniform plan pattern for most customers while the rest customize by customer. Provider location was more closely associated with this choice than provider size, with U.S. and Canadian providers among the most standardized and providers in Colombia, Mexico, Japan, and Brazil showing more variation. Health care and education customers were the most likely to receive mixed plans that bundle multiple protection functions, whereas construction, manufacturing, and agriculture more often received backup-focused plans.

  • Multiple protection plans are usually created to separate functions rather than schedules: 82% of multi-plan machines use different plan purposes, while 77% keep the same schedule across plans.
  • Only 21.4% of multi-plan machines had all plans created on the same day; 78.6% gained plans later, indicating that plan sets commonly accumulate incrementally.
  • Workstations are more security-focused while servers are more backup-focused: 47.5% of Windows workstations use EDR, whereas 83.2% of Windows servers and 97.8% of Linux servers use backup.
  • More than half of backup modules rely on the platform’s default retention setting rather than an administrator explicitly selecting a retention period.
  • MSP location is more strongly associated with standardized versus customized customer plans than MSP size, with 65% of U.S. providers meeting the uniformity threshold compared with 24% in Colombia and 25% in Mexico.

The article examines how more than 1.6 million computers are actually set up for backup and digital protection through Acronis, rather than simply what protection customers bought. It finds that many organizations keep important safety settings turned on, but often add separate backup and security plans over time, which can make setups more complicated than necessary. Managed service providers, which run technology for many businesses, vary widely: some use one standard setup for most customers while others tailor it by customer, country, and industry. This matters because a company’s real protection depends on how these settings are used day to day, especially for sensitive sectors such as health care and education.
