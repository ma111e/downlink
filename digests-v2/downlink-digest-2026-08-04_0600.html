CISA Adds Exploited N-able N-central Flaw to KEV After Customer Compromises

CISA added actively exploited N-able N-central flaw CVE-2026-18577 to its KEV catalog after a limited number of customers were compromised, enabling authentication bypass, admin access, and possible pivoting into managed endpoints. Organizations should upgrade to N-central 2026.3 HF1 and review Take Control activity and related indicators of compromise.
KEY POINTS
  • 01CISA added CVE-2026-18577, a high-severity flaw affecting N-able N-central, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after reports of active exploitation in the wild.
  • 02CVE-2026-18577 is an incomplete patch for CVE-2026-18556 and can allow authentication bypass and account takeover in susceptible N-central versions, with a fix available in version 2026.3 HF1.
  • 03Successful exploitation can let remote attackers gain administrative access to vulnerable N-central servers and use the Take Control feature to pivot into managed endpoints and deploy persistence mechanisms.
  • 04N-able reported a limited number of customer compromises and shared indicators including a file named “svchost.exe,” a service named “Cloudflared,” and four IP addresses associated with Mullvad or NordVPN exit nodes.
  • 05Huntress observed threat actors targeting the flaw across multiple organizations, including reconnaissance, process enumeration, and lateral movement, but said there is no indication of a broad, indiscriminate campaign at this stage.

CISA added CVE-2026-18577, a high-severity flaw affecting N-able N-central, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after reports of active exploitation in the wild. The vulnerability has a CVSS score of 8.2 and is described as incomplete patching for CVE-2026-18556. It allows authentication bypass and account takeover in susceptible versions of N-central, and N-able addressed the issue in version 2026.3 HF1.

Successful exploitation can let remote attackers gain administrative access to vulnerable N-central servers. Attackers can then abuse the built-in Take Control feature to pivot into managed endpoints and deploy persistence mechanisms. N-able said a limited number of customers were compromised through CVE-2026-18577, while Huntress observed targeting across multiple organizations but no sign that the activity had become a broad, indiscriminate campaign.

N-able shared indicators of compromise including a file named “svchost.exe” in device users’ documents folders, a registered service named “Cloudflared,” and inbound connections from four listed IP addresses. Huntress said the IP addresses were Mullvad or NordVPN VPN exit nodes and reported post-exploitation patterns such as reconnaissance for key servers, process enumeration, and lateral movement. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies are being recommended to apply fixes by August 6, 2026, and review N-central Take Control activity in their environments.

  • The flaw is notable because it resulted from incomplete patching of an earlier vulnerability, CVE-2026-18556, meaning systems thought to be protected by the prior fix may still require the newer 2026.3 HF1 update.
  • N-able’s indicators focus on activity after compromise, including a suspicious “svchost.exe” in users’ documents folders and a “Cloudflared” service, which makes endpoint and service review important in addition to patching N-central itself.
  • The attackers used legitimate-looking paths into victim environments, including N-central Take Control sessions tied to the default “MSP Support” username and VPN exit nodes from Mullvad and NordVPN.
  • Huntress reported targeted post-exploitation behavior such as looking for domain controllers and moving laterally, while also stating there was no sign of a broad indiscriminate campaign at the time of reporting.

A U.S. government cyber agency added a newly abused security flaw in N-able N-central, software used to manage other computers remotely, to its urgent warning list after some N-able customers were compromised. The flaw can let outsiders get administrator-level access to vulnerable N-central servers and then use the software’s remote control feature to reach the computers those servers manage. N-able says only a limited number of customers were affected, but the issue matters because tools built for trusted remote management can give attackers a powerful way to stay inside many networks once they break in.

Silent Push Enables Cybersecurity Companies to Build Proactive Security Products with First-Party Infrastructure Intelligence

Silent Push is expanding API access to its first-party infrastructure intelligence so cybersecurity vendors can build products that detect adversary infrastructure before attacks begin. The offering includes preemptive Indicators of Future Attack, historical DNS/WHOIS data, behavioral fingerprinting, Traffic Origin data, and integrations for automated and AI-driven security workflows.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Silent Push is expanding access to its proprietary first-party infrastructure intelligence platform for cybersecurity companies through flexible APIs and integrations.
  • 02Silent Push says its Indicators of Future Attack identify adversary infrastructure while it is still being built, an average of 104 days before it is weaponized.
  • 03The offering is designed for cybersecurity product and research teams as well as cyber threat intelligence organizations that want to enhance detection capabilities, accelerate research, and differentiate their offerings.
  • 04Key capabilities include API-first integration, MCP Server integration, historical DNS and WHOIS intelligence, behavioral fingerprinting, Traffic Origin data, and custom intelligence feeds.
  • 05Silent Push offers deployment options including direct Data API access, MCP Server integration for AI workflows, full platform access for research teams, and customized intelligence feeds.

Silent Push announced expanded access to its proprietary first-party infrastructure intelligence platform for cybersecurity companies. The API-first offering allows product teams, research teams, and cyber threat intelligence organizations to integrate Silent Push data into their products and workflows. The company says this data powers its own threat detection and research capabilities.

Silent Push contrasts its approach with traditional threat intelligence that relies on indicators after an attack has occurred. Its Indicators of Future Attack are described as identifying adversary infrastructure while it is still being built, an average of 104 days before it is weaponized. The company says this can help cybersecurity vendors deliver earlier detection, richer context, and more proactive protection for customers.

Key capabilities include API access for enrichment, scoring, infrastructure context, bulk lookups, and automated workflows, along with MCP Server integration for AI-native and agentic security environments. The offering also includes historical DNS and WHOIS intelligence, behavioral fingerprinting, Traffic Origin data, and custom intelligence feeds tailored to threat categories, geographies, or infrastructure patterns. Silent Push says deployment options include direct Data API access, MCP Server integration, full platform access for research teams, and customized intelligence feeds.

  • Silent Push is positioning its data as first-party intelligence that is not available through third-party feeds or recycled threat intelligence, making exclusivity a central differentiator for cybersecurity vendors.
  • The article frames the offering as useful not only for detection products but also for customer-facing threat intelligence and published research, indicating the data is intended to support both operational and market-facing use cases.
  • MCP Server integration is included alongside API access, showing Silent Push is explicitly targeting AI-native and agentic security environments as part of its distribution strategy.
  • The ability to tailor feeds by threat category, geography, or infrastructure pattern gives vendors a way to embed more specialized intelligence into products rather than relying only on broad, general-purpose data feeds.

Silent Push announced that it is giving other cybersecurity companies access to its own data through APIs and integrations, so they can add that data to their products and research work. The company says this data can spot online infrastructure that attackers are building an average of 104 days before it is used. This matters because security vendors and their customers may be able to find and block threats earlier, instead of waiting until after an attack has already happened.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

This is a company/product announcement about expanded API access to Silent Push’s intelligence platform, not a specific incident, vulnerability, breach, patch, or threat actor operation. It may be relevant to teams evaluating threat intelligence integrations, but it is primarily promotional and low urgency for most security professionals.
