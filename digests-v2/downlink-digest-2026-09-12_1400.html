Dutch NCSC: Critical Check Point VPN flaws exploitation is imminent

The Dutch NCSC warns that critical Check Point VPN flaws CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 are likely to be exploited soon and could enable remote code execution. Organizations should urgently apply Check Point patches or LivePatch protections and restrict Site-to-Site VPN access to trusted IP addresses.
KEY POINTS
  • 01The Dutch NCSC warns that exploitation of critical Check Point VPN flaws CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 is likely to occur soon, despite no public proof-of-concept exploit being reported.
  • 02CVE-2026-85102 involves improper certificate-data validation during VPN negotiation, while CVE-2026-85103 is a heap overflow in the VPN certificate ASN.1 decoder; both can enable remote code execution.
  • 03Affected releases include R81.20, R82, R82.10, R81.10.x, R82.00.x, and several end-of-support releases, while Check Point VPN R82.20 is not affected.
  • 04Check Point released fixes on September 9 through LivePatch Take 24 for supported releases and specified Jumbo Hotfix Accumulator or Spark build updates.
  • 05The NCSC urges administrators to install updates promptly and, for Site-to-Site VPN deployments, restrict VPN rules to specific trusted IP addresses.

The Dutch National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has warned that exploitation of two critical Check Point VPN flaws, CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103, is expected soon. Although no public proof-of-concept exploit has been reported, the agency assesses both the likelihood of exploitation and the potential impact as high. Check Point issued fixes for the flaws on September 9.

CVE-2026-85102 stems from improper validation of certificate data during VPN negotiation, while CVE-2026-85103 is a heap overflow in the VPN certificate ASN.1 decoder; both could allow remote code execution. Successful exploitation could let an attacker take full control of a system, view or change confidential data, and disrupt operations. Organizations are urged to apply updates promptly, and Site-to-Site VPN users are advised to limit VPN access rules to specific trusted IP addresses.

  • The NCSC rates both the likelihood and potential impact of these flaws as high even though no public proof-of-concept exploit has been reported.
  • Check Point LivePatch protections have reportedly been available since September 9 and can apply without rebooting, but only for R82.10, R82, and R81.20 and not for every configuration.
  • Organizations using the Site-to-Site VPN component can reduce exposure while patching by limiting VPN rules to explicitly trusted IP addresses.
  • Check Point VPN R82.20 is not affected, while several older end-of-support releases are listed as affected.

The Dutch national cybersecurity agency warns that two serious flaws in Check Point software used to connect remote workers to company networks may soon be abused by attackers. The problems affect several versions of Check Point’s network-security products, and an attacker could potentially take control of affected systems, access or change confidential information, or disrupt business operations. Organizations using the affected software should install the available updates quickly and limit connections to trusted internet addresses where possible.

CISA Adds 5 Actively Exploited Artifactory, ScreenConnect, and RouterOS Flaws to KEV

CISA added five actively exploited flaws in JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik RouterOS to its KEV catalog, citing attacks that enable authentication bypass, privilege escalation, remote code execution, and device takeover. Organizations should urgently apply vendor updates, with federal agencies facing patch deadlines in September 2026.
KEY POINTS
  • 01CISA added five actively exploited vulnerabilities affecting JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik RouterOS to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.
  • 02Attackers chained two newly listed Artifactory flaws with CVE-2026-82329 between August 15 and September 8, 2026, to obtain administrator control of self-hosted servers and deploy backdoors.
  • 03The ScreenConnect client flaw CVE-2026-84869 can, under certain circumstances, allow files to be transferred and executed through an active remote session without authorization or host confirmation, and ConnectWise said ScreenConnect servers are not affected.
  • 04CERT Polska observed unknown threat actors exploiting the MikroTik RouterOS flaws CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 to take control of vulnerable devices without authentication in an exploit chain dubbed MikroTrick.
  • 05Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies must patch the RouterOS flaws by September 13, 2026, the ScreenConnect flaw by September 14, 2026, and the Artifactory flaws by September 25, 2026.

CISA added five actively exploited flaws affecting JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik RouterOS to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. The issues include authorization and authentication weaknesses in Artifactory, an unauthorized file-transfer-and-execution condition in the ScreenConnect client, and RouterOS flaws that enable memory disclosure, denial of service, policy-mask changes, and privilege escalation. The additions follow reports that these flaws are being exploited in the wild.

Attackers were observed chaining the two Artifactory flaws with CVE-2026-82329 to obtain administrator control of self-hosted servers and deploy backdoors between August 15 and September 8, 2026. Wiz reported follow-on activity including persistent administrator accounts, malicious Groovy plugins for code execution, and Rust-based backdoors. Separately, Huntress linked ScreenConnect exploitation to three unrelated incidents involving malicious VBScript payloads, while CERT Polska observed attackers using the RouterOS flaws in an exploit chain dubbed MikroTrick to take control of devices without authentication. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies must patch the RouterOS flaws by September 13, 2026, the ScreenConnect flaw by September 14, 2026, and the Artifactory flaws by September 25, 2026.

  • The Artifactory incidents show that multiple individually distinct flaws can be chained to bypass access controls and reach full administrator control, rather than relying on a single issue alone.
  • The ScreenConnect issue affects the client installed on the host system rather than ScreenConnect servers, so organizations should not assume their server infrastructure is the only component requiring attention.
  • Observed Artifactory follow-on activity included persistent administrator accounts, malicious Groovy plugins for code execution, and Rust-based backdoors, indicating that compromise can remain after the initial access method is addressed.
  • CISA assigned earlier federal patch deadlines to the RouterOS and ScreenConnect issues than to the Artifactory flaws, with RouterOS due by September 13, 2026 and ScreenConnect due by September 14, 2026.

The U.S. cyber agency CISA warned that five software flaws in JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik RouterOS are being used by attackers in real attacks. Organizations that use these products could have their servers, remote-support computers, or internet routers taken over, have files run without permission, or have sensitive information exposed. In some cases, attackers have already created lasting administrator accounts and installed backdoors, which can let them return later. Users should install the available updates quickly, and U.S. federal agencies have specific September 2026 deadlines to do so.
