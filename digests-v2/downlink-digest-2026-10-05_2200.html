OpenAI is adding invisible watermarks to ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU

OpenAI will add invisible textGrain watermarks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex output in the EU, while API developers elsewhere can opt in. The watermark is statistical rather than definitive and can become unreliable after editing, translation, or short outputs.
KEY POINTS
  • 01OpenAI says it will add an invisible textGrain watermark to eligible ChatGPT and Codex text output in the European Union over the coming weeks.
  • 02The textGrain system subtly changes model word choices to create a statistical pattern that can later be detected without being visible when text is read or copied.
  • 03API developers worldwide can opt in to watermarking for supported models, but the feature is disabled by default, and detector access will initially be limited to approved researchers and expert organizations.
  • 04OpenAI reported that replacing 10% of words with synonyms reduced watermark detection from about 92% to 66%, while replacing 25% reduced detection to 17%.
  • 05OpenAI cautioned that an absent watermark does not prove human authorship, and a detected watermark does not identify the user, account, prompt, conversation, or extent of human editing.

OpenAI says it will add an invisible watermark to eligible text produced by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union over the coming weeks. Its textGrain technology subtly changes word choices to create a statistical pattern that can later be detected, without changing how the text looks when read or copied. API developers worldwide can opt in for supported models, but watermarking is disabled by default outside this EU rollout.

OpenAI acknowledges that the watermark can become much less detectable after normal editing. In tests of 400-token passages, replacing 10% of words with synonyms lowered detection from about 92% to 66%, while replacing 25% lowered it to 17%; shorter text and mathematics responses also produced weaker results. OpenAI says a missing watermark does not prove human authorship, while a detected watermark cannot identify the user, account, prompt, conversation, or how much a person edited the final text.

  • The watermark’s usefulness drops sharply after ordinary rewriting: changing one quarter of the words to synonyms reduced detection to 17% in OpenAI’s evaluation.
  • Detection depends on both text length and subject matter, with 400-token psychology responses detected more often than 200-token responses and mathematics performing worse because there are fewer alternative word choices.
  • A successful detection only indicates that a statistical pattern is present; it cannot identify the person, account, prompt, or conversation behind the text.
  • OpenAI is deploying the feature as a regional default in the European Union while making it optional, rather than enabled by default, for API developers worldwide.

OpenAI plans to add an invisible marker to eligible text made by ChatGPT and Codex for people in the European Union. The marker subtly changes word choices so approved researchers and organizations may later be able to tell whether text likely came from OpenAI’s tools, while developers elsewhere can choose to enable it. It is not proof of who wrote something because normal editing, translation, or short passages can make the marker hard or impossible to find.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

This is a specific product-policy rollout with relevance to AI content provenance and the practical limits of detecting generated text. It is not an active security incident or urgent defensive action, but the documented evasion through ordinary editing is useful context for professionals evaluating AI-output detection.
