SafePal data breach impacts 39,798 customers, stolen info for sale

SafePal says an order-tracking authorization flaw exposed order details for 39,798 customers, and a threat actor is now claiming to sell the stolen data. Wallet seed phrases, private keys, passwords, and payment data were not exposed, but affected customers should watch for targeted phishing and scam calls.
KEY POINTS
  • 01SafePal says a data breach affected about 39,798 customers who placed orders between March 2, 2025, and April 11, 2026.
  • 02The exposed information includes names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers, and purchase information, but SafePal says wallet seed phrases, private keys, passwords, bank account information, payment card numbers, government-issued identification numbers, and other credentials were not exposed.
  • 03SafePal says the breach resulted from an authorization flaw in the order-tracking function of a plug-in that allowed unauthorized access to another customer’s order information.
  • 04A threat actor claims to be selling the stolen SafePal customer data on a cybercrime forum, though BleepingComputer has not independently verified that the actor possesses the data.
  • 05SafePal says affected customers do not need to replace their hardware wallets or move cryptocurrency because of the breach, but customers who shared seed phrases or private keys in response to phishing should treat their wallet as compromised.

SafePal is warning of a data breach affecting about 39,798 customers who placed orders between March 2, 2025, and April 11, 2026. The exposed information includes names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers, and purchase information. SafePal says the incident did not expose wallet seed phrases, private keys, passwords, bank account information, payment card numbers, government-issued identification numbers, or other credentials.

A threat actor now claims to be selling the stolen SafePal customer data on a cybercrime forum, although BleepingComputer has not independently verified that the actor possesses the data. SafePal says the stolen information could be used for targeted phishing and other social engineering attacks, and customers reported SafePal phishing emails and phone calls as early as May. The company says it has taken down more than 30 fraudulent websites and phishing links tied to the incident.

SafePal says its investigation found an authorization flaw in the order-tracking function of a plug-in that allowed unauthorized access to another customer’s order information. The company also found a separate configuration error that stopped a data-cleanup process between September 2025 and April 2026, causing order data to be retained as far back as March 2025. SafePal says it fixed the vulnerability, added protections, purged personal data from active e-commerce servers for affected orders, and is working with a third-party security firm to validate the fix and review its order-processing systems.

  • SafePal’s online verification tool can help customers check exposure, but the article says the seller is also using order ID and shipping country details from stolen orders to prove the data is legitimate to potential buyers.
  • A separate data-cleanup configuration error made the breach broader because order data was retained as far back as March 2025 instead of being removed as intended.
  • Customers reported SafePal-themed phishing emails and phone calls as early as May, months before SafePal notified impacted customers on August 16.
  • SafePal’s guidance focuses on phishing risk rather than wallet replacement, because the company says the breach did not compromise wallet access or funds unless a customer already shared a seed phrase or private key.

SafePal says a flaw in its order-tracking system let someone steal order information for about 39,798 customers who bought products between March 2, 2025, and April 11, 2026. The exposed details include names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers, and purchase information, but not wallet recovery phrases, private keys, passwords, bank details, payment card numbers, or IDs. This matters because the stolen information is reportedly being offered for sale and could be used to send convincing scam emails or make phone calls that trick customers into giving away access to their cryptocurrency.

This is a specific, recent data breach involving nearly 40,000 SafePal customers, with stolen order data reportedly being sold and already tied to targeted phishing risks. It is especially relevant for professionals tracking cryptocurrency-related scams, customer data exposure, and third-party e-commerce flaws, though it is not a critical infrastructure-scale or widely deployed software exploitation event.

Anthropic’s status page classified Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork as experiencing a major outage, while Claude Console and the Claude API were listed as operational.

Anthropic did not disclose the cause of the outage while the incidents were under investigation. The article’s updates state that authentication issues were reported at 21:58 UTC and degraded performance was reported at 22:07 UTC. By 22:40 UTC, Anthropic confirmed that all services were restored.

Anthropic’s Claude service had a major outage that caused login problems, slow or failed loading, unfinished requests, and other errors for people using Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork. The problem began around 21:58 UTC on August 16, while Claude Console and the Claude API were still listed as working.
