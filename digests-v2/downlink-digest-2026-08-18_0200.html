HeliosAI Agent: Finally, a Tier 2/3 Analyst You Can Trust

Mitiga launched HeliosAI Investigation, a new HeliosAI Agent capability that guides Tier 2/3 analysts through cross-cloud and SaaS investigations using evidence-backed reasoning from its normalized forensic data lake. The release also adds natural-language log search and AI summaries for Workbench events.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Mitiga says HeliosAI Investigation is a new capability in the HeliosAI Agent and is generally available to all Mitiga customers.
  • 02HeliosAI Investigation is designed to guide Tier 2 and Tier 3 analysts through cross-cloud and SaaS investigations by surfacing leads, connecting identity activity across services, checking behavior against baselines, and showing evidence and confidence behind each verdict.
  • 03The article says HeliosAI Investigation works across AWS, GCP, Azure, Microsoft 365, Okta, and SaaS applications as one continuous investigation.
  • 04Mitiga’s latest HeliosAI Agent release also adds natural-language log search for plain-English log queries and AI Summary for Workbench Events to summarize groups of forensic events into a narrative.
  • 05Mitiga states the HeliosAI Agent is built on its full-fidelity, normalized Cloud Security Data Lake, which it says already cuts alert triage time by 90%.

Mitiga announced HeliosAI Investigation, a new capability in the HeliosAI Agent and part of the Helios AIDR operating model. The capability is generally available to all Mitiga customers and is positioned as a way to help Tier 2 and Tier 3 analysts move from alert triage into full investigation. Mitiga says the same engine already cuts alert triage time by 90%.

HeliosAI Investigation is designed to guide analysts through cross-cloud and SaaS investigations rather than only answering questions they already know to ask. The article says it can surface the next lead, connect identity activity across services, check behavior against a normal baseline, and identify related activity such as an OAuth grant tied to S3 access. It works across AWS, GCP, Azure, Microsoft 365, Okta, and SaaS applications as one continuous investigation.

The release also adds natural-language log search and AI Summary for Workbench Events to the HeliosAI Agent. Natural-language log search lets analysts ask plain-English questions to retrieve raw log data without writing queries, while AI Summary for Workbench Events turns groups of forensic events into a clear narrative. Mitiga says each conclusion and summary is grounded in its full-fidelity, normalized Cloud Security Data Lake and can show the evidence and confidence behind its findings.

  • Mitiga frames data normalization as a trust requirement for AI-assisted investigations, arguing that unstructured or uncorrelated telemetry gives a model more room to produce confident but unsupported conclusions.
  • The product’s emphasis on showing what raised or lowered confidence suggests Mitiga is positioning explainability as a core requirement for analysts deciding whether to escalate serious cross-cloud incidents.
  • Mitiga separates guided investigation from natural-language log search, indicating that it sees two distinct analyst needs: help discovering the next question and fast access to raw data when the question is already known.
  • The article positions HeliosAI Investigation as an extension from alert triage into full investigation, meaning Mitiga is trying to move AI support deeper into the incident response workflow rather than limiting it to initial alert handling.

Mitiga has released new features for its HeliosAI Agent, including HeliosAI Investigation, which helps security analysts look into suspicious activity across cloud and business apps such as AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Microsoft 365, Okta, and other SaaS tools. It matters because serious incidents often involve many small clues spread across different services, and this tool is meant to gather those clues, show the evidence behind its conclusions, and turn them into a clear story. The people most affected are Mitiga customers and the Tier 2 and Tier 3 analysts who investigate whether an incident should be escalated.
