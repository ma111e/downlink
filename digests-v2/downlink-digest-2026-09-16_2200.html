Anthropic wants Claude to analyze your bank account and financial data

Anthropic is testing Claude Money, an iOS feature that would let users link bank accounts and ask Claude about spending and financial planning. Details on supported banks, connection methods, availability, and privacy controls have not yet been announced.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Anthropic is testing a personal-finance feature called Claude Money that would let users connect bank accounts to Claude and ask about spending, plans, and more.
  • 02Claude Money appeared as a new Money section in the Claude iOS app, but the feature does not appear to be available to most users.
  • 03Anthropic has not disclosed which banks Claude Money will support, how accounts will be connected, or whether it will be limited to the United States or certain states.
  • 04OpenAI already offers ChatGPT Finances, which uses Plaid to connect bank accounts, credit cards, brokerages, and other financial accounts.
  • 05OpenAI says it does not train AI models on personal financial data and that ChatGPT Finances supports more than 12,000 U.S. financial institutions.

Anthropic is testing a personal finance feature called Claude Money that would let users connect bank accounts directly to Claude. A new Money section was spotted in the Claude app for iOS, alongside Chats, Code, Artifacts, Dispatch, and Cowork. The page says users can link accounts and ask Claude about spending, plans, and more.

The feature does not appear to be available to most users, and its references appeared in the app before an expected announcement. Anthropic has not disclosed which banks will be supported, how accounts will be connected, or whether the feature will be limited to the United States or certain states. The article compares it with OpenAI’s ChatGPT Finances, which connects bank accounts, credit cards, brokerages, and other financial accounts through Plaid and is said to support more than 12,000 U.S. financial institutions.

  • Claude Money references appeared in the iOS app before an expected announcement, leaving key operational details such as supported banks and account-connection methods undisclosed.
  • Anthropic’s planned feature closely resembles ChatGPT Finances, which already connects financial accounts through Plaid and supports more than 12,000 U.S. financial institutions.
  • The feature is not broadly available despite appearing in the Claude iOS app, indicating that the visible interface does not represent a general release.

Anthropic is testing a Claude app feature called Claude Money that would let people link their bank accounts and ask the AI about spending, plans, and other financial questions. The feature has appeared in the iPhone app but is not widely available, and Anthropic has not said which banks it supports, how it connects to accounts, or where it will be offered. It matters because it could give an AI service access to highly sensitive financial information, raising practical questions about privacy and availability, especially outside the United States.

Cryptography Is Negotiated: PQC Needs Network Data | Corelight

Post-quantum cryptography migration is not just a cryptographic upgrade: organizations need network visibility to identify negotiated protocols, certificates, and dependencies before quantum-safe changes can be deployed safely.
KEY POINTS
  • 01The article argues that cryptography is negotiated between communicating systems.
  • 02The article focuses on post-quantum cryptography, or PQC.
  • 03The article says that PQC planning needs network data.
  • 04The article is published by Corelight.

Corelight’s article, “Cryptography Is Negotiated: PQC Needs Network Data,” discusses post-quantum cryptography (PQC). Its central point is that cryptography is negotiated between communicating systems. The article connects PQC planning to the need for network data.

The article presents PQC as an issue involving the encryption choices systems make when they communicate. Network data is described as necessary for understanding those negotiated cryptographic choices. This makes visibility into network communications relevant to a PQC transition.

  • The article frames post-quantum cryptography as a network-wide transition problem because encryption settings are chosen during communications between systems.
  • The article emphasizes that network data is needed to understand those real-world encryption choices before planning a post-quantum cryptography transition.

The article explains that moving to new encryption designed to resist future quantum computers requires understanding how systems currently choose and use encryption when they communicate. This affects organizations that run connected systems, because they need clear network records to find old encryption and plan changes without disrupting important services.
