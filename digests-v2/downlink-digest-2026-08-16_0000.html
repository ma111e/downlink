Large-scale DDoS attacks disrupted Threema secure messaging service

Threema suffered severe service disruptions after large-scale DDoS attacks hit its infrastructure and colocation partner, with attackers repeatedly changing tactics to evade mitigation. The company has added specialized upstream DDoS protection to reduce the risk of similar outages.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Multiple distributed denial-of-service attacks targeted the Threema secure messaging service earlier this week, causing severe disruptions to communications.
  • 02Threema said the attacks were difficult to defend against because the threat actor persisted for an extended period and continually changed tactics to circumvent mitigation measures.
  • 03The attacks targeted both Threema and its colocation partner, Nine, and Threema said it was not entirely clear whether it was the primary target or one of multiple targets.
  • 04Organizations using Threema On-Prem did not experience issues because they rely on their own infrastructure.
  • 05Threema implemented specialized DDoS protection to filter attack traffic upstream and reduce the load on its infrastructure.

Multiple distributed denial-of-service attacks targeted the Threema secure messaging service earlier this week, causing severe disruptions to communications. Users began reporting service interruptions on Tuesday around 6 PM UTC, and Threema initially said the cause appeared to be a network outage at its colocation partner. Users in Switzerland, India, and China continued to report problems the next day, and Threema later confirmed it was facing a series of DDoS attacks.

Threema said the attacks were large-scale and targeted both its own service and its colocation partner, Nine. The company said it was not entirely clear whether Threema was the primary target or whether the attacks were directed at multiple targets. Organizations using Threema On-Prem did not experience issues because they rely on their own infrastructure.

The company said the attacks were difficult to defend against because they persisted for an extended period and the threat actor continually changed tactics to circumvent mitigation measures. An unrelated technical issue also prevented Threema from updating its current system status page, leading the company to take it offline until the problem was fixed. To reduce the chance of similar incidents, Threema implemented specialized DDoS protection to filter attack traffic upstream and reduce load on its infrastructure.

  • Threema’s status page showed no problems while users were still reporting outages because an unrelated technical issue prevented the company from updating the current system status page.
  • Organizations using Threema On-Prem avoided the disruption because their service depended on their own infrastructure rather than Threema’s affected infrastructure.
  • The attacks against both Threema and its colocation partner Nine made it unclear whether Threema was the primary target or affected as part of a broader campaign.
  • Threema added specialized upstream DDoS protection after the incident, indicating its existing defenses were not sufficient for this large-scale, shifting attack pattern.

Threema, a paid Swiss messaging app focused on privacy and security, had major service problems after large floods of unwanted internet traffic overwhelmed parts of its service and its hosting partner, Nine. Some users had trouble connecting or sending messages, while organizations using Threema On-Prem were not affected because they run it on their own infrastructure. This matters because even services built around secure communication can become hard or impossible to use when their networks are overwhelmed, disrupting real-world communications for customers.

New AmnesiaStealer macOS malware hijacks browser sessions via remote control

AmnesiaStealer is a new macOS infostealer spread through ClickFix lures that can copy victims’ Chromium browser profiles and let attackers remotely control hidden, authenticated browser sessions. This gives hackers live access to accounts using the victim’s existing cookies, device, and network context, going beyond ordinary data theft.
KEY POINTS
  • 01AmnesiaStealer is a new macOS information-stealing malware distributed through ClickFix campaigns that use a fake GitHub download page to deliver a password-protected ZIP archive.
  • 02The malware can collect data from 16 Chromium-based browsers, as well as passwords, cryptocurrency wallets, Apple Notes, documents, keychain data, Telegram sessions, system information, and browser profiles.
  • 03Jamf researchers found that AmnesiaStealer includes a stream_module retrieved with the remote_stream command that gives operators remote control over authenticated sessions from a headless browser instance.
  • 04The stream_module can duplicate user profiles from Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Vivaldi, Arc, Opera, Brave, and Chromium because they share the same DevTools Protocol, launch flags, and cookie encryption.
  • 05Jamf said AmnesiaStealer appears to be the first documented macOS malware to combine a cloned Chromium profile with Chrome DevTools Protocol-based live remote control through a hidden browser running on the infected computer.

A new information-stealing malware called AmnesiaStealer is targeting macOS users through ClickFix campaigns that use a fake GitHub download page. The lure drops a password-protected ZIP archive containing the AmnesiaStealer Mach-O payload. Jamf researchers found that the campaign used the same template previously used to spread the Atomic and MacSync infostealers.

AmnesiaStealer can capture the victim’s macOS password and use it to collect keychain data, browser profiles, Apple Notes, Telegram sessions, documents, system information, and cryptocurrency wallet data. It can exfiltrate cookies, saved logins, browsing history, bookmarks, extensions, local state, and other profile data from 16 Chromium-based browsers. The malware also identifies cryptocurrency wallet details by enumerating extensions and IndexedDB data.

A notable feature is its stream_module, retrieved through the remote_stream command, which lets an operator remotely control authenticated browser sessions from a hidden headless browser instance. The module can duplicate user profiles from Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Vivaldi, Arc, Opera, Brave, and Chromium, then connect to the operator through WebSocket channels and the Chrome DevTools Protocol. Jamf said this turns an infected host into a live, operator-driven browser using the victim’s authenticated sessions, and users are advised not to run terminal commands found online unless they fully understand them.

  • AmnesiaStealer’s live browser control is materially more dangerous than ordinary file theft because it lets the attacker use the victim’s existing logged-in sessions while preserving the victim’s browser, host, and network identifiers.
  • The malware’s browser-control feature works across multiple Chromium-based browsers because they share the Chrome DevTools Protocol, launch flags, and cookie encryption, so switching among those browsers does not avoid this technique.
  • On macOS 26, AmnesiaStealer has a fallback that can replace Chrome’s Safe Storage key with an attacker-supplied value, making previously stored cookies and passwords permanently unreadable while allowing the attacker to decrypt data later.
  • The campaign’s fake GitHub ClickFix lure reused a template previously used to spread Atomic and MacSync infostealers, showing that familiar-looking download pages and copied campaign templates remain a practical delivery method.
  • The article’s clearest user-facing mitigation is to avoid running terminal commands found online unless the user fully understands them, because the ClickFix command is what executes the loader that installs the malware.

A new threat called AmnesiaStealer is targeting Mac users through fake download pages that trick people into running commands and opening a protected file. Once installed, it can steal passwords, browser data, Apple Notes, Telegram sessions, documents, cryptocurrency wallet data, and other sensitive information. Most importantly, it can secretly open a copy of the victim’s already logged-in web browser so the attacker can use their online accounts as if they were sitting at the same computer. This matters because it can let criminals get into real accounts without needing to steal a password first, using the victim’s existing trusted device and internet connection.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

This is a specific finding about an actively distributed macOS infostealer using ClickFix lures and a novel live remote-control capability against authenticated Chromium browser sessions. It is broadly relevant to security professionals because it affects macOS users, multiple major Chromium-based browsers, and demonstrates a materially more dangerous technique than ordinary file collection.

  • Customers reported SafePal-themed phishing emails and phone calls as early as May, months before SafePal notified impacted customers on August 16.
  • SafePal’s guidance focuses on phishing risk rather than wallet replacement, because the company says the breach did not compromise wallet access or funds unless a customer already shared a seed phrase or private key.

SafePal says a flaw in its order-tracking system let someone steal order information for about 39,798 customers who bought products between March 2, 2025, and April 11, 2026. The exposed details include names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers, and purchase information, but not wallet recovery phrases, private keys, passwords, bank details, payment card numbers, or IDs. Mexico's Tax Authority, Sent From a Yahoo Account

Attackers impersonated Mexico’s tax authority using a real Yahoo Japan mailbox, so SPF, DKIM, DMARC and Microsoft composite authentication all passed while the actual identity claim was false. The phishing attempt relied on a clean PDF containing only a link to an aged dormant domain, showing why email authentication and file scanning alone can miss credential-harvesting lures.
KEY POINTS
  • 01A Spanish-language tax notice impersonating Mexico’s revenue service reached an accountant at a medical device manufacturer from a genuine free consumer Yahoo Japan mailbox.
  • 02SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and Microsoft composite authentication all passed because the message was sent through Yahoo Japan’s legitimate outbound infrastructure and was not spoofing the sender domain.
  • 03The display name and message body falsely claimed to represent the Mexican government and finance ministry, including a fabricated tax administration reference code.
  • 04The attached 3.2 KB PDF was structurally clean and contained only Spanish text instructing the recipient to click a link to view fiscal notification details.
  • 05The landing domain dyxcyk.com had been registered roughly eleven months before the message was sent and had no observed legitimate use, and Themis scored the message at 90% confidence for credential theft.

A Spanish-language tax notice impersonating Mexico’s revenue service reached an accountant in the administration function of a medical device manufacturer. The message genuinely came from a free consumer Yahoo Japan mailbox, so SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and Microsoft composite authentication all passed at the top confidence tier. The article emphasizes that the checks confirmed the mailbox and provider infrastructure, not the claimed identity of a government tax authority.

The deception appeared in the display name and message body, which claimed to represent the Mexican government and a finance ministry and included a fabricated tax administration reference code. The email also carried a 3.2 KB PDF that was structurally clean, with no JavaScript, form fields, or automatic open action. Its entire content was a Spanish instruction to click a link to consult fiscal notification details, plus a note that it should be opened on a computer.

The link led to dyxcyk[.]com, a short domain registered roughly eleven months before the message was sent and described as having no observed legitimate use. The article says this weakened simple domain-age heuristics, because the domain was not newly created but also had no real history. Themis scored the message at 90% confidence for credential theft, and the incident was resolved automatically as phishing with the message permanently deleted from the single affected mailbox.

  • A maximum authentication result can create false reassurance because it only confirms control of the sending mailbox, not whether the sender’s claimed identity matches a government agency or other institution.
  • Using a clean PDF as a simple text wrapper for a URL can bypass malware-focused attachment checks because there is no executable content, JavaScript, form field, or automatic action to detect.
  • Domain age alone is a weak trust signal because the landing domain was registered roughly eleven months earlier but had no observed legitimate use, making dormancy more useful than creation date by itself.
  • An external sender warning and fully passing authentication can both be correct while pointing in opposite directions, showing that provenance checks and user-facing sender warnings answer different questions.
  • A category mismatch between the claimed institution and the sending domain, such as a tax authority using free consumer webmail, is a cheap and actionable signal that SPF, DKIM, and DMARC do not evaluate.

An accountant at a medical device manufacturer received a Spanish email pretending to be from Mexico’s tax authority, but it actually came from a real free Yahoo Japan email account. Because the account was real, the usual email checks all said the message was legitimate, even though the name and message were fake. The email used a harmless-looking PDF that only told the person to click a link, which could have led to stolen login details. This matters because people can be fooled even when technical checks pass, especially when attackers hide behind real email services and old unused websites.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

This is a specific phishing case with broad relevance because it shows how attackers can pass SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and Microsoft composite authentication by using a genuine consumer webmail account. It is not a breaking major breach or critical vulnerability, but the concrete details around clean PDF link-wrapping, dormant domain use, and identity mismatch make it a useful read for security professionals.
