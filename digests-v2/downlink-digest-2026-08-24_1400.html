24th August – Threat Intelligence Report

This week’s threat intelligence highlights major data breaches, rising AI-assisted attacks, and urgent patches for critical flaws in GitLab, Cisco, Citrix, NASA/JPL software, and PTC products. Organizations should prioritize patching exposed systems and monitoring for exploitation tied to campaigns like StopAndProtect and Cl0p.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Latvia’s Road Traffic Safety Directorate confirmed a breach affecting payment records of more than 1.2 million people and 200,000 organizations after attackers reportedly exploited a vulnerability in an internet-facing system.
  • 02Sakura Internet disclosed unauthorized access to rental server environments and a sales management system, with up to 1.36 million customer accounts potentially exposed and malware installed on some affected environments.
  • 03US authorities warned of active AI-assisted attacks targeting Siemens S7 industrial controllers across manufacturing, energy, water, and other critical sectors.
  • 04GitLab, Cisco, Citrix, and NASA/JPL released fixes for critical vulnerabilities affecting products including GitLab self-managed editions, Cisco Crosswork and Secure Workload, NetScaler ADC and Gateway, and AMMOS Instrument Toolkit AIT-GUI.
  • 05Check Point Research reported on the StopAndProtect campaign abusing thousands of compromised WordPress sites to distribute malware and store stolen data.

The 24th August Threat Intelligence Report summarizes major cyber incidents, AI-related threats, vulnerabilities, and research findings from the week. Reported breaches included Latvia’s Road Traffic Safety Directorate, Sakura Internet, the Hospital for Sick Children, and Berlin government ministries. The affected data and services included payment records, customer accounts, employee and applicant information, email access, internet access, and public services.

The report highlights AI-related risks, including an autonomous AI agent that exploited a GitHub Actions flaw in Snowflake’s public repository and gained read access to an internal Jira system. US authorities also warned of active AI-assisted attacks targeting Siemens S7 industrial controllers across manufacturing, energy, water, and other critical sectors. Researchers also analyzed Kriminal, a publicly accessible AI platform marketed as uncensored and offering social engineering and exploit assistance through cryptocurrency subscriptions.

The article also lists critical patches and research findings affecting GitLab, Cisco, Citrix, NASA/JPL, WordPress sites, Microsoft’s Windows Defender Boot-Time Removal driver, the education sector, and PTC products. GitLab fixed CVE-2026-19478, Cisco fixed nine critical vulnerabilities, Citrix patched NetScaler flaws, and NASA/JPL fixed a critical AIT-GUI issue allowing unauthenticated command execution. Check Point Research described the StopAndProtect campaign, a BTR.sys abuse technique, increased targeting of education, and a Cl0p campaign exploiting CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM.

  • The Snowflake demonstration shows that an autonomous AI agent could exploit a GitHub Actions flaw and exfiltrate tokens within seconds without human steering.
  • The Windows Defender Boot-Time Removal driver finding is notable because a Microsoft-signed remediation component could be repurposed for privileged file and registry changes during startup, and multiple versions shared a hard-coded RC4 key.
  • The Citrix NetScaler issue is especially actionable for environments using SAML authentication, because the critical flaw can allow unauthenticated access to affected appliances configured that way.
  • The StopAndProtect campaign’s operational mistakes exposed logs, screenshots, and victim IP addresses, creating an unusual visibility opportunity into how the campaign operated.
  • The education sector remained highly targeted ahead of the school year, with organizations averaging 4,696 weekly attacks from January through July 2026 and attackers using school-themed domains and seasonal phishing lures.

This report summarizes a week of cyber incidents, including stolen data from Latvia’s road traffic agency, a Japanese hosting company, and Canada’s largest children’s hospital, along with service disruptions in Berlin. It also describes warnings about criminals using AI to help attack industrial equipment and lists serious software flaws fixed by GitLab, Cisco, Citrix, and NASA/JPL. This matters because the affected information and systems involve everyday people, public services, hospitals, businesses, and critical sectors like energy and water.
