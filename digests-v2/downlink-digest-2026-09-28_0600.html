CISA orders feds to patch exploited Citrix flaws by Wednesday

CISA ordered U.S. federal agencies to patch two actively exploited critical Citrix NetScaler RCE zero-days, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, by September 30. Organizations with internet-facing NetScaler appliances should assess for compromise and preserve evidence before patching where possible.
KEY POINTS
  • 01CISA added CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog and ordered U.S. federal civilian agencies to secure vulnerable Citrix appliances by September 30.
  • 02Citrix confirmed that both NetScaler vulnerabilities are being actively exploited and urged affected customers to install updated versions immediately.
  • 03The flaws can allow unauthenticated attackers to run code remotely on vulnerable NetScaler appliances; CVE-2026-88772 requires DTLS to be enabled.
  • 04CISA advised organizations to check for signs of compromise and preserve forensic evidence before patching where possible, because updates may reduce forensic visibility.
  • 05Shadowserver tracks more than 23,000 internet-exposed IP addresses with NetScaler fingerprints, although it is unknown how many are patched, vulnerable, or honeypots.

CISA has ordered U.S. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to secure vulnerable Citrix NetScaler appliances by September 30 after adding CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog. Citrix confirmed that both critical flaws are being exploited in zero-day attacks and urged customers to install updates immediately. The vulnerabilities affect NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway deployments.

Both flaws can let an attacker take remote control of a vulnerable appliance without needing to log in. CVE-2026-88771 affects deployments using default configurations, while CVE-2026-88772 requires DTLS to be enabled, which Citrix says is enabled by default on VPN virtual servers. CISA, Citrix, and CERT-EU advise affected organizations to assess whether their internet-facing appliances may already be compromised and to preserve forensic evidence before patching where possible, since updates can reduce forensic visibility.

  • Citrix warned that its generic indicators of compromise may miss real intrusions and may have limited forensic value, recommending experienced forensic investigators for potentially affected deployments.
  • Organizations may need to preserve evidence before applying updates because CISA said patching can result in a loss of forensic visibility.
  • CVE-2026-88771 affects NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments using default configurations, while CVE-2026-88772 depends on DTLS, which Citrix says is enabled by default on VPN virtual servers.
  • The disclosure page carrying Citrix’s active-exploitation warning reportedly includes a noindex directive that discourages search engines from indexing it.
  • Since November 2021, CISA has listed 26 actively exploited Citrix vulnerabilities, including six that ransomware groups abused.

U.S. authorities told federal agencies to quickly update Citrix NetScaler systems after criminals were found using two serious security holes to take control of them remotely without a password. The issue affects organizations that use these Citrix systems to manage network traffic or provide remote connections, especially systems reachable from the internet. Because thousands of such systems are exposed online and Citrix says signs of intrusion can be hard to detect, affected organizations may need expert help checking whether attackers were already inside before applying updates.

Bitget resumes Bitcoin withdrawals after $387.5 million crypto heist

Bitget resumed Bitcoin withdrawals after a suspected North Korean-linked breach of its wallet infrastructure led to the theft of approximately $387.5 million in crypto assets. The exchange says the flaw has been fixed, user balances are covered by its Protection Fund, and withdrawals for other assets will be restored on a phased schedule.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Bitget resumed Bitcoin withdrawals after suspending all withdrawals following unauthorized transfers from a limited number of its crypto wallets.
  • 02Bitget said its latest on-chain tracing and transaction classification found that $387.5 million was transferred to attacker-controlled addresses.
  • 03Bitget CEO Gracy Chen attributed the incident to North Korean hackers who allegedly breached a critical backend system in Bitget’s wallet infrastructure and spoofed transaction data.
  • 04Bitget said user account balances remain unaffected, its Protection Fund covers the financial impact, and trading and deposits continue to operate.
  • 05The exchange plans to resume withdrawals for Ethereum-related networks on September 29, selected USDT networks on September 30, and other tokens, fiat, and P2P assets beginning October 2.

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget resumed Bitcoin withdrawals after suspending all withdrawals following a major theft from its hot and warm wallets. The exchange initially reported \(351.6 million stolen, but later said on-chain tracing and transaction classification showed that \)387.5 million had been transferred to attacker-controlled addresses. Bitget said it fixed the security weakness involved and will restore withdrawals for other assets in stages.

Bitget CEO Gracy Chen attributed the incident to North Korean hackers, citing on-chain analysis and IP behavior patterns. She said the attackers breached a critical backend system in Bitget’s wallet infrastructure and spoofed transaction data, causing the exchange’s authorization process to move funds from compromised wallets. Bitget said customer balances remain unaffected, its Protection Fund covers the financial impact, and trading and deposits continue to operate.

  • Bitget kept trading and deposits operating while pausing withdrawals, indicating that its containment response was targeted at preventing further unauthorized outbound transfers rather than shutting down the entire exchange.
  • The incident affected several blockchain networks and assets, including Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BSC, Base, ETH, XRP, BNB, AVAX, USDT, and USDC, so recovery required a phased restoration of withdrawal services.

How Data Lineage Catches a File That’s Trying Not to Be Found

Netskope promotes its One Data Lineage capability, which combines inline, SaaS API, and endpoint telemetry to trace files even after local renames and edits break traditional filename, hash, and application-ID matching.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Files often lose stable identifiers as they move between applications and devices because application IDs, filenames, and content hashes can change after downloads, edits, renames, and uploads.
  • 02Netskope One Data Lineage ranks evidence by strength, favoring events that identify both a source and destination, then shared application object IDs, content matches under limited conditions, and finally name, location, and time correlations.
  • 03The product deliberately avoids treating matching content alone as proof that a file moved and keeps uncertain connections visually distinct from proven lineage.
  • 04Netskope combines inline network activity, SaaS API connector activity, and endpoint events to construct file lineage.
  • 05Endpoint events can record local file creation, editing, renaming, copying, deletion, and transfers through browsers, USB drives, network shares, or Bluetooth, helping link network events that otherwise appear unrelated.

Netskope describes how its One Data Lineage feature reconstructs a file’s path when it is downloaded, renamed, edited, and uploaded to another location. Application-specific IDs do not persist across services, filenames can change, and content hashes may change after edits or even during uploads. As a result, network-based monitoring can observe a download and an upload without being able to prove they involve the same file.

The product ranks available evidence, prioritizing events that identify both source and destination, followed by shared application object IDs and more limited content, name, location, and time matches. It avoids treating matching content alone as proof of file movement and marks uncertain connections separately from proven lineage. Netskope combines inline network visibility, SaaS application API activity, and endpoint events such as local renames, edits, copies, and transfers to create a continuous record; endpoint events are currently in beta for incident-triggered lineage.

  • A file’s content hash may change even without a user edit because some applications alter its bytes during upload, making hash-based tracking unreliable across services.
  • A complete-looking lineage graph can be less useful than an incomplete one if relaxed matching rules create false links between unrelated files.
  • Edits and renames are retained in a file location’s timeline rather than creating separate graph nodes, preserving investigative detail without cluttering the movement graph.
  • Endpoint visibility is specifically necessary when the evidence connecting a download and a later upload was created entirely on the device and never crossed the network.

The article describes Netskope’s way of following a file after someone downloads it, changes its name and contents on a computer, and uploads it elsewhere. This matters because ordinary network monitoring may see the download and later upload but cannot prove they are the same file after those changes. By also recording what happens on the computer itself, such as renaming, editing, copying, or moving files to a USB drive, the system can provide a more complete record for people investigating how sensitive data moved.
