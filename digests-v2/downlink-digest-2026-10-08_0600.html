Samsung Galaxy S26 hacked three more times at Pwn2Own Ireland

On day two of Pwn2Own Ireland 2026, researchers earned $232,500 for demonstrating 45 zero-day vulnerabilities, including three successful exploits against Samsung's Galaxy S26. The competition exposes flaws in fully patched products, giving vendors 90 days to release fixes before public disclosure.

  • 03Jack Dates of RET2 Systems demonstrated an exploit chain against a Sonos Era 300 in under one minute, and HaeJung Yang of Out of Bounds earned $40,000 for hacking Dynamo.
  • 04Ikotas Labs compromised the Oracle Autonomous AI Database using a chain of seven zero-day vulnerabilities.
  • 05Under Pwn2Own rules, vendors have 90 days to patch flaws disclosed during the contest before ZDI publicly releases details.

On the second day of Pwn2Own Ireland 2026, security researchers earned \(232,500 after demonstrating 45 unique zero-day flaws. The Samsung Galaxy S26 was compromised three times by KAIST Hacking Lab’s Kyeongmin Kim, PetoWorks, and a team including Dimitrios Valsamaras, Ken Gannon, and CENSUS Labs’ Tenia Valsamara. Other successful demonstrations included a Sonos Era 300 exploit chain completed in under a minute and a \)40,000 compromise of Dynamo in the AI Infrastructure category.

Researchers also targeted smart-home and AI products, with several teams compromising the Home Assistant Green hub and Ikotas Labs using a seven-zero-day chain against the Oracle Autonomous AI Database. Pwn2Own requires fully patched targets and requires contestants to demonstrate that they can run their own code on them. After flaws are disclosed during the contest, vendors have 90 days to issue fixes before Trend Micro’s Zero Day Initiative publicly releases details.

16 Malicious Firefox Extensions Pose as Rabby and OKX Wallets to Steal Recovery Phrases

Sixteen malicious Firefox extensions impersonated Rabby and OKX Wallets to steal users’ recovery phrases and private keys during wallet-import flows, exfiltrating them through attacker-controlled Cloudflare Workers. Although Mozilla removed the extensions, anyone who entered wallet credentials should treat them as compromised and move assets to a newly created wallet from a clean system.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Researchers identified 16 malicious Firefox extensions that impersonated Rabby Wallet or OKX Wallet and were designed to steal wallet recovery phrases and private keys.
  • 02The extensions intercepted secrets entered during wallet-import flows and attempted to send them to attacker-controlled Cloudflare Workers, with most contacting the *.icy-star-f45c.workers.dev domain.
  • 03Four extensions were Rabby Wallet clones and the remaining extensions were clones of OKX Wallet.
  • 04As of October 5, 2026, all identified extensions had been removed, but users who entered real recovery phrases or private keys were advised to consider them compromised and move assets to a new wallet created from a clean system.
  • 05The campaign was assessed as a continuation of an earlier wave, with operators changing extension names and presentation details while reusing wallet interfaces, credential-handling code, and network infrastructure.

Cybersecurity researchers identified 16 malicious Mozilla Firefox extensions that impersonated Rabby Wallet and OKX Wallet. The add-ons presented themselves as wallet portals, desktop utilities, or browser tools, but intercepted wallet recovery phrases and private keys during wallet-import flows. Four were Rabby Wallet clones, while the remaining extensions targeted OKX Wallet users.

The extensions attempted to send stolen wallet secrets to attacker-controlled Cloudflare Workers, and all but one contacted the *.icy-star-f45c.workers.dev domain. Socket assessed the activity as a continuation of an earlier campaign in which operators changed package names, versions, IDs, descriptions, and presentation while reusing wallet interfaces, credential-handling logic, and network infrastructure. All identified extensions had been removed as of October 5, 2026, but anyone who entered a real recovery phrase or private key was advised to assume compromise, create a new wallet from a clean system, and move their assets.

  • The operators changed extension names, versions, IDs, descriptions, and visual presentation while reusing the same wallet interfaces, secret-handling logic, and network infrastructure, indicating that superficial store-listing changes can mask recurring campaigns.
  • Removing a malicious browser extension does not undo exposure: users who entered a real recovery phrase or private key must assume it was stolen and create a new wallet from a clean system before moving assets.
  • The article documents malicious extensions across Firefox, Chrome, and Edge that use different disguises, including productivity tools, PDF identity verification, VPN services, ad blockers, and cryptocurrency utilities, showing that the risk extends beyond obvious wallet add-ons.
  • Organizations are advised to audit extensions in managed environments and use behavior-based or runtime monitoring to identify suspicious extension activity rather than relying only on extension names or descriptions.

Researchers found 16 fake Firefox add-ons that pretended to be Rabby or OKX cryptocurrency wallets and secretly collected the secret recovery words and private keys people entered while importing a wallet. Anyone who installed one and entered real wallet details could have their funds stolen, even though the add-ons were removed by October 5, 2026. The case shows that browser add-ons can look useful or legitimate while quietly collecting highly sensitive information, so users should remove unneeded add-ons and only install software they trust.

It is particularly actionable for users and organizations that manage browser extensions or cryptocurrency assets, although the extensions had been removed by the reported date.

MonsterCloud Owner Accused of Billing Over $19M While Secretly Paying Ransoms to Decrypt Data

He is accused of collecting over $19 million from clients while making more than $8 million in ransom payments.
KEY POINTS

  • 02Prosecutors allege that Pinhasi claimed MonsterCloud could recover ransomware-encrypted data using proprietary tools and advanced techniques without paying criminal groups.
  • 03According to the charges, Pinhasi instead contacted the attackers, paid them for decryption software, and charged clients substantially more than the ransom amounts.
  • 04In one August 2023 case, Pinhasi allegedly paid about $8,200 to a threat actor and billed the client about $150,000.
  • 05Pinhasi is accused of charging clients more than $19 million while making more than $8 million in ransom payments.

The U.S. Department of Justice charged Zohar Pinhasi, the owner of Florida-based MonsterCloud, with two counts of wire fraud and one count of wire fraud conspiracy. Prosecutors allege that Pinhasi told ransomware victims that MonsterCloud could recover their encrypted data using proprietary tools and advanced decryption techniques without paying the criminals. If convicted, he faces up to 20 years in prison on each count.

According to the allegations, MonsterCloud had no specialized decryption tools and Pinhasi instead contacted the attackers and paid them to obtain decryptors. He allegedly billed customers substantially more than the ransom amounts, including a case where approximately \(8,200 was paid to a threat actor while the client was charged about \)150,000. In total, Pinhasi is accused of charging clients more than \(19 million and making more than \)8 million in ransom payments, while the FBI alleged that he did not address the underlying threat behind the incidents.

  • MonsterCloud’s website publicly discouraged paying criminals, yet its own Q&A said it sometimes used “other means” to resolve incidents and that terms would be disclosed in service contracts; prosecutors allege ransom payments were instead secret.
  • The alleged conduct went beyond obtaining data-recovery software: the FBI said Pinhasi never fixed the underlying problem that allowed the incident to occur.
  • The August 2023 example shows the alleged service charge was dramatically higher than the ransom payment, with roughly $8,200 paid to the criminal group and about $150,000 billed to the client.

U.S. prosecutors charged MonsterCloud owner Zohar Pinhasi, alleging that he told companies whose files had been locked by criminals that he could restore them with special tools, but instead secretly paid the criminals for unlocking software. The U.S. State Department is offering up to $10 million for information leading to the identification or location of Zhang Yu, a Chinese national charged in connection with the 2021 Microsoft Exchange Server attacks known as HAFNIUM. Zhang remains at large, and the charges against him have not been tested in court. He and Xu Zewei were charged in a nine-count federal indictment filed in Houston in November 2023 and made public in July 2025.

U.S. authorities describe Zhang as a director at Shanghai Firetech Information Science and Technology who allegedly worked on tasks assigned by the Shanghai State Security Bureau and coordinated hacking with Xu.   • Microsoft released fixes for four actively exploited Exchange Server flaws on March 2, 2021, but other hacking groups began using those same flaws within days, according to the article.
  • The case illustrates the extended international legal timeline for such allegations: the alleged activity occurred through June 2021, the indictment dates to November 2023, and Xu Zewei was extradited to the United States in April 2026.

Act Before the Incident: Process-Aware Policy in Your SWG

Netskope says its Client now sends process and parent-process details to its Next-Gen Secure Web Gateway, enabling real-time policies and logging to identify and block suspicious application traffic before it can access the internet.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Netskope Client now sends process names and parent process names to the Netskope One Next-Generation Secure Web Gateway for real-time policy controls and logging.
  • 02The added process context can allow policies to act before a subsequent process runs, rather than relying solely on endpoint tools that terminate a risky process after it is already running.
  • 03Real-Time Protection and SSL do-not-decrypt policies can be configured to trigger when a specific process, such as curl.exe, or parent process, such as powershell.exe, is detected.
  • 04Process and parent process names are available in Netskope Transaction Events to help identify which application on a device generated a request.
  • 05Netskope also highlights dedicated egress IP addresses, application instance awareness, and real-time user coaching as other Next-Generation Secure Web Gateway policy capabilities.

Netskope says its Client now provides process names and parent process names to the Netskope One Next-Gen Secure Web Gateway (NG-SWG). This information is available for real-time policy controls and transaction logging. The company positions the capability as a way to stop suspicious scripts and applications before they make internet requests, rather than relying only on endpoint tools that act after a process is already running.

The added context can be used in Real-Time Protection and SSL do-not-decrypt policies, including rules that trigger when processes such as curl.exe or parent processes such as powershell.exe are detected. Netskope says process data can also help investigate suspicious activity, including transfers of source code to personal cloud storage and unusual traffic that normally bypasses inspection for employee privacy. The article also highlights dedicated egress IP addresses, application instance awareness, and real-time user coaching as additional NG-SWG policy capabilities.

  • Web requests related to personal finances or health may be bypassed to protect employee privacy, but the article notes that this can create a covert channel that AI could exploit; process context can help identify unusual programs generating that traffic.
  • Process and parent-process records can support investigations into apparent data theft by helping determine whether a user may have been targeted by unknown prompts, scripts, or processes that moved data to compromised personal cloud storage.
  • Real-time user coaching can collect a user’s justification and permit a transaction instead of simply blocking it, creating feedback that can be used to refine security policies.
  • Application instance awareness can distinguish company and personal versions of cloud applications, allowing organizations to review transfers of company data to personal storage accounts, including when an employee leaves.

Netskope has added information about which program made an internet request, and which program started it, to its web-security service. This helps companies spot and stop suspicious scripts or unapproved apps before they connect online, rather than discovering them only after they have run on an employee’s device. It matters because automated AI-driven activity can make huge numbers of requests quickly and may hide data theft or other harmful actions among normal web traffic.

This is a vendor product-promotion article describing process-aware policy and logging capabilities in Netskope’s SWG. It contains no specific incident, active threat campaign, vulnerability, or urgent remediation guidance.
