Hackers exploit critical Adobe Commerce flaw to hijack customer accounts

Attackers are already trying to exploit a critical Adobe Commerce and Magento flaw, CVE-2026-71362, that can let them hijack customer accounts without authentication. Site administrators should urgently apply Adobe’s August 2026 isolated security patches after updating to the latest supported -p release.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Attempts to exploit CVE-2026-71362 in Adobe Commerce and Magento have been detected, potentially allowing attackers to hijack customer accounts.
  • 02Adobe described CVE-2026-71362 as an incorrect authorization vulnerability that could allow elevated access to sensitive resources without authentication.
  • 03Sansec said its Shield web application firewall is already blocking exploitation attempts, despite Adobe saying it was not aware of exploits in the wild for the fixed flaws.
  • 04Sansec’s analysis of Adobe’s patch found that the flaw lets attackers switch a customer session to another customer account, giving access to the victim’s account and private customer data.
  • 05Website administrators are advised to apply the August 2026 security update for supported Commerce, Commerce B2B, and Magento release lines as soon as possible.

Attempts to exploit a critical vulnerability, CVE-2026-71362, in Adobe Commerce and Magento have been detected. The flaw could potentially allow attackers to hijack customer accounts. Adobe described it as an incorrect authorization vulnerability that could be used to gain elevated access to sensitive resources without authentication.

Adobe fixed CVE-2026-71362 as part of a security update that addressed seven issues in Commerce and Magento. Although Adobe said it was not aware of exploits in the wild for any of the fixed flaws, Sansec said its Shield web application firewall is already blocking exploitation attempts. Sansec said exploitation requires no existing account, administrator privileges, or user interaction.

After reviewing Adobe’s patch, Sansec said the issue involved Magento improperly handling customer identity in an account session. The company said the vulnerability lets attackers switch a customer session to another customer account, giving access to the victim’s account and private customer data. Website administrators are advised to apply the August 2026 security update for supported Commerce, Commerce B2B, and Magento release lines as soon as possible, after ensuring they are running the latest -p release for their supported branch.

  • There is a discrepancy between Adobe’s advisory and Sansec’s observations: Adobe said it was not aware of in-the-wild exploitation, while Sansec said its WAF is already blocking attempts targeting CVE-2026-71362.
  • The flaw is especially actionable for defenders because Sansec says exploitation requires no existing account, administrator privileges, or user interaction.
  • Applying the fix may require more than a routine package update, because Sansec says the monthly fixes are distributed as isolated patch files rather than a new security release or updated Composer packages.
  • Administrators must first confirm they are on the latest -p release for their supported branch before applying the corresponding isolated patch.

Hackers are already trying to take advantage of a serious flaw in Adobe Commerce and Magento, software used to run online stores. The flaw could let someone get into a shopper’s account and see their private customer data without needing an account or any action from the victim. This affects website administrators running supported Commerce, Commerce B2B, or Magento versions, who are being urged to apply Adobe’s August 2026 security update as soon as possible.

Hundreds of fake Chrome VPN extensions route traffic through a proxy

Researchers found 737 fake Chrome VPN/proxy extensions impersonating major brands and routing users’ browser traffic through a single operator’s SOCKS5 proxies, potentially exposing destinations, source IPs, and plaintext HTTP data. Google has removed some, but Socket says more than 500 remain available, so users should check for the listed extension IDs and reset Chrome proxy settings if needed.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Socket researchers found more than 737 Chrome Web Store extensions that impersonated well-known VPN and proxy services while routing users’ traffic through SOCKS5 proxies operated by a single provider.
  • 02The extensions impersonated brands including Proton VPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN, and Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 DNS resolver.
  • 03The extensions were downloaded nearly 75,000 times, mainly by Russian users looking for tools to bypass blocked services in the country.
  • 04Socket identified behaviors including routing all browser traffic through SOCKS5 proxies on port 1082, resolving proxy hostnames through Cloudflare or Google DNS-over-HTTPS, and advertising nonexistent premium server locations for subscription fraud.
  • 05Google removed more than 200 related extensions, but Socket said over 500 were still available in the Chrome Web Store and recommended users remove any listed extensions and confirm Chrome’s proxy configuration is normal.

More than 737 browser extensions on the Chrome Web Store impersonated well-known VPN and proxy services while routing users’ traffic through SOCKS5 proxies operated by a single provider. The extensions impersonated brands including Proton VPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN, and Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 DNS resolver. Socket researchers said the campaign used 40 publisher accounts and a shared analytics account.

The extensions were downloaded nearly 75,000 times, mainly by Russian users looking for tools to bypass blocked services in the country. Socket said that with all browser traffic forced through the relay, the operator’s server could read every destination, every TLS SNI value, the victim’s source IP, and any request body sent over plain HTTP. Based on strings found in the extensions, the campaign appears to have been an attempt to funnel customers to a subscription-based VPN service in Russia.

Socket identified several behaviors, including 520 extensions routing all browser traffic through SOCKS5 proxies on port 1082 and 104 extensions resolving proxy hostnames through Cloudflare or Google DNS-over-HTTPS. Some extensions advertised nonexistent premium servers in Japan, Singapore, Canada, Australia, and Turkey for subscription fraud. Google removed more than 200 related extensions, but Socket said over 500 were still available and recommended that users check for the listed extension IDs, remove any matches, and confirm Chrome’s proxy configuration is back to normal.

  • The campaign’s scale was obscured by fragmentation across 40 publisher accounts, but Socket linked the extensions through shared infrastructure, including a shared analytics account.
  • Even though the extensions used a proxy mechanism similar to legitimate services, Socket identified deception indicators such as brand impersonation, nonexistent premium locations, misleading reviewer disclosures, and remote configuration added after approval.
  • Some extensions used Cloudflare or Google DNS-over-HTTPS to resolve proxy hostnames, which Socket said helped protect the operator’s domain from scrutiny.
  • Because all browser traffic was forced through the relay, the operator could observe every destination, TLS SNI value, victim source IP, and any request body sent over plain HTTP.
  • The campaign appears to have targeted demand from Russian users seeking ways around blocked services, while strings in the extensions suggested an attempt to funnel customers to a subscription-based VPN service in Russia.

Researchers found more than 737 Chrome add-ons pretending to be well-known VPN and proxy services, including Proton VPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN, and Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1. These add-ons were downloaded nearly 75,000 times, mostly by Russian users trying to reach blocked services, and they sent people’s browser traffic through servers controlled by one provider. This matters because whoever ran those servers could see where users were going online, their internet address, and any unencrypted information they sent. Google removed more than 200 of the add-ons, but researchers said over 500 were still available, so affected users should remove them and check that Chrome’s traffic settings are back to normal.
