Police dismantle KillSec ransomware gang allegedly led by 16-year-old

Operation KillSwitch dismantled KillSec’s infrastructure, seized its dark-web leak site and 110 TB of stolen data, and led to three provisional arrests. Authorities allege the ransomware group, linked to roughly 500 successful attacks, was led by a 16-year-old.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Operation KillSwitch, conducted on September 30 by authorities from multiple countries, seized KillSec’s data leak site and server infrastructure.
  • 02Authorities provisionally arrested three suspects, searched eight properties in Greece, Romania, Spain, and the United Kingdom, and identified a 16-year-old as KillSec’s suspected main operator.
  • 03Hamburg Police said five servers were identified and shut down, including KillSec’s main server and servers allegedly used to store stolen data.
  • 04Law enforcement seized at least 110 terabytes of stolen data, while investigators have so far determined that around 500 KillSec attacks were successful.
  • 05KillSec allegedly breached organizations by exploiting software flaws and poorly secured internet-facing devices and platforms, then extorted victims by threatening to publish stolen data.

Operation KillSwitch, an international investigation led by German authorities, seized KillSec’s dark-web data leak site and server infrastructure on September 30. Authorities from Belgium, the United States, Finland, Germany, Greece, the Netherlands, Romania, Spain, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom participated, alongside Europol, Eurojust, Bitdefender, and Group-IB. Three suspects were provisionally arrested, eight properties were searched in Greece, Romania, Spain, and the United Kingdom, and investigators identified a 16-year-old as the group’s suspected main operator.

Hamburg Police said the investigation identified and shut down five servers, including KillSec’s main server and servers allegedly used to store stolen data. Law enforcement also seized at least 110 terabytes of stolen data to prevent continued unauthorized access, while investigators have so far determined that about 500 attacks were successful out of around 1,000 suspected attacks worldwide. KillSec is accused of exploiting software vulnerabilities and poorly secured edge devices and platforms to steal corporate data, then demanding ransom payments by threatening to publish that data; investigators also found that members used artificial intelligence to support their infrastructure and identify potential victims.

  • The alleged main operator was 16 years old, and a suspected developer was still a minor during some of the alleged offenses, highlighting that key roles in the group were reportedly held by teenagers.
  • Although investigators estimate about 1,000 suspected attacks worldwide, they had confirmed roughly 500 as successful at the time of reporting, indicating the investigation remains ongoing.
  • The seizure included at least 110 terabytes of stolen data, which authorities said was intended to stop continued unauthorized access to victims’ information.
  • Investigators found that KillSec members used artificial intelligence to build and maintain their infrastructure and identify potential victims.
  • The seized computers, servers, and data may identify additional victims, attacks, participants, and cryptocurrency proceeds beyond those already known to investigators.

Police from several countries shut down the online systems used by the KillSec extortion group, arrested three suspects, and say its alleged leader is 16 years old. The group is accused of breaking into organizations’ computer systems, stealing sensitive information, and demanding money by threatening to publish it. Investigators believe about 500 break-ins succeeded, including at least 70 involving organizations in Germany, and they seized 110 terabytes of stolen data to limit further misuse. The case shows that even young people can run large international crime operations that can expose organizations’ private data and cause major financial harm.

WordPress Backdoor Rebuilds Itself After Cleanup Using Files, Database, and Shared Memory

Sucuri uncovered the SC WordPress backdoor, which persists across files, the database, and shared memory so it can rebuild itself after cleanup and enable remote site takeover through an Ethereum-based C2 channel. Separately, the wpForo Forum plugin SQL injection flaw CVE-2026-1581 is being actively exploited.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Sucuri researchers identified the SC WordPress backdoor, which uses multiple copies across site files, the database, and shared memory to restore itself after partial cleanup.
  • 02The malware is installed as a fake Hyper Engine Kit plugin in both must-use and normal plugin locations, with additional loaders in .user.ini, WordPress drop-in files, and a theme file.
  • 03SC can hide from WordPress plugin and update screens, contact a command-and-control server through the Ethereum blockchain, fingerprint infected sites, create a hidden administrator account, and retrieve further payloads.
  • 04On servers supporting System V shared memory, SC stores its code in a RAM segment that can survive file deletion and database cleanup.
  • 05The unauthenticated SQL injection issue CVE-2026-1581 affects wpForo Forum versions through 2.4.14 and has been actively exploited.

Sucuri researchers described a WordPress compromise involving a backdoor dubbed SC that persists through at least eight locations across files, the WordPress database, and System V shared memory. Each surviving component can recreate the others, meaning the malware can return after partial cleanup without a new infection attempt. The components include loaders configured through .user.ini, WordPress drop-in files, a theme file, and duplicate fake Hyper Engine Kit plugins in both must-use and standard plugin directories.

The backdoor hides from plugin and update screens, communicates with a command-and-control server through the Ethereum blockchain, fingerprints infected sites, retrieves additional payloads, creates a hidden administrator account, and can run PHP code or inject JavaScript targeting visitors. On systems supporting System V shared memory, its code can remain in RAM after files and database content are deleted, while scheduled WordPress tasks can trigger redeployment. Separately, the unauthenticated SQL injection flaw CVE-2026-1581 in wpForo Forum versions through 2.4.14 is under active exploitation, with Previdian observing fewer than 20 attempts since July 3, 2026.

  • SC has no single removable component: deleting one copy can cause another copy in a drop-in, theme, database, archive, or shared-memory segment to recreate it on the next request.
  • Its use of System V shared memory means the malicious code can remain available even after both website files and database content are cleaned, and the segment may be owned by another account on shared hosting.
  • SC uses legitimate Ethereum blockchain infrastructure for its command channel, making its remote-control communications less dependent on a conventional dedicated server.
  • The infection can use scheduled WordPress tasks, including randomly named ones, so reinfection can occur on a schedule rather than only when a visitor loads a page.
  • The article reports fewer than 20 observed attempts to exploit CVE-2026-1581 since July 3, 2026, despite the flaw being under active exploitation.

Researchers found a harmful program on WordPress websites that copies itself into several places, so it can return even after someone deletes parts of it. It can give criminals control of an affected site, add unwanted code that targets visitors, create a secret administrator account, and remove other site add-ons. The case shows that cleaning an infected website may require finding and removing every hidden copy, and it comes as a separate flaw in the wpForo Forum WordPress add-on is being actively abused.
