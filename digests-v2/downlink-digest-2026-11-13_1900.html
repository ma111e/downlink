Trezor confirms crypto-stealing implants in three wallets, releases fix

Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 wallets sold by Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis contained cellular-connected hardware implants that could steal recovery phrases and passphrases. Firmware 2.13.1 blocks the known implants, but affected buyers should move funds to a new wallet because previously captured recovery phrases remain exposed.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 wallets sold by Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis contained malicious hardware implants designed to capture recovery phrases and passphrases.
  • 02The implants used an extra circuit board between the wallet’s main board and display to monitor screen activity, encrypt captured information, and send it through a mobile network to an attacker-controlled server.
  • 03Trezor released firmware version 2.13.1 on November 11, which it says prevents the identified implants from capturing information on updated devices.
  • 04The update cannot protect recovery phrases that may have been captured before installation, and Trezor advises CryptoBilis customers to move cryptocurrency to a newly created wallet with a new recovery phrase.
  • 05Trezor reduced its global reseller network by about 80% and is offering replacement devices to customers who purchased a Trezor wallet from CryptoBilis since March 2026.

Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 hardware wallets sold by Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis contained malicious physical implants designed to steal wallet recovery phrases and passphrases. The confirmed devices were purchased between August 20 and September 23, 2026, and Trezor identified two of the cases through owner-submitted internal photographs after examining the original device in its laboratory. Trezor said 48 CryptoBilis customers contacted the company after its October warning, while checks of devices from other regional resellers found no evidence of tampering.

The implant was an extra circuit board placed between the Safe 5’s main board and display, allowing it to observe information shown or entered on the screen without changing the legitimate firmware or Secure Element. It could encrypt captured recovery words or passphrases and transmit them through a mobile network to an attacker-controlled server, retaining the data and retrying if no connection was available. Trezor’s firmware version 2.13.1, released November 11, blocks the identified implants from capturing new information, but cannot protect secrets that may have been captured earlier; the company advises CryptoBilis buyers to move funds to a newly created wallet and is offering replacements for devices purchased from that reseller since March 2026.

  • The implants could steal secrets without altering Trezor’s legitimate firmware or Secure Element, showing that a device can be physically compromised even when its normal software remains unchanged.
  • Trezor’s firmware 2.13.1 can make the identified implants unable to capture new information, but it cannot undo exposure of recovery phrases or passphrases collected before the update.
  • Although only three compromised Safe 5 devices have been confirmed and no affected Trezor customer has reported lost funds, Trezor is offering replacements to all CryptoBilis buyers since March 2026 rather than only to confirmed cases.
  • Trezor found no evidence of tampering in devices inspected from other regional resellers, while still cutting its reseller network by about 80% to impose stricter supply-chain controls.

Trezor found that three Safe 5 crypto wallets sold by Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis had hidden extra circuit boards that could record a customer’s secret wallet recovery words and passphrases, then send them to criminals through a mobile network. The affected devices were sold between August 20 and September 23, 2026, and Trezor is offering replacements to CryptoBilis customers who bought devices since March 2026. A software update stops the known hidden boards from collecting new information, but anyone whose secret words may already have been recorded should move their cryptocurrency to a newly created wallet. The incident shows that a device can be altered before it reaches a buyer, putting digital money at risk even when the wallet’s normal software has not been changed.

Trezor confirmed a real supply-chain compromise affecting Safe 5 hardware wallets, where physical implants could capture recovery phrases and transmit them over cellular networks. The impact is currently limited to three confirmed devices from CryptoBilis and no reported Trezor fund losses, but affected users need to act because firmware updates cannot protect secrets captured before installation.

FBI flash alert says Artex tool automating cyberattacks at ‘machine speed’

The FBI warns that Artex, an open-source AI agent framework, is enabling criminals and state-backed groups to automate end-to-end cyber intrusions at machine speed with minimal expertise. Although its original GitHub repository was removed, forks and commercial variants continue to support campaigns targeting critical sectors worldwide.
KEY POINTS
  • 01The FBI issued a flash alert warning that the open-source Artex framework can automate multiple phases of cyber intrusions, from reconnaissance through data exfiltration, at machine speed.
  • 02According to the FBI, Artex can allow threat actors with minimal technical expertise to run complex, simultaneous operations, with some intrusions reaching privileged access in under three hours.
  • 03Although its original GitHub repository was removed, the FBI said Artex continues to spread through forks, localized variants, archived copies, and a commercial version.
  • 04Censys identified more than 1,600 publicly exposed Artex console instances, while noting that total deployments are likely higher because some systems are private or behind firewalls.
  • 05The article says Artex has been linked to attacks on South Korean and Japanese financial institutions and to campaigns targeting sectors including government, financial services, technology, defense, energy, health care, manufacturing, telecommunications, and legal services.

The FBI issued a flash alert warning that the open-source Artex AI framework is being used by cybercriminals and nation-state actors to automate cyber intrusions from reconnaissance through data exfiltration. The agency said Artex can reduce the time needed to chain attack techniques, with some intrusions moving from target selection to privileged access in under three hours. It also enables actors with limited technical expertise to conduct complex operations against multiple targets simultaneously.

Artex was released on GitHub in August and later removed by its developer after reported abuse, but the FBI said forks, localized variants, archived copies, and a commercial version remain available. Censys research cited by the article found more than 1,600 publicly exposed Artex console instances, while the actual number of deployments may be higher. The framework has been linked to attacks on South Korean and Japanese financial institutions and campaigns affecting sectors including government, finance, technology, defense, energy, healthcare, manufacturing, telecommunications, and legal services.

  • Artex’s availability persists despite removal of its original GitHub repository because forks, localized variants, archives, and a commercial edition continue to circulate.
  • The FBI said attacker-controlled Artex installations most often use Chinese AI models, particularly the DeepSeek Flash family, because of their capability, low cost, and lack of observable safeguards when prompts are framed as penetration testing.
  • Amazon observed a self-sustaining credential-theft operation in which stolen AI-model access keys funded the AI processing used to find additional credentials.
  • Artex agents have been observed mapping trust relationships between organizations and seeking weaker third-party systems, such as supplier portals, mobile backends, legacy services, and third-party integrations.
  • Although Artex activity can generate enough attempts to trigger defensive sensors, the FBI said its speed can still make response difficult.

The FBI says Artex, a freely available AI-based tool, is being used by criminals and state-backed groups to speed up break-ins to computer systems, from finding targets to stealing data. It has been linked to attacks on financial organizations in South Korea and Japan and has targeted government, energy, health care, technology, defense, and other important sectors. The tool can let people with limited technical skill run many attacks at once, making it harder for organizations to respond quickly. Even though its original online source was removed, copied and paid versions remain available.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

The FBI flash alert describes active, widespread abuse of Artex to automate intrusion activity across critical sectors, including reported campaigns against South Korean and Japanese financial institutions and a ConnectWise supply-chain incident. Its continued availability through forks and commercial versions makes the finding broadly relevant to security teams.

The incident is part of a wider surge in cyberattacks prompting Japan to fund stronger national cyber defenses and faster breach reporting.

  • 02The city said the leaked addresses belonged to people who received emails from its system between July 2026 and October 31, and warned residents not to open suspicious links or attachments in messages claiming to be from Osaka.
  • 03Arara said a November 2 cyberattack exposed 77 million email addresses from nearly 400 customers, including TKC and Sega, and was used to send millions of malicious emails impersonating credit card companies and financial institutions.
  • 04Japan’s prime minister announced emergency cybersecurity measures, including increased use of AI for defense, stronger information gathering, offensive cyber operations, victim support, and about $62 million in emergency funding.
  • 05Mihoko Matsubara of NTT said October accounted for 58% of Japan’s reported cyber incidents this year and that about 380 million pieces of personal data had been stolen so far this year.

Osaka said hackers accessed Arara’s email distribution service, which TKC Corporation uses to maintain the city’s online administrative services platform. More than 1 million email addresses belonging to people who received messages from the system between July 2026 and October 31 were exposed. The city warned its 2.8 million residents to avoid opening links or attachments in suspicious emails claiming to be from Osaka.

Arara said a November 2 cyberattack exposed 77 million email addresses across nearly 400 customers, including TKC and Sega, and that the service was used to send millions of malicious messages impersonating credit card companies and financial institutions. The Osaka incident occurred amid a wider increase in Japanese cyber incidents affecting organizations including Japan Airlines, Sony Network Communications, and Capcom. Japan announced emergency measures including greater use of AI for cyber defense, stronger information gathering, offensive cyberattacks, support for breach victims, and about $62 million in emergency funding.

  • A compromise at a single third-party email provider created a cascading impact across nearly 400 customers, including municipal services, major businesses, and other organizations.
  • The incident did not only expose email addresses: Arara said its service was used to send millions of fraudulent messages impersonating credit card companies and financial institutions, creating a direct risk of deception for recipients.
  • The scale of Japan’s reported incidents was highly concentrated in October, which NTT’s Mihoko Matsubara said accounted for 58% of all incidents reported in the country that year.
  • Matsubara warned that personal information already traded on the dark web has been used for fraudulent securities transactions and could be combined into detailed profiles of Japanese citizens.
  • Japan’s emergency response explicitly treats cyber incidents as a broader national problem rather than something individual affected organizations can manage alone.

Hackers broke into an email-sending service used by Osaka’s online city services, exposing more than 1 million email addresses of people who received city messages between July and October. The same incident may have exposed 77 million email addresses held by nearly 400 Japanese customers, including businesses and government-related organizations. ARTEX and beyond: AI-enabled penetration testing platforms

Cisco Talos reports that ARTEX, an AI-enabled penetration-testing framework, is being abused by criminal and China-linked actors to automate reconnaissance, credential attacks, cloud abuse, and post-compromise activity at machine speed. Organizations should prioritize identity and secrets protection, monitoring for rapid anomalous activity, and preplanned containment measures.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Cisco Talos reports that ARTEX is an AI-enabled penetration-testing framework that can use planner and worker agents to automate reconnaissance, vulnerability testing, and follow-on actions with limited operator input.
  • 02Public reporting and Talos observations associate ARTEX activity with financially motivated cybercriminals and China-nexus actors, including VAPOR PANDA, Storm-0940, and Storm-3358.
  • 03Reported ARTEX campaigns have used password spraying, exposed or stolen credentials, and application or authorization flaws to target organizations in sectors including government, finance, telecommunications, defense, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, and technology.
  • 04Talos identified more than 2,500 ARTEX-associated C2 IP addresses and observed overlapping infrastructure with tools including VShell, AdaptixC2, Mimikatz, Cobalt Strike, GoPhish, and Fscan.
  • 05Talos recommends protecting credentials and exposed secrets, hardening public-facing systems and cloud resources, monitoring for rapid unusual activity, and preparing procedures for fast containment.

Cisco Talos reports growing in-the-wild use of AI-enabled penetration-testing frameworks, including ARTEX, which can automate reconnaissance, vulnerability testing, and follow-on actions with little operator input. ARTEX uses planner and worker agents, supports different large language models, and records assets, findings, and progress in a shared graph. Although designed for authorized security testing, reporting and Talos observations associate its use with financially motivated cybercriminals and China-nexus threat actors.

Reported ARTEX campaigns have included password spraying, use of exposed or stolen credentials, exploitation of application and authorization flaws, cloud-resource enumeration, privilege changes, web-shell deployment, and remote-access tool deployment. Targets have included government, financial, telecommunications, defense, pharmaceutical, semiconductor, and technology organizations across multiple regions, with reported outcomes including data exfiltration and downstream cloud access. Talos identified more than 2,500 ARTEX-associated C2 IP addresses and recommends organizations protect credentials and secrets, harden public-facing and cloud systems, monitor rapid unusual activity, and prepare for fast containment.

  • In one reported case, an ARTEX agent progressed from searching public GitHub repositories for exposed credentials to exfiltrating data from Azure Key Vault within five minutes.
  • Dosu reported a separate ARTEX-related incident in which an attacker generated more than 80,000 requests in 90 minutes after moving from account creation and an authorization flaw into connected systems.
  • Talos found more than 2,500 ARTEX-associated C2 IP addresses, but cautioned that tools found on the same infrastructure do not prove ARTEX invoked them or that a single actor operated all of them.
  • ARTEX is not shipped with a traditional command-and-control framework, yet its extensibility can allow operators to connect compatible tool servers, helping explain its deployment alongside VShell, GoPhish, and AdaptixC2.
  • Talos found more than 700 internet-reachable PentAGI instances, highlighting that related AI-enabled testing platforms are already broadly exposed online.

Cisco Talos says ARTEX, a tool meant to help test computer security, is being misused by criminal groups and China-linked groups to automate break-ins. These groups have targeted organizations including government offices, banks, telecommunications providers, defense companies, drug companies, and technology firms in many countries. The tool can rapidly search for weak points, stolen passwords, and access to online services, making it easier for attackers to steal data or gain wider control before people can react.

Adapting to AI penetration testing orchestrators: The Modern Security Model

AI-driven penetration-testing orchestrators such as ARTEX are rapidly proliferating, enabling attackers to automate reconnaissance, exploitation, credential abuse, and lateral movement at far greater speed and scale. Organizations should shift to an identity-gated, layered security model using WAF and bot controls, anomaly detection, schema validation, threat intelligence, credential hygiene, and micro-segmentation.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Cloudflare reports that more than 50 families of AI-based penetration-testing frameworks attempted to scan its customers in the three days before publication, using thousands of IP addresses.
  • 02The ARTEX repository was removed by its creator on October 8, but Cloudflare says more than 12,000 repositories and several hundred unique forks now contain lineage from the original project.
  • 03Cloudflare observed incident-response cases in which attackers used static, non-expiring CI/CD tokens with broad cross-environment access to move from a hyperscaler environment into a customer’s Cloudflare environment.
  • 04In one investigation, Cloudflare observed ARTEX-associated activity creating Worker scripts, making more than 69 requests per minute across Cloudflare infrastructure, and issuing 400 database-dump requests in an hour.
  • 05The article recommends a six-part security model that includes WAF rate limits and anomaly detection, bot defenses, identity-gated access and micro-segmentation, API schema validation and mutual TLS, and threat intelligence.

Cloudflare reports a rapid increase in AI-based penetration-testing and exploitation orchestrators, with more than 50 framework families scanning its customers globally in three days and thousands of associated IP addresses. ARTEX, an open-source agentic penetration-testing system, was removed by its creator on October 8, but Cloudflare says its removal was followed by more than 12,000 repositories and several hundred unique forks with lineage from the original project. Daily requests associated with AI pentesting and exploitation tools have grown 30-fold since October 1.

These frameworks coordinate multiple AI agents to map targets, identify potential weaknesses, validate findings, and carry out actions in parallel. Cloudflare observed incident-response cases where attackers used static, non-expiring CI/CD tokens with broad access to move from a hyperscaler environment into a customer’s Cloudflare environment, and one ARTEX-related case involved numerous Worker scripts, more than 69 requests per minute, and 400 database-dump requests in one hour. The article recommends a six-part Modern Security Model built around WAF controls and anomaly detection, bot defenses, identity-gated access and micro-segmentation, schema validation and mutual TLS, and threat intelligence.

  • Removing the original ARTEX repository appears to have produced a “hydra effect,” as Cloudflare observed steep growth in unique instances and forks after the October 8 takedown.
  • The article highlights that the most consequential risk is not only automated scanning from the internet: stolen static CI/CD tokens can give these tools broad access to chain actions across cloud environments.
  • ARTEX activity observed in one incident included creating separately named Worker scripts for different tasks, showing how an automated framework can organize and execute multiple post-compromise actions in parallel.
  • The recommended defenses emphasize making sensitive systems unreachable without verified identity and limiting movement between environments, because credential compromise is treated as an expected condition rather than an exceptional one.

The article says that AI-powered tools for testing computer systems are spreading quickly and can also be used by attackers to search for weaknesses, break in, and move through connected systems with little human effort. Cloudflare has seen more than 50 families of these tools scanning its customers, and investigators found one case where a stolen account key was used to create many malicious programs and try to extract database information. This matters to organizations and their customers because a single leaked or overly powerful access key can let an attacker reach multiple systems much faster than before. The article recommends stronger checks at internet-facing services, tighter limits on who can access sensitive systems, and separating systems so an intruder cannot easily move further after getting in.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

The article provides concrete Cloudflare observations of ARTEX-related activity and credential abuse, but its primary purpose is a broader trend and best-practice discussion rather than reporting an urgent, independently actionable incident. It is useful context for teams assessing automated reconnaissance and token security, but is not a must-read event alert.
