UK charges suspects linked to Russian Coms call spoofing platform

UK authorities charged five London suspects linked to Russian Coms, a caller ID spoofing platform used to make over 1.8 million scam calls by impersonating banks, telecoms, and law enforcement. The platform has been tied to tens of millions in losses and an estimated 170,000 victims worldwide.

UK authorities charged five people following a National Crime Agency investigation into Russian Coms, a caller ID spoofing platform used by criminals to make over 1.8 million scam calls. The five charged are Ayoub Sehailia, Zakkaria Sehailia, Usman Din, Denis Ozmus, and Fadila Salem, all from London. The charges include conspiracy to supply articles for use in connection with fraud, transferring or converting criminal property, and, for Zakkaria Sehailia, failing to comply with a notice to provide phone passcodes.

Russian Coms was established in 2020 and began as a handset before moving to a web-based application, with both versions marketed and sold. The platform allowed criminals to hide their identity by making calls appear to come from pre-selected numbers, often belonging to financial institutions, telecommunications companies, and law enforcement agencies. Since 2020, it has been linked to tens of millions in financial losses affecting an estimated 170,000 victims.

Before its shutdown, criminals used Russian Coms to make over 1.3 million calls to 500,000 unique phone numbers across more than 107 countries, including the United Kingdom, the United States, New Zealand, Norway, and France. The platform was promoted on Telegram, Snapchat, and Instagram and offered features including encrypted calls, a web phone, no-logs, international calls, voice-changing services, instant handset wipes, and 24⁄7 support. Its shutdown was part of Operation Henhouse, which led to 290 arrests across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.

  • Russian Coms was run like a paid commercial service for criminals, offering six-month contracts, 24/7 support, encrypted calls, voice-changing services, and instant handset wipes.
  • The platform’s promotion on Telegram, Snapchat, and Instagram shows that access to scam-enabling tools was marketed through mainstream and social messaging channels, not only hidden forums.
  • The scam model relied on impersonating trusted institutions such as banks, telecom companies, and law enforcement, with victims persuaded to move money themselves to attacker-controlled accounts.
  • The platform had a broad international reach, with calls made to 500,000 unique phone numbers across more than 107 countries before its shutdown.

Cloudflare introduced Precursor, a client-side, session-based verification system for detecting automated or agentic behavior across a full web application. Precursor uses dynamically injected JavaScript to continuously collect behavioral signals as visitors interact with a site, extending client-side detection beyond specific Challenge or Turnstile moments. Precursor collects interaction signals such as pointer movement, keyboard activity, focus changes, and page visibility, then sends buffered data to an edge evaluation layer for analysis.
