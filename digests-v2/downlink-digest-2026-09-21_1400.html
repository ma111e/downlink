Microsoft fixes broken Excel copy and paste for all Office users

Microsoft released fixes for an Excel bug introduced by September 2026 updates that caused copy, paste, autofill, and formula dragging to fail silently across several Office versions. Users with Office 2016 or LTSC editions should install the relevant updates; Paste Special may temporarily work for some conditional-formatting workbooks.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Microsoft fixed an issue caused by September 2026 security updates that made Excel copy-and-paste operations, autofill, and formula dragging fail for some users.
  • 02The issue affected Excel 2024, 2021, 2019, and 2016, as well as Excel Online in Office Online Server, and failed paste attempts could occur without any error or warning.
  • 03Affected users must manually download and install KB5002665 for Office 2016 or the corresponding updated builds for Office LTSC 2019, 2021, and 2024.
  • 04Microsoft warned that Office 2016 and Office LTSC 2019 may still have paste failures in workbooks containing conditional formatting.
  • 05For the remaining conditional-formatting issue, Microsoft recommends using Paste Special as a temporary workaround.

Microsoft has fixed a known issue in Excel caused by September 2026 security updates, including KB5002914, that broke copy-and-paste, autofill, and formula dragging for some users. The issue affected Excel 2016, 2019, 2021, and 2024, along with Excel Online in Office Online Server. Paste attempts could fail silently, leaving the source selected while making no change to the destination and showing no error or alert.

Affected users must manually install KB5002665 for Office 2016 or the specified updated builds for Office LTSC 2019, 2021, and 2024. Microsoft said paste operations may still fail in Office 2016 and Office LTSC 2019 when a workbook contains conditional formatting. For those cases, the company recommends using Paste Special as a temporary workaround, with options such as pasting formulas, values, formats, or links.

  • The paste failure is especially easy to miss because Excel can leave the original content selected and make no change at the destination without displaying an error message or alert.
  • Microsoft’s fix requires affected users to manually obtain version-specific updates rather than relying on a single universal update.
  • Even after installing the available fixes, Office 2016 and Office LTSC 2019 users may still encounter failures in workbooks that contain conditional formatting.
  • For the remaining affected workbooks, Paste Special offers several targeted alternatives, including pasting formulas, values, formats, or links.

Microsoft fixed a problem caused by September 2026 updates that could make Excel fail to copy or paste information without showing an error. It affected people using Excel 2016, 2019, 2021, and 2024, as well as Excel in Office Online Server. Users need to install the matching Microsoft update, although some older versions may still have trouble when spreadsheets use special formatting; in those cases, a different paste option may help temporarily. This matters because people can lose time or unknowingly work with unchanged spreadsheet data when Excel appears to accept a paste but does not actually do it.

Google fined €403 million over location data privacy violations

Ireland’s Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million for GDPR violations involving insufficient transparency and excessive retention of user location data collected through Web & App Activity, Location History, and Android Location Accuracy. Google must bring its processing practices into compliance within six months.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Ireland’s Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million for multiple GDPR violations involving the processing of users’ location data.
  • 02The investigation examined Google’s Web & App Activity, Location History, and Android Location Accuracy features during the period from May 25, 2018, through February 4, 2020.
  • 03The regulator found that Google did not meet GDPR requirements when processing location data through Web & App Activity and Location History and failed to demonstrate compliance for Location Accuracy.
  • 04The DPC said Google did not provide sufficient transparency for all three features and retained location data from Web & App Activity and Location History longer than necessary.
  • 05Google must bring its data-processing practices into compliance within six months, and it says the case concerns historical policies that it has since updated.

Ireland’s Data Protection Commission (DPC) fined Google €403 million for multiple GDPR violations involving the handling of users’ location data. The inquiry began in February 2020 after complaints from consumer rights organizations and examined practices active from May 25, 2018, through February 4, 2020. Google has been ordered to bring its data-processing practices into compliance within six months.

The investigation covered Web & App Activity, Location History, and Android’s Location Accuracy feature. The DPC found that Google did not meet requirements for processing location data through Web & App Activity and Location History, failed to demonstrate compliance for Location Accuracy, did not meet transparency obligations, and retained some location data longer than necessary. The regulator said people could have been unaware that their location was used to influence ads or infer interests, reducing their control over personal data.

Google said the case concerns historical policies that it has since updated. It said it introduced easier location-data controls, including automatic deletion settings, and that Google Maps Timeline data is now stored on the device and deletes data older than three months. Google also said Web & App Activity does not save precise device location, but rather an estimated general area.

  • Google’s Location Accuracy feature was available even to people without a Google Account, extending the inquiry beyond account-based Google services.
  • The regulator said location information could be used to influence people with ads or infer their interests, illustrating that location data can affect more than map-related services.
  • Google says Google Maps Timeline data is now stored on the device and automatically deletes information older than three months.
  • Google stated that Web & App Activity no longer saves a device’s precise location, instead retaining an estimated general area.

Ireland’s privacy regulator fined Google €403 million for how it handled people’s location information, including data gathered through Google accounts, Google Maps, and Android phones. The regulator said people may not have understood that their locations could be used to shape ads or guess their interests, and that Google kept some of this information longer than needed. This matters because location records can reveal where people go, what they do, and their routines, so unclear controls can leave users with less control over private details about their lives. Google says these were older practices and that it has since added easier controls and automatic deletion options.

This is a significant, concrete GDPR enforcement action against Google involving location-data handling across widely used services and Android. It is not an immediate technical threat, but the size of the fine and the required compliance changes make it broadly relevant to privacy, security, and data-governance professionals.

⚡ Weekly Recap: Cisco 0-Day, AI Agent RCE, ClickFix Attacks, ClickFix Surge, and Browser Hijacks

This week’s security roundup highlights an actively exploited Cisco ISE authentication-bypass zero-day, zero-click RCE risks in AI coding-agent plugins, and a surge in ClickFix and browser-hijacking campaigns. The central takeaway is that attackers are increasingly abusing trusted software, supply chains, browser prompts, and exposed services, making prompt patching and stricter verification essential.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Cisco warned that CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity flaw in Identity Services Engine, is being actively exploited and can let an unauthenticated remote attacker bypass the web-based management interface.
  • 02Researchers demonstrated Plugin4Shell, a zero-click remote-code-execution issue affecting Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, and Google Gemini CLI by bypassing plugin SHA-pinning verification.
  • 03Brevo said that a compromised Cloudflare API key allowed an attacker to inject malicious ClickFix scripts into Brevo pages, embedded customer JavaScript files, and more than 100,000 customer websites for about five and a half hours.
  • 04A Brazilian banking-malware operation tracked as REF9334 has used the KREMLIN toolkit to install malicious Chrome and Edge extensions that steal credentials, session tokens, and sensitive data.
  • 05Aikido Security found four npm packages containing the previously identified Shai-Hulud worm more than three months after its payload hash had been published and indexed.

Cisco warned that CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity flaw in Identity Services Engine, is under active exploitation and can allow an unauthenticated remote attacker to bypass the web-based management interface through a crafted API request. The roundup also highlights Plugin4Shell, which researchers said enables zero-click remote code execution in Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, and Gemini CLI by bypassing plugin SHA-pinning checks. These issues illustrate how weaknesses in trusted administration systems, plugins, and software supply chains can create short paths to unauthorized access.

ClickFix activity remained a major theme, including a Google Docs sidebar that instructed targets to copy malicious commands and a Brevo supply-chain incident that injected fake Cloudflare prompts into Brevo and customer websites. Brevo said a compromised Cloudflare API key was used to deploy a Worker that affected its own pages, embedded JavaScript files, and more than 100,000 customer sites for about five and a half hours. The article also covers browser-focused credential and cryptocurrency theft, including the KREMLIN banking-malware operation and a campaign using Google Visualization API to deliver browser-based skimming code.

Other developments included the reappearance of the Shai-Hulud worm in npm packages, a new Panzer ransomware operation, and a review questioning the remediation and severity results in Anthropic’s Project Glasswing ledger. The article lists a large number of recently disclosed CVEs affecting products from vendors including Cisco, Google, Microsoft, Okta, Palo Alto Networks, Dell, Apple, and others. Its central message is that familiar applications, packages, browser prompts, login flows, and cloud configurations can become attack paths, requiring organizations to patch important issues and closely review what they trust.

  • ClickFix lures are extending beyond fake operating-system fixes: the article describes campaigns that use a Google Docs sidebar, browser address-bar JavaScript, and Tampermonkey installation prompts to deliver malicious code or maintain persistence.
  • Brevo’s supply-chain incident affected both site administrators and ordinary visitors differently: administrators could receive a malicious WordPress plugin, while visitors could see a fake Cloudflare prompt directing them to run a command.
  • Plugin SHA pinning alone did not protect the four affected AI coding agents because the demonstrated flaw allowed a repository owner to make a supposedly pinned checkout resolve to malicious code without verifying the resulting commit.
  • The Shai-Hulud payload was republished in four npm packages after remaining undetected for more than three months despite the registry’s stated package-scanning practices.
  • Anthropic’s Project Glasswing ledger review found that only 202 of 26,153 claimed findings had been addressed after nearly five months, and maintainers classified substantially fewer findings as critical or high severity than Claude did.

This weekly roundup describes security problems affecting widely used systems, including Cisco’s login-management software, web browsers, AI coding tools, and customer websites. Criminals and researchers found ways to bypass logins, secretly replace trusted add-ons, steal banking and cryptocurrency information, and trick people into copying harmful commands into their computers. Businesses and individuals can be affected when familiar tools, websites, or update prompts are compromised, sometimes exposing accounts or installing unwanted software. The larger concern is that these paths are becoming faster and harder to notice, so keeping software updated and treating unexpected instructions with caution matters.
