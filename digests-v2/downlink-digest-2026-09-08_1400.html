Censys reports that approximately 70% of the roughly 134,000 Internet-exposed industrial control system hosts it observes globally are on consumer and mobile networks. The article says this creates an exposure notification gap because registration data for mobile-connected equipment often points to the telecommunications provider rather than the organization operating the device. As a result, researchers may be able to see an exposed device but may not have a reliable way to identify and notify the owner responsible for securing it.

The article says this gap matters because industrial control systems are used in environments such as substations, pump houses, remote well sites, and unstaffed automation sites where cellular connectivity may be the best or only option. It cites a July 30, 2026 CISA alert warning that threat actors were targeting Internet-exposed PLCs in water and wastewater systems. According to CISA, attackers accessed exposed controllers, changed passwords to lock operators out, and altered device IP addresses to disconnect them, leading to boil-water notices and extended periods of manual operation.

Censys observes an average of 134,000 distinct hosts running ICS services and tooling exposed on the Internet in early 2026, up roughly 4% from about 129,000 in 2024. North America accounts for more than 38% of global ICS exposure, while the United States alone accounts for 32% with over 40,000 exposed hosts. Europe’s share fell from 36.5% in 2024 to 32% in early 2026, with France, Italy, and Belgium together accounting for about 85% of that decline.

  • The main risk highlighted is not just that industrial devices are online, but that researchers may be unable to identify the actual organization responsible for fixing them when the connection traces back only to a mobile carrier.
  • CISA warned that cellular modems installed by operators, vendors, or system integrators may be undocumented or missing from routine attack surface scans, meaning some exposed systems may be unknown even to the organizations using them.
  • The United States has roughly the same share of global ICS exposure as all of Europe combined, showing that one country represents a disproportionately large portion of the observed exposure.
  • Europe’s reduction in global ICS exposure is concentrated mainly in France, Italy, and Belgium, and the article explicitly notes that the data does not prove what caused the decline.

Censys says many Internet-connected industrial machines, such as equipment used in substations, pump houses, remote well sites, and water systems, are connected through consumer or mobile networks. That can make it hard for researchers to find the actual utility, manufacturer, or operator responsible for fixing a risky device, because the address may only point to a phone company. This matters because attackers have targeted exposed controllers in water and wastewater systems, locking operators out or disconnecting equipment, which led to boil-water notices and manual operation.
