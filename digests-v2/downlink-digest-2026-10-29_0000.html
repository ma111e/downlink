Analysis of configuration data from more than 1.6 million endpoints finds that organizations commonly use multiple centrally managed protection modules and retain strong default security settings, but plan structures vary by machine role, industry, region and MSP practices. The main opportunity is to simplify incrementally accumulated plans, explicitly set backup retention and align standardized templates with customer risk profiles.

Acronis analyzed live configuration records from more than 1.6 million active endpoints to examine how protection plans are used in practice. The report finds that 65% of endpoints run two or more protection modules, while Windows workstations average 5.6 modules managed from one console. Anti-malware and anti-ransomware protections are widely retained: the behavioral engine is enabled in 84.7% of anti-malware plans, and a ransomware response is configured in 87.9% of active-protection plans. The findings support the report’s view that secure default settings are generally being kept in place.

Protection configurations differ by machine type, region, industry, and managed service provider approach. Workstations are more security-focused, while servers are more backup-focused, with backup enabled on 83.2% of Windows servers and 97.8% of Linux servers. Among machines with multiple separate plans, 82% split plans by purpose rather than schedule, and most plans were added over time instead of created together. The report recommends standardizing plans by machine role, reviewing accumulated plans for unnecessary complexity, explicitly setting backup retention, recording exceptions to strong defaults, and matching provider templates to customer industry and risk needs.

  • Multiple protection plans are usually created to separate backup from security rather than to use different schedules: 82% of multi-plan machines split plans by purpose, while 77% retain the same schedule across them.
  • Only 21.4% of multi-plan machines had all plans created on the same day; 78.6% added plans later, indicating that plan sets commonly accumulate as requirements or available modules change.
  • Workstations are more security-focused while servers are more backup-focused: 48% of Windows workstations use EDR, whereas 83% of Windows servers and 97.8% of Linux servers have backup enabled.
  • MSP standardization is more strongly associated with provider location than provider size, with 65% of U.S. and 64% of Canadian providers applying one plan pattern to most customers, compared with 24% in Colombia and 25% in Mexico.
  • Health care and education customers are much more likely to receive a combined protection plan, at 59% and 50% respectively, while traditional industries such as construction, manufacturing and agriculture are primarily served by simpler backup-focused plans.

This report examined how more than 1.6 million computers are set up for backup and security by organizations and the outside companies that manage them. It found that many keep important protections turned on and combine several kinds of protection, but the setups vary widely by country, industry, and the type of computer. This matters because buying security software alone is not enough: the way it is configured determines whether important data can be recovered and whether systems are better protected from threats such as ransomware.
