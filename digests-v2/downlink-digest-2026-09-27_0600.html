Warning: Two Unpatched Citrix NetScaler RCE Zero-Days Under Active Exploitation

Security firm watchTowr reports that two unpatched Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway remote-code-execution zero-days are being actively exploited, while Citrix has not yet confirmed the flaws or released fixes. Organizations using exposed NetScaler appliances should consider isolation and incident-response measures because patching, once available, may not remove pre-existing attacker access.

  • 02Citrix had not confirmed the reported flaws or released fixes as of Sunday morning, and watchTowr said Citrix communications and patches were expected early in the week of September 28.

  • 05Citrix’s guidance for suspected NetScaler compromise recommends preserving evidence, isolating the appliance, changing stored credentials and affected user passwords, revoking certificates and private keys, and keeping the management interface off the public internet.

Security firm watchTowr said on September 26 that two unpatched remote-code-execution vulnerabilities in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway appliances are being actively exploited. Citrix had not confirmed the flaws or issued fixes as of Sunday morning, although watchTowr said vendor communications and patches were expected early in the week of September 28. The reported issues are separate from CVE-2026-19490, an authentication-bypass flaw Citrix fixed on August 19.

NetScaler appliances sit at enterprise network edges and handle remote access, traffic distribution, and user authentication, so organizations operating them must decide whether to keep them online, isolate them, or power them off while no vendor workaround or compromise indicators are available. Because exploitation reportedly began before a fix existed, applying a future patch would not establish whether attackers had already accessed an appliance. Citrix’s existing suspected-compromise guidance recommends preserving evidence before isolation, resetting stored and affected user credentials, revoking certificates and private keys, and ensuring the management interface is not exposed to the public internet.

  • Organizations cannot rely on a future patch alone to establish whether they were affected, because watchTowr said the reported exploitation began before any fix existed.
  • No vendor workaround or indicators of compromise had been published, leaving operators to make containment decisions without confirmed technical guidance for the newly reported flaws.
  • NetScaler 13.1 reached End of Maintenance on September 15, and Citrix had not said whether that version would receive a fix for the reported issues.
  • The Netherlands National Cyber Security Center's 2025 NetScaler checking scripts may help investigate systems, but their documentation says they only look for possible signs of compromise and do not guarantee detection.
  • Citrix recommends preserving snapshots, logs, support bundles, and packet-engine core dumps before isolating an appliance suspected of compromise.

Security firm watchTowr says attackers are actively using two newly discovered weaknesses in Citrix NetScaler systems, which many organizations use to manage employee remote access, website traffic, and sign-ins. Citrix has not yet confirmed the reports or issued a repair, leaving affected organizations to decide whether to keep these systems online, isolate them, or shut them down. This matters because a break-in through one of these internet-facing systems could let an attacker remain inside an organization even after a future update is installed.

Frequently asked questions about reported Citrix NetScaler zero-day vulnerabilities

Reports indicate two unpatched Citrix NetScaler zero-days enabling remote code execution are being actively exploited, but Citrix had not yet issued CVEs, an advisory, patches, or public indicators of compromise as of September 27, 2026.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Public reports indicated that two unpatched Citrix NetScaler zero-day flaws capable of remote code execution were being actively exploited in the wild.
  • 02As of September 27, 2026, Citrix had not published a formal security advisory, assigned CVE identifiers, or released patches for the reported flaws.
  • 03The reported zero-days were not believed to be related to CVE-2026-19490 or CVE-2026-19489, which were previously disclosed Citrix NetScaler vulnerabilities with patches available.
  • 04No public proof-of-concept code, indicators of compromise, or details identifying the responsible threat actors were available as of September 27, 2026.
  • 05Citrix NetScaler devices have been frequent targets historically, with 13 NetScaler-related entries in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as of September 27, 2026.

Reports surfaced on September 25–26, 2026, that two unpatched Citrix NetScaler zero-day vulnerabilities capable of remote code execution were being actively exploited. The reports were tied to an alleged NCSC-NL pre-notification and subsequent confirmations from watchTowr and Kevin Beaumont, although Tenable had not independently reviewed the pre-notification. As of September 27, Citrix had not published a formal security advisory, assigned CVE identifiers, or provided patches.

The reported issues were not believed to be related to CVE-2026-19490 or CVE-2026-19489, previously disclosed NetScaler vulnerabilities for which patches were available. No public proof-of-concept, indicators of compromise, or threat-actor details were available, and reporting had not determined whether the attacks were widespread. Citrix NetScaler has been a frequent target historically: CISA’s KEV catalog contained 13 NetScaler-related entries as of September 27, 2026, and Tenable stated that patches were expected early in the week of September 28.

  • The initial reporting stemmed from an alleged NCSC-NL pre-notification distributed under TLP:AMBER+STRICT restrictions, and Tenable stated that it had not independently reviewed that notification.
  • Public reporting had not established whether the active exploitation was widespread, despite confirmation that attacks were occurring.
  • Tenable's historical research found that roughly two-thirds of threat activity targeting Citrix NetScaler over the prior seven years involved advanced persistent threat groups, while about one-third involved ransomware groups and affiliates.
  • Tenable expected to classify the issues under its Vulnerability Watch program and release product detection coverage only after CVE identifiers were assigned and patches became available.

Reports say that two previously unknown security flaws in Citrix NetScaler devices are being used in real-world attacks, but the maker had not yet released fixes or full details. Organizations that use NetScaler ADC or Gateway devices may be affected, because the flaws could let an outsider take control of a device over the internet. These devices have been frequent targets in the past, and attackers have included both state-linked groups and criminal extortion groups, making the lack of available fixes especially concerning.
