SYS010 malware abuses browser management to spy on Gmail, Outlook

Bitdefender reports that the SYS010 malware campaign uses malicious Meta ads for animated wallpapers to enroll browsers in attacker-controlled management and force-install extensions that steal session cookies, Meta business data, and Gmail/Outlook emails. The extensions can also delete emails, potentially hiding security alerts and intercepting account recovery codes.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Bitdefender tracks a SYS010 campaign that uses Meta ads for free animated wallpapers to deliver malware to Windows users.
  • 02The malicious apps provide the advertised animated wallpaper function while modifying Chrome, Edge, and Firefox management settings to force-install browser extensions.
  • 03The extensions can steal session cookies and collect Facebook Page, advertising-account, and business-account information, including spending and payment details.
  • 04Bitdefender found that the Chrome extension can read recent Gmail and Outlook messages, send their contents to attackers, and permanently delete selected emails.
  • 05Bitdefender recommends avoiding software downloads from social-media ads and refusing administrator prompts whose purpose is unclear.

Bitdefender Labs reports that SYS010, likely an updated version of the SYS01 infostealer, is being distributed through Meta ads that promote free animated wallpaper applications for Windows. The applications provide the advertised wallpaper functionality while also deploying malware in the background. Researchers identified a malicious SYS010 ad on October 16 and found hundreds of ads in the ongoing campaign, with one ad reaching 6,275 users in the European Union.

The malware alters Chrome, Edge, and Firefox management settings to force-install malicious browser extensions, using repeated administrator prompts to obtain the necessary permissions. The extensions can steal session cookies, collect Facebook Page and advertising-account information, and access recent Gmail and Outlook messages; the Chrome extension can also send message contents to attackers and permanently delete selected emails. Bitdefender warns that this could allow attackers to intercept login codes, hide security alerts, cause unauthorized advertising charges, expose customer conversations, and make compromised accounts harder to recover.

  • The malicious wallpaper applications actually deliver the promised animated wallpapers, helping conceal the malware running in the background.
  • The attackers repeatedly display an administrator-approval request every three seconds, up to 99 times, to pressure users into granting the permissions needed to change browser controls.
  • An unexpected “managed by your organization” notice in a personal browser can indicate that attackers have taken over browser management settings.
  • By reading and deleting selected Gmail or Outlook messages, the extensions could intercept login codes and remove security alerts before the account owner sees them.

Criminals are using Facebook and Instagram ads for free animated wallpapers to trick Windows users into installing software that appears to work but secretly adds tools to their web browsers. These tools can read recent Gmail and Outlook messages, steal access to signed-in accounts, delete selected emails, and collect details from Facebook business and advertising accounts. This can lead to unauthorized ad charges, stolen social-media accounts, exposed customer conversations, and hidden security warnings that make it harder for victims to regain control.

OPEN SOURCE ↗

This is an active, ongoing malvertising campaign that uses browser-management controls to force-install extensions capable of stealing session access, Meta business data, and Gmail or Outlook emails. It is broadly relevant to Windows users and organizations with Meta advertising accounts, especially because the malware can hide security alerts by deleting emails.
