Skullcandy Dime 3 earbuds expose users to Bluetooth hijacking

Although Skullcandy says firmware 1.0.0.30 fixes the issue, existing users currently have no consumer-accessible way to update vulnerable units.
KEY POINTS
  • 01CERT/CC warned that Skullcandy Dime 3 wireless earbuds accept Bluetooth pairing requests from nearby unpaired devices without requiring user interaction.
  • 02Skullcandy Dime 3 devices running firmware version 1.0.0.28 are affected by CVE-2025-20701, a high-severity missing-authentication vulnerability in the Airoha Bluetooth Audio SDK.
  • 03An attacker in close range can connect without a pairing PIN, physical access to the earbuds case, or an approving pairing request, and can later interrupt the owner’s connection, hijack audio playback, access the headset profile, and capture live microphone audio.
  • 04Skullcandy says the issue was fixed in firmware version 1.0.0.30, but CERT/CC says users with existing vulnerable units currently have no consumer-accessible method to update them.
  • 05CVE-2025-20701 was discovered by ERNW researchers, presented at the TROOPER cybersecurity conference, and affects a broad range of earbud and headphone products from multiple vendors.

CERT/CC is warning that Skullcandy Dime 3 wireless earbuds accept Bluetooth pairing requests from nearby unpaired devices without requiring user interaction. Devices running firmware version 1.0.0.28 are affected by CVE-2025-20701, a high-severity missing-authentication vulnerability in the Airoha Bluetooth Audio SDK. The Skullcandy Dime 3 model S2DCW uses this SDK to handle wireless connectivity and communication between the earbuds and connected devices.

An attacker in close range can connect to a vulnerable device over Bluetooth without a pairing PIN, physical access to the earbuds case, or an approving pairing request. After pairing, the attacker’s device becomes trusted and can automatically reconnect when nearby. CERT/CC says this can let the attacker interrupt the owner’s connection, hijack audio playback, access the headset profile, and capture live microphone audio.

Skullcandy says the issue was fixed in firmware version 1.0.0.30, but CERT/CC notes that customers with existing earbuds running firmware version 1.0.0.28 currently have no known consumer-accessible way to update them. The article says the flaw was discovered by ERNW researchers and presented at the TROOPER cybersecurity conference last year. Airoha published SDK updates for the issue on August 4, 2025, and Apple addressed the same flaw for Beats Studio Buds through a firmware update released in June.

  • The practical risk persists even though Skullcandy has a fixed firmware version, because CERT/CC says customers with vulnerable existing units cannot update them through the app or any known consumer-accessible method.
  • A successful rogue pairing may not be obvious to the owner, because the only sign may be a “new device paired” notification that can be mistaken for a brief connection loss and reconnection.
  • The attacker’s device becomes trusted after pairing, so the issue is not limited to a one-time nearby connection and can allow automatic reconnection when the attacker is nearby again.
  • The vulnerability is not unique to Skullcandy, as the same Airoha Bluetooth Audio SDK flaw affects a broader range of earbud and headphone products from multiple vendors.

A warning says Skullcandy Dime 3 wireless earbuds with older firmware can accept connections from nearby strangers without the owner approving it. People who bought affected earbuds running version 1.0.0.28 could have their audio interrupted and, after the stranger’s device is accepted, their live microphone sound captured when the attacker is close by. Skullcandy says a newer version fixes the problem, but the article says regular customers currently have no way to update older units themselves. This matters because a popular, affordable earbud product could expose private conversations even when the owner did not knowingly connect to anyone else.

Silent Push announced version 6.1 of its platform, with a focus on protecting organizations earlier and accelerating analyst workflows. The release is led by a next-generation Brand and Infrastructure Impersonation engine designed to begin protection during the preparation phase, before attacks can be weaponized. Silent Push says the engine helps customers monitor their infrastructure and associated threat actors in time to act.

The new impersonation detection engine can be pointed at an organization’s owned infrastructure to find look-alike domains impersonating it. Identified domains are scored by risk, enriched with registrar, hosting, and page-title context, and clustered by the threat actor behind them. The engine runs on a schedule, notifies teams as new candidates appear, and supports exporting relevant domains as evidence for internal use or takedown partners.

Platform 6.1 also adds workflow and integration updates, including My Assets, Advanced Pivot Control, expanded Insight Search, and Total View enhancements such as a PADNS timeline, IP certificates, PDF export, and screenshot date filtering. Silent Push added integrations for Palo Alto Cortex XSOAR, a Chrome Extension, AI assistants and agentic workflows through an MCP server, and bidirectional STIX/TAXII exchange through an updated TAXII Server. Additional updates include support for TLP: AMBER+STRICT in custom analyst reports and localized interfaces in Korean and Spanish alongside English, with Japanese coming soon.

  • Silent Push is positioning early detection of look-alike infrastructure as a takedown workflow, since the new engine exports relevant domains as evidence for internal teams or takedown partners.
  • The release emphasizes reducing repeated analyst effort by letting teams define their owned domains, IP ranges, ASNs, and TLDs once in My Assets and reuse them as one-click inputs across the platform.
  • Silent Push is extending its intelligence beyond its own interface into existing analyst workflows, including SOAR playbooks, browser-based investigations, AI assistants through MCP, and bidirectional STIX/TAXII sharing.
  • The updated TAXII Server changes Silent Push from a one-way intelligence source into a two-way exchange point by allowing customers to push their own STIX/TAXII intelligence into the platform.
