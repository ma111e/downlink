  • 03The XCPE backdoor could install apps silently, disable Google Play Protect, run system-level commands, capture screenshots, collect device and account information, and create remote access to devices.

Google and industry partners disrupted the XCPE botnet, which Google says involved more than 1.3 million Android TV streaming boxes manufactured with a persistent backdoor already embedded in their firmware. Google attributed the operation with high confidence to Chinese manufacturer Videostrong Technology Co., alleging it deliberately installed the backdoor and sold access to criminal affiliates. The company revoked Videostrong’s Android TV license and banned it and future affiliates from making certified Android devices or supplying software to certified vendors.

The XCPE backdoor, tracked as x.cpe and com.oemservice, operated with Android system-level privileges and received commands through command-and-control infrastructure and an MQTT message broker. Operators could silently install apps, disable Google Play Protect, execute commands, take screenshots, collect device and account data, and establish remote access. The infected devices were used for ad fraud, residential proxy services, and delivery of additional malware, while the takedown also disrupted infrastructure linked to at least 10 million other compromised devices. Google said the malware remains on affected devices and can reinstall itself from the /oem/ partition, so users need clean firmware or must disconnect the devices from the internet.

  • The XCPE infection was embedded in device firmware during manufacturing, meaning affected owners could receive a compromised device without installing anything or falling for a scam.
  • Removing the malware from an affected device is not sufficient because it can automatically reinstall from the /oem/ partition; installing clean firmware or disconnecting the device from the internet is required to stop its activity.
  • Google said the disruption of XCPE command-and-control servers also cut off more than 10 million additional compromised devices, including set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware that were infected through other distribution methods.
  • The botnet functioned as a platform for multiple criminal affiliates, combining fake advertising activity, residential internet proxy services, and deployment of additional backdoors on the same devices.
  • Google stated that Videostrong used obfuscation to evade certification testing and loaded malicious code after devices first booted, prompting Google to change its certification review process.

Google says Chinese TV-box maker Videostrong secretly built harmful software into more than 1.3 million Android TV streaming boxes before they were sold. Criminal partners could use those boxes without owners’ knowledge to generate fake advertising activity, route other people’s internet traffic, and add more harmful software. Google shut down much of the network used to control the devices and banned Videostrong from making certified Android devices, but owners may need clean replacement software or to take affected boxes offline because the unwanted software can return after removal. The case shows that a device can be unsafe from the moment it is purchased, rather than becoming infected only after its owner makes a mistake.
