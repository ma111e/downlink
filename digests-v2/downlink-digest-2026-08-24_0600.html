Netskope Adds PQC Key Exchange to Next Gen SWG

Netskope added hybrid post-quantum key exchange support to Netskope One Next Gen SWG, using X25519MLKEM768 to protect traffic against future quantum decryption risks. The feature is available now in commercial environments as an optional tenant-level flag, with broader environment support planned.
KEY POINTS
  • 01Netskope added support for X25519MLKEM768, a hybrid key-exchange algorithm, to Netskope One Next Gen SWG.
  • 02The new support covers both client-to-Netskope traffic and Netskope-to-destination-server traffic.
  • 03The feature is a tenant-level flag designed for controlled testing rather than a forced rollout.
  • 04Transaction events show post-quantum cryptography use so customers can verify when it is working.
  • 05The feature is available in commercial environments today, with support for FedRAMP (US), PBMM (Canada), Data Plane on-Premises, and Virtual Private Edge planned for future releases.

Netskope has added support for X25519MLKEM768 to Netskope One Next Gen SWG. The article says this is a hybrid key-exchange algorithm that pairs elliptic-curve cryptography with a post-quantum method. Netskope says this helps keep a connection protected even if one of the two approaches is broken in the future.

The change is presented in the context of concerns that encrypted data captured today could be stored and decrypted later once capable quantum computers exist. The article highlights data with a long shelf life, such as health records, financial history, government communications, and trade secrets. It also notes that NIST finalized its first set of post-quantum cryptography standards in 2024, including ML-KEM.

The new support covers both client-to-Netskope traffic and Netskope-to-destination-server traffic. Netskope says the feature is available in commercial environments today as a tenant-level feature flag for controlled testing, with transaction events showing post-quantum cryptography use. Support for FedRAMP (US), PBMM (Canada), Data Plane on-Premises, and Virtual Private Edge is planned for future releases.

  • Netskope is using a hybrid approach that keeps today’s elliptic-curve cryptography alongside a post-quantum method, so protection does not depend entirely on either method remaining unbroken.
  • The feature is positioned for gradual adoption because customers can enable it at the tenant level for controlled testing rather than being forced into an immediate migration.
  • The article treats post-quantum migration as especially urgent for organizations with long-lived sensitive data, because copied encrypted data could become readable later if quantum computers advance.
  • Netskope’s implementation is tied to its OpenSSL 3.5 upgrade, indicating the company is relying on native cryptographic library support rather than a separate add-on.

Netskope added a new option to its Netskope One Next Gen SWG service that helps protect web traffic from a future where powerful quantum computers could break today’s encryption. This matters because information stolen or copied today, such as health records, financial history, government messages, or trade secrets, could be saved and decrypted years later. Organizations using Netskope in commercial environments can turn this on when they are ready, while support for other environments is planned for later.
