OpenAI teases Astra, its next major AI model, after it solves 10 long-standing math problems

OpenAI teased Astra, its next major AI model built for long-running complex tasks, after an internal version helped produce and formally verify advances on ten long-standing math and theoretical computer science problems. The model may eventually launch as GPT-5.7, GPT-6, or under another name.

OpenAI has revealed Astra, an unreleased model designed to handle complex, long-running tasks. The company described Astra as “our next major model” after an internal version produced ten significant advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science. OpenAI said the problems had seen no progress on their central results for at least a decade, and in most cases much longer.

The work covered areas including high-dimensional geometry, coding theory, arithmetic circuit complexity, group theory, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography, and extremal combinatorics. Examples cited in the article include the existence of non-sofic groups, a disproof of Connes’s rigidity conjecture, new bounds for high-dimensional sphere packing, and results resolving several problems posed by Paul Erdős. OpenAI said the total number of tokens needed to find the solutions would cost roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates.

Human researchers used the same model to prepare the arguments as manuscripts, and Astra formalized every argument as a Lean certificate so the proofs could be checked using the mathematical verification system. The Information also independently confirmed that OpenAI is working on Astra, a new model family built for long-running workloads. OpenAI has reportedly not decided whether the model will be released as GPT-5.7, GPT-6, or under another name, and the article says it could have a consumer version while a more powerful variant requires special approval.

  • OpenAI highlighted that the total token cost to find the ten solutions would be roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates, suggesting the reported mathematical advances were not presented as requiring extraordinary API spending.
  • Astra’s workflow included formalizing every argument as a Lean certificate, which means OpenAI emphasized machine-checkable verification rather than relying only on human-written manuscripts.
  • The article says Astra may be released under a consumer-accessible model name or handled with restricted access for a stronger variant, indicating OpenAI has not yet settled how broadly the most capable version will be available.
  • The reported results span multiple specialized fields, including lattice cryptography and quantum complexity, showing that OpenAI is positioning Astra as a general long-running research model rather than a tool for one narrow area.

OpenAI revealed Astra, an unreleased AI model meant to work on very difficult, long-running problems, after an internal version helped make progress on ten major math and theoretical computer science problems that had been stuck for at least a decade. Human researchers used the model to prepare the arguments, and Astra turned them into checkable proof certificates using a system called Lean. This matters because OpenAI says the model is a major breakthrough that could help AI agents work together on large problems, though the company has not decided whether it will be released as GPT-5.7, GPT-6, or another name.
