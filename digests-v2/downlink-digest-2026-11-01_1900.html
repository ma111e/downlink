FBI links $6.3 billion Binance crypto heist to North Korean hackers

The FBI attributed the record $6.3 billion Binance cryptocurrency theft to North Korean TraderTraitor/Lazarus hackers, who allegedly gained access through a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop. Binance says it has covered about 95% of affected assets and plans to resume withdrawals while authorities seek to block laundering addresses.

The FBI has attributed the theft of more than \(6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on October 29, 2026, to North Korean state-sponsored hackers known as TraderTraitor. The group is also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38. The theft, estimated at approximately \)6.36 billion across ten blockchains, is described as the largest cryptocurrency heist on record.

Binance said the attackers compromised a DevOps engineer’s laptop, accessed a service container, and submitted unauthorized withdrawal transactions, with most funds transferred within 20 minutes. The FBI has observed the group converting stolen assets primarily into Ethereum and warned that the funds may be laundered through decentralized finance platforms and instant swap services; it published 628 addresses associated with the theft and asked relevant providers to block linked transactions. Binance said it has covered about 95% of the affected amount, will bear the losses rather than pass them to users, and intends to resume an initial phase of withdrawals on November 1.

  • The theft illustrates how compromising one employee device with access to operational systems can enable unauthorized withdrawals at exchange-wide scale.
  • Most of the approximately $6.36 billion was moved across ten blockchains within 20 minutes, highlighting the limited time available for exchanges and blockchain-service providers to respond after suspicious withdrawals begin.
  • The FBI’s publication of 628 laundering addresses gives cryptocurrency exchanges and related providers concrete indicators they can use to block transactions tied to the theft.
  • The group’s documented use of fake recruiting offers, malicious coding tests, and in-person relationship building shows that its operations rely heavily on deceiving employees rather than only targeting technical systems.
  • Binance said the final 5% of the affected amount is harder to replace because it consists of less-liquid tokens, indicating that recovery can depend on whether stolen assets can be acquired without readily available markets.

The FBI says North Korean hackers known as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in digital currency from Binance after gaining access through a Binance DevOps engineer’s compromised laptop. Binance customers were affected because withdrawals were disrupted, though the company says it will absorb the losses and has covered about 95% of the stolen amount. The case shows how criminals can use fake job offers and other personal deception to get into companies handling large amounts of money, while moving stolen funds through services designed to swap digital currencies.
