In plain words

Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, a criminal service that used an AI-style chatbot to help people break into email accounts and plan scams. The chatbot could read a victim’s inbox, identify trusted contacts and payment approvals, and draft messages that pretended to come from those contacts. Healthcare organizations were among the targets, so Health-ISAC joined Microsoft’s legal action; the case matters because tools like this can make financial fraud more targeted and convincing.

Summary
Microsoft, with Health-ISAC as a co-plaintiff, disrupted EvilTokens, an AI-enabled cybercrime platform that helped criminals compromise email accounts, analyze inboxes, and craft targeted financial-fraud impersonation messages.

Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, a cybercrime platform that used AI at each stage of its operations. The service helped criminals compromise email accounts and create detailed plans for financial fraud and scams. Health-ISAC joined Microsoft’s legal action as a co-plaintiff because healthcare organizations were among those targeted.

At the center of EvilTokens was an AI-style chatbot that could analyze a victim’s inbox. It could identify trusted relationships, payment authorizations, sensitive responsibilities, and other situations in which fraud was more likely to work. The platform could also recommend fraud strategies and draft messages impersonating trusted contacts to persuade victims to take action.

  • EvilTokens was designed not only to access email accounts but also to turn the contents of a victim’s inbox into tailored fraud opportunities.
  • By identifying trusted relationships and payment authorizations, the chatbot could help criminals focus impersonation attempts on situations where fraud was most likely to succeed.
  • The involvement of Health-ISAC as a co-plaintiff shows that healthcare organizations were directly among the platform’s targets.
  • The platform’s ability to draft messages posing as trusted contacts could make scam communications appear more credible to recipients.

Introducing Ghostwriter Skills
specterops.io · 22 Sep 2026 16:08 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGSghostworksghostwriterghostwriter-skillsjinja2specterops
52
In plain words

SpecterOps has released Ghostwriter Skills, a set of reusable AI-assisted workflows for people who use its Ghostwriter reporting tool during security assessments. The tools can help turn old reports into reusable formats, check reports for missing or inconsistent information, and create a first draft of a management-friendly summary. This matters because it may reduce repetitive report work and catch mistakes before a report is delivered, while keeping final decisions and writing responsibility with the human author.

Summary
SpecterOps has launched Ghostwriter Skills, a portable collection of AI-agent workflows for creating and reviewing Ghostwriter report templates, checking report readiness, and drafting executive summaries. The skills can work offline or connect read-only to Ghostwriter through its GraphQL API.

SpecterOps has introduced Ghostwriter Skills, a collection of portable Agent Skills for recurring Ghostwriter workflows. The initial release covers converting sample reports into templates, reviewing DOCX and PPTX templates, checking whether projects are ready for reporting, and drafting executive summaries from report data. The project is maintained separately from Ghostwriter and versioned independently. It is intended to work with Agent Skills-compatible tools including Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot.

The template-creation skill analyzes supplied documents and identifies material that can be replaced with Ghostwriter variables, loops, or fields while preserving document structure where possible. The review skill checks for Ghostwriter compatibility issues and broader document problems, including problematic Jinja placement, broken references, missing styles, and PowerPoint layout or placeholder issues. The report-readiness skill sorts results into blockers, warnings, passed checks, and items it could not assess, while the executive-summary skill creates a draft grounded in existing report content. Most skills can operate offline, and connected workflows use Ghostwriter’s GraphQL API with project-read service tokens that cannot alter Ghostwriter data.

  • A template can appear correct in Word or PowerPoint yet still fail during Ghostwriter rendering or final PDF preparation because of hidden issues such as bookmarks, document relationships, page breaks, fields, or Jinja syntax placement.
  • The template-creation workflow can reuse a live Ghostwriter instance's existing custom-field specification to recommend fields already used by an organization, rather than only proposing new fields.
  • Report-readiness results are organized into blockers, warnings, passed checks, and checks that could not be assessed, giving reviewers a prioritized list instead of a simple pass-or-fail answer.
  • Connected use relies on project-read service tokens, so the skills can retrieve relevant Ghostwriter information without allowing the language model to modify data in Ghostwriter.
  • The executive-summary skill is explicitly positioned as a draft based on existing report material, with the report author retaining responsibility for risk judgment, client context, and final communication.
Key Points
  • 01SpecterOps introduced Ghostwriter Skills, a separately maintained and independently versioned collection of portable Agent Skills for common Ghostwriter workflows.
  • 02The initial release includes skills to convert sample reports into Ghostwriter templates, review DOCX and PPTX templates, assess a project's report readiness, and draft an executive summary from existing report content.
  • 03The create-template skill analyzes supplied documents, preserves structure where possible, and produces a converted template with a report describing mappings, suggested custom fields, and items needing review.
  • 04The report-readiness skill evaluates a Ghostwriter project for issues such as incomplete findings, unresolved objectives, placeholders, CVSS inconsistencies, and missing expected BloodHound data.
  • 05Most skills support offline use, while connected workflows can use Ghostwriter's GraphQL API with a project-read service token that does not allow the LLM to alter Ghostwriter data.
