pCloud Password Manager Hit by Cyber Attack Involving Email Address Exposure and Login Attempts
thehackernews.com · 28 Nov 2026 08:08 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGS1passwordgithubluxembourgmsecuremseven-softwarepcloudpcloud-passphishingswitzerland
78
In plain words

pCloud Pass, a service that stores passwords, says it stopped an attack in which a small number of users’ email addresses were exposed and someone tried to sign in to their accounts. Some users received alerts about an unfamiliar device connecting to their account, even when they had an extra sign-in verification step enabled. pCloud says stored passwords and notes remain protected because they can only be opened with the user’s master password, but affected users should be alert for fake messages trying to steal that password or recovery words.

Summary
pCloud Pass contained a cyberattack that exposed a small number of user email addresses and triggered login attempts, but says encrypted vault data, passwords, and notes were not compromised. Users should watch for phishing attempts seeking their master password or recovery words.

pCloud Pass said it promptly contained a cyberattack that exposed a small number of user email addresses and involved login attempts. Users had reported alerts showing an unknown device connected to their accounts, including accounts with two-factor authentication enabled. pCloud said the incident was limited to its password manager and did not affect pCloud Cloud Storage.

According to pCloud, passwords, notes, and other stored information were not compromised because they are encrypted and require a Master Password to access. Reported alerts identified a device named “pd” connecting from two IP addresses associated with pCloud infrastructure in Luxembourg, but it remains unclear whether the login attempts succeeded or whether encrypted vaults were accessed or downloaded. pCloud has not disclosed how the attackers entered, when the intrusion took place, or how long access lasted, and it urged users to be alert for phishing attempts seeking Master Passwords or recovery words.

  • New-device alerts were reported even by users with two-factor authentication enabled, indicating that the observed activity was not necessarily blocked before account-associated device activity occurred.
  • The unfamiliar device named "pd" was observed connecting from two IP addresses assigned to pCloud infrastructure in Luxembourg, rather than from an obviously external network.
  • The “Open Settings” link in at least one alert reportedly redirected through pCloud tracking to a passqa1.pcloud.com address that appeared to be an internal quality-assurance environment and did not load.
  • pCloud did not disclose how attackers gained access, when the intrusion occurred, how long access lasted, or whether login attempts succeeded and encrypted vaults were accessed or downloaded.
  • The incident follows other reported password-manager security incidents involving mSecure and 1Password during the same month, underscoring the trust and transparency concerns raised when password-management providers experience intrusions.
Key Points
  • 01pCloud Pass said it promptly contained a cyberattack that exposed a small number of user email addresses and involved login attempts.
  • 02pCloud said passwords, notes, and other information stored in pCloud Pass were not compromised because they are encrypted and require a master password to access.
  • 03The incident was limited to pCloud Pass and did not affect pCloud Cloud Storage services, according to the company.
  • 04Users reported receiving new-device sign-in alerts for a device called "pd," including users who had two-factor authentication enabled.
  • 05pCloud urged users to watch for phishing messages that request their master password or recovery words through email, phone, or chat.

JUSTIFICATION

This is a specific security incident affecting a password manager, with exposed user email addresses, reported unfamiliar-device alerts, and unresolved questions about whether login attempts succeeded. It is broadly relevant because password-manager users are high-value phishing targets, although pCloud says encrypted vault contents were not compromised and the affected user count was small.
