CISA Flags SonicWall SonicOS MFA Bypass Flaw Amid Reports of Possible Exploitation

In plain words

The U.S. government’s cybersecurity agency has highlighted a serious flaw in SonicWall network software after reports that it may already have been used in real attacks. The problem could let someone with certain valid login details get around an organization’s extra sign-in check, such as a code or approval prompt. Organizations that use affected SonicWall systems, especially their remote-access service, should install the available updates because an attacker could otherwise gain access more easily.

Summary
CISA added SonicWall SonicOS CVE-2026-19868 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after reports of possible real-world exploitation, as the flaw can let users with alternate valid credentials bypass SAML-enforced MFA on SSL-VPNs. Organizations should promptly update SonicOS and review exposure to the affected SSL-VPN service.

CISA added CVE-2026-19868, a high-severity SonicWall SonicOS issue, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after reports that it may have been used in real-world attacks. SonicWall said it investigated a report of potential exploitation but did not provide details about the attack or the party behind it. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies have been recommended to apply patches by November 20, 2026.

The issue affects the SonicOS SSL-VPN service and may allow a user with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method to bypass SAML authentication controls. Successful use of the flaw may also bypass multi-factor authentication enforced through SAML. SonicWall urged customers to update to the latest version and also released fixes for three other high-severity SonicOS flaws affecting the management interface and SSL-VPN service.

Separately, SonicWall recently disclosed two unpatched vulnerabilities affecting end-of-life Global Management System products. The company said it will not patch those GMS issues and advised customers to upgrade to Network Security Manager instead. The additional patched SonicOS flaws include two issues that could enable unauthorized operations under specific conditions and one that could cause an application-level denial of service.

  • SAML-based multi-factor authentication may not protect SonicOS SSL-VPN access if alternate login methods remain available to users with valid credentials.
  • CISA’s addition of the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and SonicWall’s investigation of a potential exploitation report make patching more urgent than a routine software update.
  • The same update cycle addresses both possible unauthorized management-interface actions and a remote SSL-VPN denial-of-service condition, so organizations may need to assess more than the MFA-bypass issue.
  • Organizations using end-of-life SonicWall Global Management System products face a separate concern because SonicWall said it will not patch two other disclosed flaws affecting those systems and instead recommends moving to Network Security Manager.
Key Points
  • 01CISA added CVE-2026-19868, a high-severity SonicWall SonicOS flaw, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following reports of possible real-world exploitation.
  • 02The flaw affects the SonicOS SSL-VPN service and may allow users with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method to bypass SAML controls, including SAML-enforced multi-factor authentication.
  • 03SonicWall said it investigated a report of potential exploitation and urged customers to update to the latest version as soon as possible.
  • 04SonicWall also patched three other high-severity SonicOS issues, including two management-interface flaws that could allow unauthorized operations and an SSL-VPN buffer overflow that could cause application-level denial of service.
  • 05Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies are recommended to apply the patches by November 20, 2026.

U.S. Agencies Warn Refrigeration Controller Attacks Could Cause Fires and Inventory Losses

In plain words

U.S. agencies warn that criminals are breaking into internet-connected systems that control refrigeration and temperature monitoring, including systems used by supermarkets and facilities storing medical products. Attackers can raise temperatures or force equipment into defrost mode, spoiling food and medicines, damaging equipment, and potentially creating fire risks. Recent attacks affected hundreds of stores across Europe, and a French blood-storage organization reported dangerously changed settings at three locations. The problem matters because these systems help keep everyday food, medicine, and other sensitive goods safe for people to use.

Summary
U.S. agencies warn that internet-exposed, poorly secured industrial refrigeration controllers are being actively targeted to alter temperatures or trigger defrost cycles, risking fires, spoiled food and medicine, and equipment damage. Operators should remove unnecessary internet access, isolate OT systems, patch devices, and strengthen authentication.

U.S. agencies including CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA, and DOT warned that threat actors are actively targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide. The advisory says attackers may exploit unpatched or internet-accessible controllers, authentication bypasses, arbitrary code injection, and weak configurations. They can remotely change settings or force units into continuous heating and defrost cycles.

These actions can cause inventory loss, equipment hazards, fire risks, and unsafe storage conditions for food, medicine, and other temperature-sensitive products. Recent attacks hit hundreds of supermarkets across Europe, while the French Blood Establishment reported extremely high temperature and defrost settings at three blood-plasma storage facilities after an incident involving a refrigeration-equipment provider. Operators are urged to use strong authentication, update systems, identify and isolate internet-accessible operational devices, replace default passwords and legacy devices, and remove unnecessary remote access.

  • The reported impact extends beyond spoiled retail food: altered refrigeration settings affected blood-plasma storage facilities, illustrating that a single equipment provider incident can reach medically sensitive operations.
  • Despite a major reduction in publicly exposed Danfoss StoreView instances from 3,004 to 708 between October 9 and November 5, 2026, hundreds of systems remained reachable from the internet.
  • The attacks can impair both cooling control and temperature visibility, meaning operators may lose the ability to verify current storage conditions during an incident.
  • The affected targets ranged from major supermarket chains to small grocers and butchers in at least 12 European countries, showing that organizations of varied sizes were exposed to the same controller-related risk.
Key Points
  • 01U.S. agencies warned that threat actors are actively targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide by exploiting unpatched or internet-accessible controllers.
  • 02Attackers may alter temperature settings or force continuous heating and defrost cycles, creating risks of inventory loss, equipment damage, fire hazards, and unsafe storage of food or medicine.
  • 03Recent attacks exploited internet-accessible refrigeration controllers at hundreds of supermarkets across Europe, raising temperatures or forcing defrost mode and causing food spoilage and financial losses.
  • 04The French Blood Establishment reported that a cybersecurity incident affecting a refrigeration equipment provider changed temperature and defrost settings to extremely high levels at three blood-plasma storage facilities.
  • 05CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA, and DOT advised operators to update systems, eliminate unnecessary internet exposure and remote access, isolate operational devices, and use strong authentication and non-default passwords.
