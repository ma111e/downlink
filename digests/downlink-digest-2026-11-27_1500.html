In plain words

Researchers say a suspected Vietnam-linked group called WeaveLotus broke into government, military, and legislative computer systems in Thailand and the Philippines, while also investigating possible targets in Indonesia. The group reportedly used publicly exposed software flaws and easy-to-guess or default passwords to reach internal documents, user information, planning records, and other sensitive data. The activity appears focused on intelligence about South China Sea defense and military agreements, which could affect regional security and foreign-policy decisions.

Summary
SOCRadar identified a suspected Vietnam-linked espionage group, WeaveLotus, targeting Thai and Philippine government, military, and legislative systems to collect intelligence related to South China Sea defense interests. The group exploited exposed applications and default or weak credentials, then used reverse SSH tunnels and credential attacks to access and exfiltrate sensitive records.

SOCRadar reported a suspected Vietnam-linked espionage activity cluster it calls WeaveLotus, which conducted five confirmed intrusions against military, government, and legislative organizations in Thailand and the Philippines. The activity, called RedOps, was observed from August to September 2026, while Indonesian government organizations were subject to reconnaissance that did not advance to confirmed compromise. The apparent objective was to collect internal records and documents connected to Southeast Asian governments, including material relevant to South China Sea defense and military agreements. Attribution is based on Vietnamese-language code comments, activity aligned with UTC+07:00 working hours, strategic target selection, and custom scripts, although SOCRadar said state sponsorship was not conclusively established.

The operators primarily entered targets by abusing exposed web-application flaws and default credentials in GeoServer, JasperReports, osTicket, and Laravel debug-mode configurations. They maintained access through reverse SSH tunnels and SOCKS5 proxies, then moved into connected systems to gather data. Reported activity included exploiting GeoServer and osTicket flaws, using default JasperReports administrative credentials to execute commands through Microsoft SQL Server, cracking Keycloak password hashes, and spraying passwords against a document-management system. The attackers obtained data including staff account information, user directories, audit logs, ZIP archives, and 349,782 records from a Thai downstream application containing planning and fiscal information.

The group also conducted automated web searches for news and documents concerning military agreements involving the Philippines, China, and the United States, with emphasis on the South China Sea. Against Indonesian organizations, it harvested certificate records, scanned ports, identified remote-access services, and collected email-service banners across more than 30 organizations. SOCRadar noted that the campaign targeted only government, military, and legislative institutions rather than private-sector entities. The incident illustrates how exposed applications, weak authentication controls, and persistent network connections were used for intelligence collection in Southeast Asian public-sector environments.

  • The operators did more than collect database records: they used optical character recognition to extract English and Thai text from stolen PNG images and attempted to access military surveillance video feeds.
  • In one Thai legislative intrusion, the attackers paired employee names with National ID numbers in password-spraying attempts against a third-party document management system, then obtained its configuration, user directory, audit logs, and 5,000 ZIP archives after successful authentication.
  • A Thai government system’s password hashes were cracked using wordlists based on Thai Buddhist Era years, and the resulting passwords were validated against its token-issuing endpoint before being used to access a downstream application.
  • Although the campaign reconnoitered more than 30 Indonesian organizations, including their remote-access and email services, SOCRadar reported that activity there did not progress beyond reconnaissance.
  • The confirmed targets were limited to government, military, and legislative institutions rather than private-sector entities, and the reported collection focus involved South China Sea-related defense and military agreements.
Key Points
  • 01SOCRadar identified a suspected Vietnam-linked activity cluster named WeaveLotus that targeted military, government, and legislative organizations in Thailand and the Philippines, with reconnaissance against Indonesian government organizations.
  • 02The RedOps campaign was observed from August to September 2026 and appears intended to collect internal government records and documents related to Vietnam's foreign-policy and military-intelligence priorities.
  • 03WeaveLotus gained initial access mainly through exposed web-application weaknesses and default credentials in GeoServer, JasperReports, osTicket, and Laravel debug-mode configurations.
  • 04The group used reverse SSH tunnels and SOCKS5 proxies to retain internal access and move into other networks and applications.
  • 05Confirmed intrusions included the extraction of hundreds of thousands of records from a Thai government-related downstream application and staff account data from a Philippine government organization's osTicket system.
