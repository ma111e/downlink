OpenAI warns GPT-6.2 Astra will hit usage limits 40% faster because it fixed a bug
bleepingcomputer.com · 26 Nov 2026 21:06 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGSchatgptgpt-6-1-solgpt-6-1-terragpt-6-2-astragpt-6-2-solgpt-6-astraopenai

In plain words

OpenAI corrected a mistake that had made some ChatGPT models appear to use less of a subscriber’s allowed usage than they really should. People using GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol will now reach their plan limits about 40% and 25% sooner for the same work, although OpenAI says it will not bill them for the earlier error. Go, Plus, Pro, and Business subscribers will receive one extra usage reset, but the change may still mean they can do less before waiting for their limit to renew.

Summary
OpenAI fixed a usage-accounting bug that had undercounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol consumption, so users will now reach plan limits about 40% and 25% faster, respectively. It is providing eligible ChatGPT subscribers with one extra reset as compensation.

OpenAI said it fixed a billing mistake that had incorrectly discounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage for most users. As a result, the same work will now consume more of a user’s plan allowance. Compared with the prior week, OpenAI says users will reach their limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol. The change affects these two models, while other models retain their existing usage calculations.

OpenAI said it will not charge users for the usage difference resulting from the mistake. As an apology, it is adding one banked reset for subscribers on Go, Plus, Pro, and Business plans. Users can restore exhausted limits through ChatGPT Settings by opening Usage and clicking Reset, and the added reset expires one month later. The article notes that the one-time reset does not equal the previously discounted ongoing usage, although OpenAI says both models remain more usage-efficient than GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.

  • A single extra usage reset does not provide the same ongoing capacity as the previous discounted accounting, because GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol work will continue to consume allowances faster after the reset is used.
  • Eligible subscribers can restore exhausted usage limits by going to ChatGPT Settings, selecting Usage, and clicking Reset; the added reset expires after one month.
  • The accounting change is limited to GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, so users of other models retain their existing usage calculations.
  • OpenAI says users will not be charged for the usage difference caused by the prior accounting mistake.
Key Points
  • 01OpenAI fixed a billing mistake that had incorrectly discounted usage of its GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol models for most users.
  • 02Following the fix, OpenAI says equivalent work will consume more plan allowance, causing users to reach limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol compared with the prior week.
  • 03OpenAI stated that GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol remain more usage-efficient than their predecessors, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.
  • 04OpenAI will add one banked usage reset for Go, Plus, Pro, and Business subscribers as an apology, and the reset expires one month later.
  • 05The revised usage calculation affects GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, while OpenAI says other models retain their existing calculations.

In plain words

This weekly roundup describes attacks and security failures affecting online services, including Todoist customers, online stores, hospitals and universities, banks, and millions of websites using NGINX. Criminals used stolen passwords, misleading ads, fake software updates, openly accessible AI computers, and poorly configured systems to steal information, run unwanted programs, redirect shoppers, and demand money. The real-world impact can include exposed customer data, stolen payment details, closed clinics, diverted ambulances, and organizations paying for computing services that strangers use without permission. It also shows that simple oversights, such as leaving a service open to the internet or giving an account too much access, can have widespread consequences.

Summary
This week’s threat roundup highlights how ordinary weaknesses—stolen credentials, exposed AI services, unmonitored infrastructure software, and misconfigured applications—are enabling major compromises. Notable developments include 10 million potentially vulnerable NGINX services, more than 250 malicious browser extensions, rising ransomware activity, and AI infrastructure abuse.

This ThreatsDay roundup covers a range of November 2026 security developments, with many incidents stemming from stolen credentials, internet-exposed services, and unsafe configurations rather than novel technical exploits. Todoist said an attacker used a stolen version-control credential to access production systems and retrieve some customer information affecting 6% of users. Censys reported that more than 10 million exposed NGINX services run affected HTTP/2 versions associated with NGINX2Root flaws, while Rapid7 identified thousands of accessible AI and notebook servers that could be misused without authentication.

The report also highlights threats that can evade ordinary checks. Microsoft documented more than 250 browser extensions whose data collection, advertising injection, and shopping-link changes were activated when installations came through paid search advertisements, and a WordPress malware campaign served harmful JavaScript only to logged-out visitors. Ransomware activity rose 23% in October 2026 to 1,049 claimed incidents, with attacks affecting critical services such as hospitals and universities; one group’s activity reportedly led to ambulance diversions, clinic closures, and campus-wide device shutdowns. Other cases involved malicious Minecraft mods, phishing pages hosted through AI website-building services, compromised online shops, and attackers abusing power-management or banking middleware to run commands and access sensitive systems.

  • Some malicious browser extensions activated harmful behavior only when users arrived through paid search ads, meaning an extension could appear legitimate during ordinary testing or review.
  • Rapid7 found that more than half of exposed Ollama servers had already been accessed by someone other than their owner, and 79% listed at least one cloud model that could use the owner’s paid subscription.
  • A WordPress malware campaign deliberately stayed inactive for logged-in users while serving arbitrary JavaScript to anonymous visitors, making a compromised site appear normal to its administrators.
  • An attacker used CyberPower PowerPanel Business event scripts to run commands as the highest-privileged Windows account, extract Active Directory secrets, and establish a persistent implant.
  • Ransomware activity became more fragmented in Q3 2026: the ten most active groups accounted for 49% of victims, down from 71% in Q1.
Key Points
  • 01Todoist said an attacker used a stolen version-control credential to access production systems, obtain additional credentials, and retrieve customer information affecting 6% of its users.
  • 02Microsoft documented more than 250 browser extensions that behaved normally unless installed through paid search advertisements, which activated browsing-data collection, ad injection, and shopping-link changes.
  • 03Rapid7 found thousands of internet-facing AI and notebook service hosts without authentication, including exposed Ollama servers that could let others use an owner’s paid AI subscription.
  • 04Censys reported that more than 10 million exposed NGINX services running affected HTTP/2 versions could be vulnerable to NGINX2Root flaws under specific feature configurations.
  • 05NCC Group reported 1,049 claimed ransomware incidents in October 2026, a 23% increase from September.
