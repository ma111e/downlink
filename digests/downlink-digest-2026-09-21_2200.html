In plain words

This weekly report summarizes recent digital break-ins, harmful software, and software flaws affecting governments, businesses, and internet users worldwide. Recent incidents exposed information from Japanese government-related systems and the Gyazo image-sharing service, disrupted systems on two oil tankers headed to Texas, and inserted harmful scripts onto roughly 100,000 websites through Brevo. It also warns that criminals and government-linked groups are using fake job offers, deceptive sign-in requests, and AI tools to steal money, account access, and personal information. The stakes are high because these events can interrupt essential services, expose private data, and give attackers lasting access to people’s accounts and organizations.

Summary
This weekly threat intelligence report highlights major breaches, active cyber campaigns, AI-enabled threats, and critical vulnerabilities requiring urgent patching, including actively exploited Cisco ISE flaws. Key developments include North Korea-linked WaterPlum activity, supply-chain compromise affecting Brevo websites, and large-scale data exposure incidents in Japan.

The 21 September threat intelligence report covers major breaches, cyber campaigns, AI-related threats, and security updates. Japan’s Digital Agency said a VPN appliance flaw exposed about 246,000 records, while Helpfeel reported that a vulnerable Gyazo image-upload server exposed more than 23 million user records and 490 million image-metadata records. Cyberattacks also disrupted onboard systems on two oil tankers traveling to Texas, and Brevo reported that a compromised Cloudflare API key was used to place malicious ClickFix scripts on roughly 100,000 websites. These incidents affected government-related systems, online services, websites, and maritime operations.

The report highlights AI as both a tool used by attackers and a target for attack, citing AI-assisted ransomware activity, criminal markets for stolen model access, and flaws affecting coding agents and enterprise copilots. It also describes Luciferus, an underground AI service capable of generating simple remote-access trojan code, and BragJack, which used malicious browser extensions to hijack AI assistants. Several major patches are covered, including Cisco fixes for CVE-2026-76460 and CVE-2026-76461, with Cisco aware of active exploitation of the former. Threat research also identified North Korea-linked WaterPlum activity, FamousSparrow espionage using the SparroWocky backdoor, Handala-linked HEAVYGRAM surveillance malware, and GhostCode phishing that abuses Microsoft’s device authorization flow to gain persistent account access.

  • A compromised Cloudflare API key enabled attackers to affect about 100,000 websites through Brevo components, showing how access to one service-provider credential can spread harmful content across many unrelated sites.
  • GhostCode can give attackers lasting Microsoft 365 access by capturing authorization tokens through the legitimate device sign-in process, without needing the victim’s password or directly defeating multi-factor authentication.
  • Cisco reported active exploitation of CVE-2026-76460, making its patch especially time-sensitive compared with vulnerabilities that are only known to exist.
  • The report describes AI as both an attacker tool and a target: it is being used to support intrusions while coding assistants and enterprise copilots are also subject to security weaknesses.
Key Points
  • 01Japan’s Digital Agency confirmed that attackers exploited a VPN appliance flaw, exposing approximately 246,000 records containing names and contact details of government officials and contractors.
  • 02Brevo said a compromised Cloudflare API key was used to inject malicious ClickFix scripts into about 100,000 websites that use Brevo components.
  • 03US, Japanese, Australian, and German authorities warned that the North Korea-linked WaterPlum campaign infected at least 30,000 devices in more than 100 countries and stole funds or credentials from over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets.
  • 04Cisco addressed CVE-2026-76460 and CVE-2026-76461, critical flaws in Cisco ISE and Secure Email Gateway, and stated that CVE-2026-76460 is under active exploitation.
  • 05Researchers identified GhostCode as a phishing kit that abuses Microsoft’s device authorization process to capture tokens and obtain persistent Microsoft 365 account access without stealing passwords or directly bypassing multi-factor authentication.
