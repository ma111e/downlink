In plain words

Italy fined Meta €805 million because Facebook on Android suggested photos and videos from people’s phone galleries without clearly asking for separate permission for that feature. The decision affects about 20 million Android Facebook users in Italy, including people who had already allowed the app to access their galleries. Meta must turn the feature off by default and clearly ask users before using it, highlighting that app permission requests should plainly explain what private information an app can access and why.

Summary
Italy’s data protection authority fined Meta €805 million for Facebook’s Android gallery-suggestion feature, finding it accessed users’ photo libraries by default without specific, informed consent. Meta must disable the feature by default and obtain separate consent within 60 days, while it plans to appeal.

Italy’s data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta €805 million over a Facebook for Android feature that suggests photos and videos from users’ phone galleries for posting. The authority found the feature was enabled by default and accessed photos, videos, and related metadata without users giving separate, explicit consent. The case concerns about 20 million Android users in Italy, and Meta said it plans to appeal the decision as disproportionate.

The Garante found that Facebook used a single gallery permission for multiple functions without clearly explaining that it also enabled gallery suggestions. It ordered Meta to turn the feature off by default within 60 days, including for users who had previously granted gallery access, and to seek specific consent through a separate request. The authority said access to information stored on a phone requires free and informed consent under Italy’s Privacy Code, even if the information is not sent elsewhere or no harm is shown.

  • The Italian authority concluded that a single broad gallery permission was insufficient because it covered several app functions and did not clearly tell users they were also enabling gallery suggestions.
  • Under the Italian rule applied in the case, accessing information stored on a phone requires free and informed consent even if no data is transmitted elsewhere and no harm is shown.
  • The order requires Meta to seek new, separate consent even from people who had already granted Facebook gallery access, rather than treating past permission as valid for this feature.
  • Italy’s authority rejected Meta’s argument that Ireland’s data protection authority should lead the case because the applicable ePrivacy rules do not use a one-stop-shop process.
  • The investigation did not assess whether gallery material was used to train AI models, and Meta denied that it had done so.
Key Points
  • 01Italy’s data protection authority fined Meta €805 million over Facebook for Android’s gallery-suggestion feature.
  • 02The authority found that the feature was enabled by default and accessed users’ photos, videos, and related metadata without separate, explicit consent.
  • 03Meta has 60 days to disable the feature by default, including for users who previously granted gallery access, and to obtain specific consent through a separate request.
  • 04The investigation concerned roughly 20 million Android users in Italy and found that the breach remains ongoing for some users.
  • 05Meta said it will appeal the decision and maintains that users grant device permission and can change it at any time.
