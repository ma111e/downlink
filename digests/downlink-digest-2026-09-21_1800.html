CISA alerts of active exploitation of three Linux kernel flaws

In plain words

The U.S. cybersecurity agency CISA says attackers are actively using three security flaws in the Linux kernel, a core part of many computer systems. One flaw had existed for 14 years, and the issues could let attackers crash systems, change cryptographic results, or gain higher access, including escaping restricted software containers. U.S. federal agencies were told to install available fixes immediately and check affected computers for signs they were already compromised, while organizations using Linux should also assess whether their systems are affected.

Summary
CISA says three Linux kernel vulnerabilities, including critical CVE-2025-39964, are being actively exploited and has ordered U.S. federal agencies to patch affected systems and perform forensic triage immediately.

CISA warned that attackers are actively exploiting three Linux kernel vulnerabilities: CVE-2025-39964, CVE-2026-53266, and CVE-2025-39682. The flaws range from medium to critical severity, and CVE-2025-39964 had existed in the kernel for 14 years. CISA gave the issues its highest priority for U.S. federal agencies, directing them to apply available updates and mitigations by the end of the day.

CVE-2025-39964 is a race condition in the AF_ALG cryptographic socket interface that may crash systems or alter cryptographic results, while researchers demonstrated privilege escalation and container escape in Google’s kernelCTF. CVE-2026-53266 is an out-of-bounds write in ebtables SNAT that may modify shared file-backed memory, and CVE-2025-39682 involves mishandling zero-length TLS records when kTLS is used. CISA also required forensic triage for affected assets to identify signs of prior exploitation; public exploits are available for CVE-2025-39682, Red Hat confirmed a known exploit for CVE-2026-53266, and none of the flaws is currently marked as used by ransomware groups.

  • CVE-2025-39964 had remained in the Linux kernel for 14 years before being identified, and researchers demonstrated privilege escalation and container escape using it in Google’s kernelCTF.
  • CISA’s “forensic triage” designation requires federal agencies to examine every affected asset for evidence that exploitation may already have occurred, rather than only applying patches.
  • Although Red Hat confirmed a known exploit for CVE-2026-53266, the researcher’s proposed privilege-escalation chain has not been demonstrated with public exploit code.
  • None of the three Linux kernel flaws is currently marked as exploited by ransomware groups.
Key Points
  • 01CISA added three Linux kernel vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog after determining they had been exploited in attacks.
  • 02The vulnerabilities are CVE-2025-39964, CVE-2026-53266, and CVE-2025-39682, with severity ratings ranging from medium to critical.
  • 03CISA gave U.S. federal agencies its highest priority for the flaws, requiring them to apply available updates and mitigations by the end of the day and conduct forensic triage on affected assets.
  • 04Public exploits are available for CVE-2025-39682, and Red Hat has also confirmed a known exploit for CVE-2026-53266.

WordPress Click2Shell flaw lets hackers execute PHP on the server
bleepingcomputer.com · 21 Sep 2026 18:23 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra

In plain words

WordPress fixed a flaw called Click2Shell that could let a stranger trick a signed-in site administrator into adding a harmful website design template. That template could then run the stranger’s code on the site’s server, even if the template was not actively being used. This could expose visitor and user information, database passwords, and login secrets, and allow criminals to create fake administrator accounts or add harmful scripts. Website owners should update to WordPress 7.1.1 because instructions for abusing the flaw are publicly available.

Summary
WordPress fixed the Click2Shell CSRF flaw in version 7.1.1, which could let an attacker trick a logged-in administrator into installing a vulnerable theme and executing arbitrary PHP on the server. Administrators should update immediately, as technical details and a proof-of-concept exploit are public.

WordPress released version 7.1.1 to fix Click2Shell, a cross-site request forgery flaw in WordPress Core 7.1.0 and earlier. Security researcher Paulos Yibelo of pwn.ai reported the issue to WordPress on August 22 and later published technical details and a proof-of-concept exploit. The flaw has no official identifier.

Click2Shell can let an attacker without a WordPress account, installation nonce, or administrator privileges trick a logged-in administrator into visiting a crafted link that force-installs a theme from the WordPress.org catalog. A flaw involving a theme-preview URL, the WordPress.org Themes API, and JavaScript in the administrator’s browser can cause an inactive vulnerable theme to execute arbitrary PHP during a Customizer preview. This could allow file changes or deletion, access to user data and the wp-config.php file, creation of rogue administrator accounts, and injection of malicious scripts; Patchstack says DISALLOW_FILE_MODS can prevent forced theme or plugin installation until an update is applied.

  • The attack chain does not require the attacker to have a WordPress account, an installation approval token, or administrative access; it only requires a logged-in administrator to open a crafted link.
  • A theme does not need to be activated for its server-side code to run, because the code can execute during a Customizer preview.
  • Author and Editor accounts cannot trigger the full chain because they lack permission to install themes, limiting the affected user role to administrators.
  • Sites with DISALLOW_FILE_MODS enabled cannot be forced to install the theme or a malicious plugin, providing a temporary mitigation for organizations unable to update immediately.
Key Points
  • 01WordPress 7.1.1 fixes the Click2Shell flaw, which affects WordPress Core 7.1.0 and earlier versions.
  • 02Click2Shell can allow an unauthenticated attacker to trick a logged-in WordPress administrator into installing a theme from the official WordPress.org catalog.
  • 03During a Customizer preview, an inactive vulnerable theme can execute arbitrary PHP code on the server.
  • 04Paulos Yibelo of pwn.ai published technical details and a proof-of-concept exploit for the issue.
  • 05Patchstack said that enabling DISALLOW_FILE_MODS prevents a website from being forced to install a theme or malicious plugin when an immediate update is not possible.

It warrants prompt attention because successful exploitation could expose credentials, enable rogue administrator accounts, and alter site files.

In plain words

BigCommerce says criminals stole access credentials for two Ribon apps and used them to place harmful code on a small number of online stores, allowing them to view existing customer information. Customers at affected shops, including UK spirits seller Master of Malt, may have had their names, email addresses, phone numbers, and delivery addresses exposed, though the company says passwords and card details were stored separately and were not exposed. The case shows that a weakness at a third-party service connected to many online stores can put shoppers’ personal details at risk even when the main shopping platform itself was not broken into.

Summary
BigCommerce removed compromised Ribon and Ribon 1.5 third-party apps after attackers used stolen application credentials to inject malicious scripts and access customer data at a small number of merchant stores. Exposed data may include names, emails, phone numbers, and shipping addresses, while payment-card information and BigCommerce’s core platform were reportedly not affected.

BigCommerce alerted multiple merchants after attackers compromised credentials for the third-party Ribon and Ribon 1.5 applications. The stolen credentials were used to inject malicious scripts into a small number of merchant storefronts and access shopper data in BigCommerce environments between September 13 and September 17. BigCommerce said it confirmed the credential compromise on September 17, 2026, and removed the apps to protect customers. The company stated that its systems and core platform were not breached.

UK-based spirits seller Master of Malt said affected information included customers’ full names, email addresses, phone numbers, and shipping postal addresses. BigCommerce said account passwords and payment-card information are stored separately and were not exposed. It uninstalled the applications from affected stores to revoke the attacker’s access, directly notified merchants, and supplied logs for the developer’s investigation. The incident may extend beyond Master of Malt, as several retailers are reportedly notifying customers about data exposure tied to the Ribon application key theft.

  • The attackers did not need to breach BigCommerce’s core platform; compromised credentials for a connected third-party app were sufficient to access customer records in merchant environments.
  • Removing the affected apps from stores was used to immediately revoke the attackers’ access, illustrating that disconnecting a compromised integration can contain an incident.
  • Unlike the earlier FreshClick incident involving ZAGG, this case reportedly exposed existing customer records rather than payment details entered during checkout.
  • Although only Master of Malt is named as an affected merchant, the article indicates that several retailers may be notifying customers and that the impact could extend to hundreds of stores.
Key Points
  • 01BigCommerce confirmed on September 17, 2026, that credentials for the third-party Ribon and Ribon 1.5 applications had been compromised and used to inject malicious scripts into a small number of merchant storefronts.
  • 02BigCommerce removed the affected applications from impacted stores, notified merchants directly, and provided log data to support the application developer’s investigation.
  • 03Master of Malt said attackers accessed shopper information between September 13 and September 17, including full names, email addresses, phone numbers, and shipping postal addresses.
  • 04BigCommerce stated that its own systems and platform were not breached and that account passwords and payment-card information were stored separately and were not exposed.
  • 05The incident may affect more than Master of Malt, with a law firm reporting that several retailers are notifying customers about data exposure connected to the Ribon application key theft.

JUSTIFICATION

This is a concrete third-party application credential compromise affecting BigCommerce merchant storefronts and potentially shopper data across multiple retailers. It is broadly relevant to organizations relying on ecommerce integrations, though the article does not identify an ongoing exploitation campaign or a required customer patch.

In plain words

Ireland’s privacy regulator fined Google more than €403 million ($462 million) over how it collected, kept, and used people’s location information in Europe. The regulator said people may not have understood that their locations could be used to shape advertising or guess their interests, and that Google kept some of this data longer than necessary. Google has six months to change its practices, highlighting that location records can reveal highly personal details about people’s lives.

Summary
Ireland’s Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million ($462 million) for GDPR violations involving the processing and retention of user location data. Google must bring its practices into compliance within six months.

Ireland’s Data Protection Commission (DPC) fined Google more than €403 million ($462 million) following an inquiry into its processing of location data. The DPC, Google’s lead European Union privacy regulator because Google’s European headquarters are in Dublin, ordered the company to correct its processing practices within six months. The investigation began in February 2020 after European consumer-rights groups raised concerns about possible violations of Europe’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

The inquiry examined Google’s handling of location data in its Web & App Activity, Location History, and Location Accuracy features between May 2018 and February 2020. The regulator assessed whether the processing was legal and fair and whether Google met transparency and accountability requirements, including requirements concerning retention of location data. The DPC said location information can reveal highly private details and that users may have been unaware it was used to influence ads or infer interests; retaining it longer than necessary increased users’ loss of control over their personal data.

  • Although the investigation concerned practices from May 2018 through February 2020, the DPC has given Google six months to change its current processing practices.
  • The DPC said keeping location data longer than necessary worsened people’s loss of control over their personal information.
  • This is the first time Ireland’s Data Protection Commission has fined Google, despite the regulator serving as Google’s lead EU privacy authority because its European headquarters are in Dublin.
Key Points
  • 01Ireland’s Data Protection Commission fined Google more than €403 million ($462 million) over its processing of location data.
  • 02The regulator ordered Google to correct its data-processing practices within six months.
  • 03The inquiry examined Google’s handling of location data through Web & App Activity, Location History, and Location Accuracy from May 2018 through February 2020.
  • 04The DPC found that Google’s practices violated GDPR requirements concerning lawful and fair processing, transparency, accountability, and the retention of location data.
  • 05The regulator said location data can reveal highly private information and that Google’s failures could have left people unaware that their location was used to influence advertising or infer their interests.
