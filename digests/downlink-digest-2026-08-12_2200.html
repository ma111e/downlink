"City-Forum" data-theft attacks target Salesforce, ServiceNow portals
bleepingcomputer.com · 12 Aug 2026 23:07 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.5

In plain words

A data theft campaign called City-Forum is taking information from company customer portals built with Salesforce and ServiceNow when those portals have been set up to let public visitors see too much. The targets include telecom companies, banks, software vendors, security and privacy companies, and public-sector portals around the world. This matters because the attackers do not need to break into the systems if sensitive records are accidentally left open to anyone on the Internet, and the activity is still increasing.

Summary
A campaign dubbed City-Forum is stealing data from misconfigured Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow customer portals that expose records to unauthenticated guest users. The activity is ongoing and appears to come from a single long-running Contabo-hosted IP, with defenders urged to lock down guest access, public APIs, self-registration, and portal search sources.

An ongoing data theft campaign dubbed City-Forum is using custom tools to steal data exposed to anonymous users through Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow customer portals. SaaS security firm Reco traced the campaign to a single server that has targeted organizations worldwide, including telecommunications companies, banks and financial services firms, enterprise software vendors, security and data privacy companies, and public-sector portals. Reco says the attacks are ongoing and that activity continues to increase.

Reco says the attacks originate from the IP address 158.220.87.79, hosted by German VPS provider Contabo, and usually use the default Go-http-client/1.1 user agent when downloading data. The campaign is not exploiting a vulnerability in Salesforce or ServiceNow, but is abusing data that organizations have mistakenly exposed to unauthenticated guest users through overly permissive sharing rules, permissions, or portal configurations. The same infrastructure and fingerprint have been observed against both Salesforce and ServiceNow environments across multiple organizations.

On Salesforce, the attacker targets older Aura endpoints such as /aura or /s/sfsites/aura, invokes HostConfigController.getConfigData to enumerate objects, and uses SelectableListDataProviderController.getItems to retrieve records. The attacker also targets newer Lightning Web Runtime sites through GraphQL requests to /webruntime/api/services/data/{version}/graphql, and checks /SiteRegister and /CommunitiesSelfReg for self-registration. On ServiceNow, the attacker targets POST /api/now/sp/search?sysparm_cancelable=true, and Reco advises administrators to review guest access, public APIs, self-registration, and exposed portal search sources.

  • Reco observed the same fingerprint across both Salesforce and ServiceNow environments, suggesting defenders can look for the combination of IP address 158.220.87.79 and the default Go-http-client/1.1 user agent across multiple portal platforms.
  • The campaign includes Salesforce Lightning Web Runtime sites through GraphQL requests to the webruntime API, a technique Reco says it has not seen in public attack tools such as AuraInspector, S-RET, or CirrusGo.
  • Disabling the Experience Builder option that allows guest users to access public APIs can block several Salesforce API endpoints used for data enumeration and theft when guest API access is not required.
  • ServiceNow transaction logs do not record the POST body for the targeted portal search endpoint, so defenders may be able to see that automated searches occurred and how much data was returned but not the exact search terms used.
Key Points
  • 01Reco says an ongoing data theft campaign dubbed City-Forum is using custom tools to steal data exposed to anonymous users through Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow customer portals.
  • 02The attacks have been traced to the IP address 158.220.87.79, hosted by German VPS provider Contabo and associated with the city-forum.com domain since at least March 2025.
  • 03Reco says the campaign is not exploiting vulnerabilities in Salesforce or ServiceNow, but is abusing overly permissive guest-user sharing rules, permissions, or portal configurations.
  • 04On Salesforce, the attacker targets Aura endpoints and newer Lightning Web Runtime sites to enumerate and retrieve records exposed to guest accounts.
  • 05On ServiceNow, the attacker targets the POST /api/now/sp/search?sysparm_cancelable=true endpoint to search Service Portals that allow guest access to data.

Android malware combo takes out loans and relays victims' credit cards

In plain words

The article describes criminals calling people while pretending to be bank employees, then convincing them to install a harmful Android app and tap their payment card on the phone. In one case, the criminals remotely used the victim’s phone to take out a loan in the victim’s name and used the card information to make purchases during a 13-minute call. Android users, especially in Czechia, Slovakia, and Slovenia, are affected, and the broader risk is that a phone call plus a fake app can lead quickly to real financial theft.

Summary
Attackers are combining the SpyNote Android RAT with new WindRelay NFC malware to remotely control victims’ phones, take out loans, and relay live payment card data for fraudulent transactions. The attack relies heavily on phone-based social engineering, including convincing victims to sideload an app, grant permissions, tap their card to the phone, and enter their PIN.

A new Android NFC relay malware called WindRelay is being used together with the SpyNote remote administration tool to steal card data and send it to attackers in real time. In an incident investigated by Group-IB, a fraudster impersonated a bank employee and convinced the victim to sideload SpyNote disguised as a legitimate app. The attacker also had the victim grant Accessibility Service permissions, giving remote access to the Android device.

After gaining access through SpyNote, the attacker installed WindRelay without further interaction from the victim and used the banking app to take out a loan in the victim’s name. The victim was then instructed to tap their payment card on the phone and enter their PIN. WindRelay turned the phone into a fraudulent contactless reader and relayed the live NFC exchange, including transaction-specific authentication data, to the attacker’s device for purchases at a genuine payment terminal.

Group-IB said the entire activity occurred during a 13-minute phone call, and transactions were approved using the PIN provided by the victim. The researchers said the combination may indicate a toolkit that provides both access to a victim’s device for banking transactions and a direct cash-out channel. Group-IB identified almost two dozen WindRelay samples submitted to VirusTotal between November 2025 and July 2026, with apparent targeting focused on Czechia, Slovakia, and Slovenia.

  • The combination of SpyNote and WindRelay gives attackers both remote access to a victim’s banking app and a direct way to cash out through relayed card transactions.
  • The attack did not require the victim to interact with WindRelay directly after SpyNote was installed, because the attacker used remote access to install WindRelay without further victim interaction.
  • The fraud succeeded without relying on modern malware features such as live screen sharing or VNC, showing that phone-based social engineering alone was enough to guide the victim through the theft.
  • Personalizing the malicious app label with the victim’s name was used to make the fake app appear more credible during the social engineering call.
  • A practical defensive step from the article is to hang up on urgent bank calls and call the official number from the organization’s website before taking any requested action.
