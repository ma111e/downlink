In plain words

Researchers at Citizen Lab say the phone of former European Parliament member Stelios Kouloglou was secretly infected more than once with Pegasus, a tool that can take over a phone without the owner clicking anything. This happened while he was serving on a Parliament committee investigating abuse of such phone-spying tools, and researchers believe the same customer was also behind spying on Russian and Belarusian journalists and opposition figures. The people affected include Kouloglou, other public figures, journalists, and potentially members of Parliament, and the article says this matters because spying on lawmakers while they investigate spyware threatens democratic oversight and privacy.

Summary
Citizen Lab says former European Parliament member Stelios Kouloglou was infected with Pegasus spyware while serving on the committee investigating spyware abuse, including during key periods of its work. Researchers linked the targeting to the same Pegasus operator behind attacks on Russian and Belarusian journalists and opposition figures, underscoring concerns that commercial spyware is being used against democratic oversight.

Citizen Lab reported that the phone of former European Parliament member Stelios Kouloglou was infected with Pegasus spyware in October 2022 and again in March 2023. At the time, Kouloglou was serving on the PEGA Committee, which was investigating misuse of commercial spyware and preparing recommendations for addressing abuse in Europe. Kouloglou, also a longtime investigative reporter, told Recorded Future News he believes the Greek government was responsible, but Citizen Lab said it has no indications that this is true.

The infections occurred during sensitive periods for the PEGA Committee, including less than a week before hearings and as members prepared a draft report, and later during intense discussions about the final report. Hannah Neumann, a German Green Party representative and PEGA Committee negotiator, said the incident showed disregard for parliamentarians’ scrutiny role and for European democracy. The committee released recommendations in May 2023, but the article says the European Commission has largely ignored them.

Pegasus is described in the article as powerful zero-click spyware, and Citizen Lab linked the Kouloglou incidents to attacks on seven Russian and Belarusian-speaking journalists and opposition figures between August 2020 and January 2023. Citizen Lab said the same email used against Kouloglou was also deployed in those attacks, and that targeting emails are unique to specific operators. Kouloglou brought his phone to Citizen Lab in May 2026, and researchers found evidence that he had received three Apple threat notifications about potential spyware, though he said he never saw them.

  • The timing of both infections was especially sensitive because they occurred shortly before major PEGA Committee hearings and during intense discussions over the committee’s draft and final spyware reports.
  • Citizen Lab’s finding that the same targeting email was used in both Kouloglou’s case and the Russian and Belarusian cases provides a concrete operational link between otherwise separate spyware campaigns.
  • Kouloglou had received three Apple threat notifications about possible spyware in recent years but said he never saw them, showing that even official warnings may fail to reach or alert intended targets.
  • The reported operator appears to have had Pegasus licensing that allowed targeting in multiple countries, which Citizen Lab said narrows the pool of possible government customers.
  • The European Commission’s lack of action on the PEGA Committee’s May 2023 recommendations is presented by multiple cited experts and politicians as a continuing policy failure despite direct evidence of spyware use against a committee member.
