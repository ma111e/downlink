Fortinet warns of critical FortiMail flaw exploited in zero-day attacks

In plain words

Fortinet says criminals are already using a serious flaw in its FortiMail email-security systems to take control of vulnerable devices without needing a password. Organizations using certain FortiMail versions are affected, and the intruders may be able to add or change files, run commands, and potentially send stored information to their own servers. Until updates are available, Fortinet recommends turning off a related email-encryption feature or limiting access to the system’s administrative controls, while U.S. federal agencies have been ordered to check for compromise and reduce the risk.

Summary
Fortinet says attackers are actively exploiting the critical FortiMail path traversal and null-byte flaw CVE-2026-104286 to write arbitrary files and execute unauthorized code on exposed management interfaces. Administrators should immediately disable IBE or restrict management access and conduct forensic triage until fixed releases are available.

Fortinet warned that CVE-2026-104286, a critical FortiMail vulnerability with a CVSS score of 9.8, is being actively exploited in zero-day attacks. The flaw affects the FortiMail management interface and can allow an unauthenticated attacker to write arbitrary files to the underlying system using crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests. Fortinet said the issue involves path traversal and improper handling of NULL characters, which could enable unauthorized code or command execution.

Affected releases include FortiMail 8.0.0 through 8.0.1, 7.6.0 through 7.6.6, 7.4.0 through 7.4.8, and 7.2.0 through 7.2.9. Fortinet advised customers to disable IBE support or restrict management-interface access to trusted private networks until fixes are available; fixed versions for the 7.4, 7.6, and 8.0 branches were listed as upcoming. The company also published file, IP address, and log indicators for forensic checks, while CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and required U.S. federal agencies to perform forensic triage and mitigate it by October 4.

  • Security updates were not yet available for affected FortiMail 7.4, 7.6, and 8.0 installations at the time of the advisory, so Fortinet directed customers to use temporary mitigations instead.
  • Fortinet’s indicators include an archive account configured to send data to remote IP address 79.141.169.187 in an /uploads directory, which the article says could indicate that archived data was being transferred from a compromised appliance.
  • The published compromise indicators include added or modified system files, including /data/etc/ld.so.preload and /data/lib/liblog.so, giving administrators concrete artifacts to check during forensic triage.
  • Fortinet provided log examples involving an IBE decoding error, a cron job related to /migadmin, and failed login attempts that administrators can use to identify potentially affected appliances.
