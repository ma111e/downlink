Google Calendar Phishing: One Link, Two Scanner Verdicts
ironscales.com · 27 Sep 2026 11:00 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGScloudflaregooglegoogle-calendargoogle-workspaceironscalesnamecheapspearphishingurl-redirection-abusevoiceml-space
78
In plain words

An attacker created a real Google Calendar event with a fake voicemail message and invited a VIP employee at an online entertainment technology company. Google sent the invitation from its legitimate systems, making the message appear trustworthy and hiding the harmful website behind a Google web address. Automated checks gave different results depending on which link they examined, but the employee reported the suspicious invite before it caused harm. The case matters because criminals can use trusted everyday services to reach people, so a simple way for employees to report strange messages can prevent damage when automated tools miss them.

Summary
Attackers abused Google Calendar to send a fully authenticated phishing invite containing a Google redirect link to a domain registered only 29 minutes earlier, causing URL scanners to rate the trusted wrapper clean while the underlying destination received only a partial warning. The incident shows that email defenses must resolve and assess final destinations rather than trust platform redirectors or sender authentication.

An attacker created a Google Calendar event titled “New Voice Recording Received,” placed a voicemail-themed lure in its description, and invited a VIP mailbox at a digital advertising group. Google Calendar sent the notification through its legitimate infrastructure, with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all passing. The message included a “Listen to voicemail” link that Google rewrote through its own google.com URL redirector.

The final destination, voiceml[.]space, was registered only 29 minutes and 26 seconds before Google sent the notification. A scan of the bare destination returned a partial “Mixed Result” verdict, while the Google redirect URL was marked clean, despite leading to the same site. The article argues that URL defenses should resolve redirectors and evaluate the final destination, while calendar notifications should assess the event organizer and Reply-To rather than relying only on Google’s trusted sender identity.

The message was ultimately reported by its recipient and mitigated after a human manual finding identified the voicemail link as malicious. The article notes that Google’s duplicated description text in hidden preview and metadata fields was normal template behavior, not attacker obfuscation. It presents the incident as an example of attackers borrowing trusted productivity platforms for delivery and authentication while using newly registered infrastructure that has little or no established reputation.

  • A clean verdict for a Google redirect URL assessed the trusted Google wrapper rather than the website ultimately reached by the recipient, demonstrating why redirectors should be resolved before reputation scoring.
  • Sender-history and authentication checks were ineffective by design because the attacker used Google’s legitimate Calendar notification service, which already had an established relationship with the recipient organization.
  • The event organizer and Reply-To address provided more useful attribution signals than the visible Google sender, because both pointed to an unrelated third-party mailbox.
  • The message body repeated the event description in hidden preview and metadata fields due to Google’s standard notification template, so treating those duplicates as attacker obfuscation would create a false finding.
  • The recipient’s report was the control that resolved conflicting automated URL verdicts and led to mitigation of the message.
Key Points
  • 01An attacker placed a voicemail lure in a Google Calendar event description and invited a VIP mailbox at a digital advertising group, prompting Google to send an authenticated calendar notification.
  • 02The malicious destination voiceml[.]space was registered 29 minutes and 26 seconds before Google sent the notification, and the bare URL received a partial "Mixed Result" scanner verdict.
  • 03Google Calendar rewrote the lure link through a google.com redirector, which was scanned as clean even though its q parameter led to the same destination.
  • 04SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all passed because the message was genuinely sent by Google Calendar rather than forged by the attacker.
  • 05The recipient reported the suspicious message, and the message was mitigated after a human manual approval recorded the malicious finding.

This is a concrete phishing incident showing how attackers can abuse Google Calendar’s legitimate notification and URL-redirection mechanisms to bypass sender reputation and create conflicting link-scanning results. It is broadly relevant to email-security teams, though it does not describe a widespread campaign, major breach, or active exploitation of a software flaw.

Anthropic turns Claude into an AI marketplace with 2,000+ plugins and connectors

In plain words

Anthropic has opened a public Claude Marketplace with more than 2,000 add-ons and connections that let its Claude AI work with services from companies such as Google, Microsoft, Atlassian, and Salesforce. Businesses can also find Claude-based products and hire consulting firms including Accenture, Boston Consulting Group, and Deloitte to help introduce Claude across their organizations. This matters because it could make it much easier for companies to add AI features to the tools they already use, while giving software makers a way to reach Claude customers. Its success is uncertain, since similar AI app marketplaces have struggled in the past.

Summary
Anthropic launched the Claude Marketplace with more than 2,000 plugins, connectors, agents, and consulting offerings, aiming to make Claude a central platform for third-party AI integrations and products.

Anthropic has launched a public Claude Marketplace that brings together AI-related plugins, connectors, agents, and other products. The marketplace already offers more than 2,000 connectors and plugins, including offerings from Atlassian, Google, Microsoft, Notion, and Salesforce. Companies can also purchase Claude-powered agents and products from partners such as CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Legora, Lovable, and Snowflake.

The marketplace also includes consulting and systems integration partners, including Accenture, Boston Consulting Group, and Deloitte, for organizations seeking help deploying Claude. Developers can create connectors and plugins using Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent Skills, while companies selling Claude-powered software can apply for marketplace listings. Anthropic says the marketplace is intended to help teams find products that work with Claude and give developers and partners a direct route to Claude customers, though the article notes that a similar OpenAI apps marketplace did not succeed.

  • Claude Marketplace extends beyond basic app connections by combining plugins, connectors, Claude-powered agents, partner products, and consulting services in one place.
  • Anthropic is opening publishing to developers through Model Context Protocol and Agent Skills, rather than limiting the marketplace to its own offerings or a small set of partners.
  • The article notes that OpenAI’s similar apps marketplace did not succeed, highlighting that major-company integrations alone may not determine whether Claude Marketplace gains traction.
Key Points
  • 01Anthropic has launched a public Claude Marketplace offering more than 2,000 connectors and plugins.
  • 02The marketplace includes integrations from companies including Atlassian, Google, Microsoft, Notion, and Salesforce.
  • 03Companies can buy Claude-powered agents and products from partners such as CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Legora, Lovable, and Snowflake.
  • 04Developers can build and publish Claude connectors and plugins using Model Context Protocol and Agent Skills.
  • 05The marketplace also lists consulting and systems integration partners, including Accenture, Boston Consulting Group, and Deloitte, for organizations deploying Claude.
