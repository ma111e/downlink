Attackers conceal phishing lures using invisible Unicode characters

In plain words

Attackers are sending millions of finance-themed scam emails that hide parts of words like “funding” by placing invisible characters inside them, making the words look normal to people but harder for some security checks to spot. Microsoft found this campaign in messages sent through infrastructure tied to the legitimate ActiveCampaign email-marketing platform, and Microsoft Defender still caught more than 99% of the messages using other warning signs. This matters because people and businesses receiving loan, funding, or credit emails may be targeted, and the same trick can also hide instructions from AI assistants unless the hidden characters are removed first.

Summary
Microsoft says attackers are hiding phishing keywords by inserting invisible Unicode tag characters, helping finance-themed lures evade some keyword-based email filters. Defenders should normalize or strip invisible Unicode characters before scanning email or passing content to AI tools.

Threat actors have adopted the ASCII smuggling technique in phishing campaigns by using invisible Unicode characters to evade email security filters. Microsoft threat researchers discovered a large-scale campaign using this technique that peaked at up to 2.37 million daily messages in late February. Microsoft said the high-volume phase persisted for roughly three months after February 9 and dropped sharply after May 15, 2026, while the broader campaign started earlier and continued without the technique.

In this campaign, attackers inserted invisible Unicode characters inside finance-related lure words to split them. For example, a keyword like “funding” could appear as “fun[invisible character]ding,” allowing it to evade filters that rely on word lists to detect suspicious or malicious messages. Microsoft said the method was used in millions of finance-themed phishing messages and worked as intended, although Defender still caught over 99% of the messages using other signals such as sender, IP, domain, and reputation checks.

Microsoft identified a cluster of 148 finance-themed sender domains on February 9 that accounted for about 96% of messages flagged by its new Defender for Office 365 hunting logic for Unicode-tag signatures. The messages promoted business funding, loans, and credit services and were delivered through infrastructure associated with the legitimate ActiveCampaign email-marketing platform. Microsoft recommends stripping or normalizing Unicode tag characters and other invisible code points before applying keyword, regex, or signature-based detection, and applying the same normalization before passing email content to AI assistants.

  • The campaign shows that a technique previously highlighted for AI prompt injection has moved into high-volume phishing evasion, with Microsoft observing up to 2.37 million daily messages during the peak period.
  • Microsoft’s finding that Defender still caught over 99% of the messages based on sender, IP, domain, and reputation signals indicates that layered detection can reduce the impact of keyword-obfuscation tricks.
  • A relatively small cluster of 148 finance-themed sender domains accounted for about 96% of messages flagged by Microsoft’s new hunting logic for Unicode-tag signatures on February 9.
  • Microsoft recommends applying the same Unicode normalization to email content before sending it to AI assistants, because the hidden-character technique can also conceal prompt-injection instructions.
