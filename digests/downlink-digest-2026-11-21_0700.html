Hackers Exploit Critical VMware, Cisco, and Apache Flaws as Espionage Attacks Emerge
thehackernews.com · 21 Nov 2026 08:49 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGSapachearmory-castleauthentication-bypassbeyondtrustbroadcomchinacisaciscodeserializationfederal-civilian-executive-branchhard-coded-credentialsrussiasapunited-statesvmware

In plain words

U.S. cyber officials added four serious software weaknesses affecting Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver, and VMware vCenter to a list of flaws that criminals or spies are already using. These weaknesses can let outsiders get into systems without permission, run commands, steal information, or change server data; one VMware flaw has been tied to a suspected China-linked spying group. U.S. federal agencies were told to install the available fixes by November 23, 2026, because unpatched systems could give attackers a path into important networks and sensitive data.

Summary
CISA added four actively exploited flaws in Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver, and VMware vCenter to its KEV catalog, urging U.S. federal agencies to patch by November 23, 2026. The VMware vCenter flaw has been linked to suspected China-nexus espionage group Armory Castle, while Cisco Expressway was used in a breach of a Russian organization.

CISA added four flaws affecting Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver Enterprise Portal, and Broadcom VMware vCenter to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after citing evidence of active exploitation. The affected issues include CVE-2026-129448 in Apache Roller, CVE-2026-76466 in Cisco Expressway, CVE-2025-42884 in SAP NetWeaver, and CVE-2026-123890 in VMware vCenter. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies were directed to patch all four by November 23, 2026.

The Apache Roller issue can let an unauthenticated remote attacker execute code through its XML-RPC endpoint, and its maintainers acknowledged that it is being actively exploited. Cisco Expressway’s hard-coded credentials flaw can enable root-level command execution on clustered devices, and F6 reported it was used to breach a Russian organization after a proof-of-concept exploit was released. The VMware vCenter authentication-bypass flaw has been linked to suspected China-nexus group Armory Castle, which reportedly combined it with a BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw to deploy custom backdoors for persistence and data theft.

  • SAP NetWeaver Enterprise Portal was added to CISA's actively exploited catalog even though the article says there were no public reports documenting exploitation of CVE-2025-42884 at the time of writing.
  • The Cisco Expressway flaw was reportedly used against a Russian organization after a proof-of-concept exploit became available, and the intruders then moved through the network and accessed sensitive data.
  • Armory Castle reportedly combined the VMware vCenter authentication bypass with a separate BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw, illustrating that attackers used multiple weaknesses together to maintain access and steal data.
Key Points
  • 01CISA added four vulnerabilities affecting Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver Enterprise Portal, and Broadcom VMware vCenter to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after finding evidence of active exploitation.
  • 02The Apache Roller flaw, CVE-2026-129448, can allow an unauthenticated remote attacker to run arbitrary code through its XML-RPC endpoint, and Apache Roller maintainers said it is being actively exploited.
  • 03CVE-2026-76466 in Cisco Expressway can allow an unauthenticated remote attacker with persistent cookies and network access to run commands as root on a clustered device through the database service.
  • 04A suspected China-nexus espionage group called Armory Castle reportedly used the VMware vCenter authentication-bypass flaw CVE-2026-123890 alongside a BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw to install custom backdoors and steal data.
  • 05U.S. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies were directed to patch all four vulnerabilities by November 23, 2026.

Signal Blocks AI Agents From Reading Private Chats via Screenshots on Android and iOS
thehackernews.com · 21 Nov 2026 08:49 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra

In plain words

Signal is turning on a setting by default that stops screenshots in an effort to keep phone and computer AI helpers from reading private Signal chats. The change affects Signal users on supported Android and iPhone devices, especially when an AI helper has been given broad permission to view what is on the screen, read notifications, or control parts of the device. It matters because these helpers can copy chat content outside Signal’s control, potentially exposing messages from everyone in a conversation even though Signal encrypts them. Signal says the protection is not complete and wants Apple, Google, and other companies to offer clearer, stronger ways for apps to block AI access.

Summary
Signal now blocks screenshots by default on supported Android and iOS devices to limit OS-level AI agents from reading private chats, though it acknowledges the safeguard cannot prevent all data-access paths. The change follows concerns that agents with broad accessibility, screenshot, or notification permissions can extract Signal content outside the app’s encryption protections.

Signal announced that it will restrict screenshots by default on supported Android and iOS devices to prevent operating-system AI agents from reading private chats. The feature applies to agentic systems built into iOS 27 and Android versions where Signal can detect an AI assistant attempting to read its screen. Signal said these systems can scan what is displayed, capture Signal data, and make it available to AI agents outside the app’s control. Meredith Whittaker, president of the Signal Foundation, called the integrations an existential threat to Signal’s privacy guarantees.

Users can disable the controls through Signal’s App Security settings, but Signal warned that doing so could expose their content and their contacts’ content to AI agents. The company acknowledged that screenshot blocking is incomplete because agents may retrieve data already present on a device through the file system or other methods. Reuters reported that Meta’s Muse agent accessed Signal messages through macOS accessibility and screenshot tools, and through Android push-notification access. Signal is urging technology companies to provide documented controls that let apps fully opt out of AI access to screens, files, notifications, APIs, and other operating-system functions.

  • Signal’s encryption cannot independently preserve privacy when an operating-system AI agent can capture chat content from the screen, notifications, or files after it is displayed on a device.
  • A single user granting broad permissions to an AI agent can create privacy exposure for every participant in that user’s Signal conversations, not only the person who enabled the agent.
  • Meta’s Muse reportedly accessed Signal on macOS through accessibility and screenshot permissions, while on Android it read Signal messages through push-notification access, showing that the access route can differ by operating system.
  • Signal’s screenshot restriction is only a partial safeguard because it does not stop an AI agent from retrieving Signal content that may already be stored on the device.
  • Apple’s iOS 27.2 includes an Excluded Apps setting for Siri, Search, and suggestions, giving users a separate operating-system control over which apps those features can access.
Key Points
  • 01Signal will restrict screenshots by default on supported Android and iOS devices to prevent operating-system AI agents from reading private chats, while acknowledging that the protection is incomplete.
  • 02Signal says users can disable the setting through its privacy and app-security controls, but doing so may expose their Signal content and their contacts’ content to AI agents.
  • 03Signal warned that screenshot blocking cannot prevent AI agents from obtaining data through other routes, including files already on a user’s device.
  • 04Reuters reported that Meta’s Muse AI agent accessed Signal messages on Apple and Android devices by using broad permissions, including macOS accessibility and screenshot tools and Android push-notification access.
  • 05Signal is urging technology companies to provide documented app-level controls that can fully opt apps out of AI data access and processing.

JUSTIFICATION

Signal’s default screenshot-blocking change is a concrete privacy control responding to demonstrated risks from operating-system AI agents with broad permissions. It is broadly relevant to professionals assessing how AI assistants, accessibility privileges, notifications, and screen capture can bypass an app’s normal privacy protections.
