In plain words

Drupal says attackers are actively using a serious flaw that can let them take control of some websites. The risk affects sites whose setup page was left reachable online after an earlier fix, including some live sites, although sites that already locked down that page are not affected. Website owners should update to the fixed Drupal versions, make sure the setup page is protected, and check whether an attacker changed the main administrator account, because a takeover can give an outsider control of the site.

Summary
Drupal warns that CVE-2026-136716 is being actively exploited to take over sites left in vulnerable or incomplete installer states after the Fail2Install fixes. Administrators should immediately update to supported patched releases, run update.php, secure install.php, and check for unauthorized changes to the user 1 account.

Drupal has disclosed CVE-2026-136716, a highly critical Drupal core flaw that is being actively exploited. The issue involves sites in unusual or incomplete installation states that may remain susceptible to site takeover following the earlier CVE-2026-132165, known as Fail2Install. Drupal said the vulnerable condition may not be visible in the user interface, and a site that has already been compromised may no longer appear vulnerable.

The risk applies to sites whose installer, install.php, remained remotely accessible after the earlier fixes; sites that had already secured the installer are not affected. Drupal released fixes in versions 11.4.14, 11.3.23, and 10.6.23, while the listed older end-of-life branches have no fix. Administrators are urged to update, run update.php, secure the installer, review logs for unauthorized activity, and verify control of the user 1 email address and password.

  • Administrators may not be able to tell from the Drupal user interface whether their site is in the vulnerable state, and a previously compromised site may no longer appear vulnerable.
  • The remaining risk can affect production websites that were left with the installer remotely accessible after the earlier Fail2Install fix.
  • Drupal recommends verifying that the user 1 email address is controlled by the site owner and considering a password reset, because unexpected changes involving that account may indicate unauthorized access.
  • StepSecurity reported that roughly 31,500 internet-facing Drupal services were running branches vulnerable to CVE-2026-132165 as of November 26, 2026, with about 15,000 more on end-of-life Drupal 8 and 9 branches.

In plain words

Bitdefender found a scam campaign that uses paid Facebook and Instagram-style ads for free animated wallpaper apps to trick people into downloading software that looks real and works as promised. Behind the scenes, it can take over web-browser access, steal Facebook and Meta business accounts, and in some cases read and erase Gmail or Outlook messages that might warn victims about suspicious logins. Small businesses and creators are especially at risk because criminals can use stolen accounts to run fraudulent ads, spend advertising money, access customer messages, and damage a trusted brand.

Summary
Bitdefender has identified SYS010, a global malvertising campaign that uses fake animated-wallpaper downloads to install managed Chrome, Edge, and Firefox extensions that steal browser cookies and Meta Business account data. The Chrome payload can also read and permanently delete Gmail and Outlook messages, helping attackers conceal account-takeover alerts and recovery codes.

Bitdefender Labs identified SYS010, a malvertising campaign that uses Meta ads for free animated wallpaper software to target Meta Business accounts. The ads direct users to dedicated websites that deliver ZIP archives containing a working wallpaper application alongside hidden malicious components. Bitdefender first identified a malicious SYS010 ad on October 16, 2026, and observed related activity across countries in Asia, Europe, North America, Africa, and South America.

The downloaded application uses a renamed, legitimately signed MeisterTask executable and extracts a PHP-based loader that creates scheduled tasks for persistence and contacts attacker-controlled servers for further instructions. Later-stage scripts abuse Chrome cloud management, Firefox extension policies, and—in selected cases—Windows device management to force-install malicious browser extensions. These extensions can steal browser cookies and Meta advertising, Business Manager, and Page data; the Chrome version can also read recent Gmail and Outlook messages and permanently delete selected messages, potentially concealing security alerts, login codes, and password-reset emails. The campaign can enable fraudulent advertising, unauthorized spending, loss of control over social accounts, exposure of customer conversations, and reputational harm for small businesses and creators.

  • The downloaded wallpaper program is built around a legitimately signed MeisterTask application renamed as Wallpaper Engine.exe, while the visible wallpaper browser genuinely works and obtains its content from an attacker-controlled server.
  • SYS010 moves its most valuable components out of the initial download: the PHP loader contains no theft code and receives later tasks only when the attacker-controlled server selects a particular installation.
  • The campaign uses legitimate browser and Windows management mechanisms to deploy extensions, which the article says can prevent victims from disabling or removing them through normal browser controls.
  • The Chrome extension can wait until no Chrome window is active before accessing email, then permanently delete selected Gmail or Outlook messages so that account-security warnings and login codes may disappear before the victim sees them.
  • Both the loader and browser extension can obtain replacement command servers through authenticated information hosted on services including Telegram and Cloudflare R2, allowing infrastructure changes without updating infected machines.
Key Points
  • 01Bitdefender Labs identified SYS010 as a malvertising campaign that uses Meta ads for free animated wallpaper applications to target Meta Business accounts.
  • 02Victims download a working wallpaper application that silently extracts a PHP-based loader, establishes scheduled-task persistence, and receives later-stage tasks from attacker-controlled servers.
  • 03SYS010 uses browser and device-management features to force-install malicious extensions in Chrome, Firefox, and, under selected conditions, Edge.
  • 04The malicious extensions can steal browser cookies and collect data from Facebook ad accounts, Business Managers, and Pages, allowing attackers to hijack sessions without passwords or two-factor authentication.
  • 05The Chrome extension can read recent Gmail and Outlook messages and permanently delete attacker-selected emails, potentially hiding security alerts, login codes, and password-reset messages.
