In plain words

The article says that Microsoft’s 2026 security report finds that companies are building increasingly complicated webs of accounts, software, cloud services, and AI helpers, creating hidden routes that attackers can use to reach important systems. This affects organizations using cloud services, automated tools, developer systems, and AI agents, because access rights can build up over time and be difficult to track. Microsoft reports that misuse of cloud accounts appeared in 78% of observed activity against critical infrastructure, showing that these connections are already valuable to attackers. The article argues that organizations need to map how access connects across their whole environment and remove unnecessary permissions that could open many routes at once.

Summary
Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report underscores that identity sprawl, cloud permissions, non-human accounts, and AI agents create interconnected attack paths that inventories alone cannot reveal. Organizations should use graph-based attack path management to identify shared privilege choke points and reduce technical debt before AI-enabled attackers can find and exploit it faster.

Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report describes a security environment shaped by interconnected risk, identity sprawl, and AI-driven acceleration. The article highlights that cloud identity abuse appeared in 78% of observed activity against critical infrastructure, while identities, permissions, applications, cloud resources, automation, and AI agents create relationships that can be difficult to see through isolated reviews. Microsoft argues that an inventory alone is insufficient and that graph-based analysis is necessary to understand what identities and automated systems can reach through direct and downstream connections.

AI agents expand the existing identity attack graph because they require access to business systems and can accumulate permissions as their responsibilities change. The article notes that 88% of enterprises are experimenting with agents, and that AI can make long-standing technical debt faster and cheaper for adversaries to discover and combine into usable paths. It also cites AzureHound appearing in 12% of the observed tool mix against critical infrastructure and argues that Attack Path Management should prioritize shared access relationships or trust conditions whose remediation can close many potential paths at once.

  • A permission that appears harmless in isolation can become high risk when it connects through applications, credentials, cloud identities, and downstream systems to sensitive infrastructure.
  • AI agents present the same access-governance problem as service accounts—permissions can accumulate after their original purpose changes—but potentially at much greater scale.
  • AI does not need to create new weaknesses to increase risk; it can make long-standing identity and configuration debt faster and cheaper for adversaries to discover and connect.
  • The most efficient remediation is not reviewing each attack route individually, but removing shared access relationships or trust conditions that thousands or millions of routes depend on.
  • CISA’s use of SharpHound and AzureHound illustrates that relationship mapping can reveal access to sensitive systems that is not apparent from reviewing individual configurations alone.
Key Points
  • 01Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report says that cloud identity abuse appeared in 78% of observed critical-infrastructure activity.
  • 02The report states that inventories alone are insufficient and that graph-based mapping is needed to show how identities, permissions, automation, and resources connect.
  • 03Microsoft reports that 88% of enterprises are experimenting with AI agents, which can accumulate excessive access and add new identities and dependencies to organizational environments.
  • 04AzureHound appeared in 12% of the observed tool mix used against critical infrastructure for mapping cloud attack paths, according to the article.
  • 05Attack Path Management can identify shared relationships across many potential attack paths, allowing a focused remediation to close multiple paths at once.
