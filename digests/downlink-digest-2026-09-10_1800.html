In plain words

A new harmful Android app called Mantax Otax is being sent to people through fake app files and deceptive messages, mainly by operators in Indonesia. Once installed, it can lock up files on older Android phones and demand money, while also reading texts, passwords, contacts, chats, location, and other personal information. It can even take photos, record the screen, and repeatedly show scary messages or play sounds to pressure people into paying. This matters because installing apps from outside Google Play or giving an untrusted app special control of a phone can expose both private data and personal files.

Summary
Mantax Otax is an Android malware spread through malicious APKs that combines file encryption on Android 9 and older with extensive spying, remote control, and harassment capabilities. Avoid sideloaded apps and granting Accessibility permissions; up-to-date devices with Google Play Protect should detect and block it.

Mantax Otax is a newly identified Android malware strain that combines file encryption, data theft, remote control, and harassment functions. Indonesian operators distribute it through malicious APK files hosted outside Google Play, using phishing and social-engineering messages. After installation, it requests Accessibility permissions, giving it extensive control over the device, and sends device details to command-and-control infrastructure obtained from GitHub.

The ransomware component targets Android 9 and older, where it can search shared storage, encrypt selected files with a victim-specific AES key from the command server, delete the originals, and append a .enc extension. It also replaces images with ransom notices and presents a full-screen Firebase-hosted chat for payment negotiations. Zimperium found that a Firebase server misconfiguration exposed attacker communications with victims.

In addition to encrypting files, Mantax Otax can steal lock-screen PINs, SMS messages and one-time passwords, call logs, contacts, browsing history, account information, location data, WhatsApp content, and Telegram chats. It can capture screenshots, record and stream the screen, take photos with device cameras, and use repeated pop-ups, videos, image overlays, and text-to-speech messages to intimidate victims. Zimperium says up-to-date Android devices with active Google Play Protect detect and block the malware, and users are advised not to install APKs from outside Google Play or grant questionable apps Accessibility permissions.

  • Mantax Otax’s file-encryption function is limited largely to Android 9 and earlier because Android 10’s storage restrictions prevent it from broadly encrypting shared files.
  • The malware obtains a victim-specific encryption key from its command server, meaning affected files are encrypted with a key tied to that individual victim.
  • Researchers accessed attacker-victim chats because the malware operators’ Firebase command server was misconfigured, exposing their communications.
  • Beyond stealing data, version 2 uses repeated pop-ups, full-screen videos, sudden image overlays, and device-spoken messages to intimidate victims into paying.
  • The malware’s ability to steal screen-lock PINs is intended to help it retain access to a compromised device.
Key Points
  • 01Mantax Otax is an Android malware strain that combines file encryption, data theft, remote control, and victim-harassment capabilities.
  • 02Indonesian operators distribute Mantax Otax through malicious APK files hosted outside Google Play, using phishing and social-engineering messages.
  • 03The malware encrypts selected shared-storage files on Android 9 and older, deletes the originals, adds a .enc extension, and replaces local images with ransom notices.
  • 04Mantax Otax can steal lock-screen PINs, SMS messages and one-time passwords, call logs, contacts, browsing history, account details, location data, WhatsApp content, and Telegram chats.
  • 05Up-to-date Android devices with active Google Play Protect detect and block Mantax Otax, according to Zimperium.
