Attackers Hijack MikroTik Routers Through Internet-Exposed SSH Without Authentication
thehackernews.com · 06 Sep 2026 09:32 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.5

In plain words

Attackers are taking over some MikroTik routers that have their remote login feature open to the internet, and they can get full control without needing a password. This affects MikroTik devices running the listed vulnerable RouterOS versions, especially where management services like SSH, web access, or bandwidth-test are exposed online. It matters because a taken-over router can be changed without the owner’s knowledge, so users are told to update, limit who can reach these services, and check for unknown accounts or settings.

Summary
Attackers are actively exploiting internet-exposed MikroTik RouterOS SSH services to gain unauthenticated administrative control. MikroTik users should immediately install the fixed RouterOS releases, restrict management access, and check for unauthorized users, scripts, logs, or suspicious device status.

Attackers are exploiting MikroTik routers that have their SSH remote-access service reachable from the internet, according to a September 5 warning from CERT Polska. The attacks can give full administrative control without authentication, and successful attacks date to at least September 2. The article says no victim count or attacker identity was available in The Hacker News’s September 6 review.

MikroTik has published fixed RouterOS releases, and CERT Polska says the fixes prevent the observed attacks. CERT recommends immediate installation of the updates, followed by checks for unauthorized configuration changes. Until updates can be installed, CERT advises turning off exposed services or limiting access to trusted management networks, especially for SSH, WWW/WWW-SSL, and bandwidth-test.

The affected RouterOS ranges reported by CERT include versions from 6.0.0 below 6.49.21, from 7.0.0 below 7.23.4, and from 7.24 below 7.24.2, with a development-channel fix in 7.25beta3. After updating, users are told to inspect logs, run /system/device-mode/print, and look for unknown users, scripts, and other unrecognized changes. CERT also points to unexpected highly privileged ops accounts and account-creation logs containing ssh:-2@ as signs to investigate.

  • Home MikroTik devices should block public access to management ports if their default firewall rules remain intact, so exposed management services may indicate non-default or altered configuration.
  • CERT Polska specifically warns not to clear RouterOS “Flagged” status before preserving evidence and completing analysis, because that status may indicate suspicious startup-detected configuration.
  • CERT Polska identifies unexpected highly privileged “ops” accounts and account-creation logs containing “ssh:-2@” as concrete signs that require investigation.
  • The article notes that although CERT calls the observed two-flaw combination “MikroTrick,” the public disclosures do not explicitly identify which two vulnerabilities are chained or how they combine to provide administrative control.
  • The article states that the available dates do not prove whether a fix was public before attacks began, so the zero-day status remains unverified.

  • 02Successful attacks have been observed since at least September 2, but the warning did not include a victim count or identify the attackers.

Four REVSTEALER-Linked Modules Disable Windows Update and Defender to Run a Crypto Miner
thehackernews.com · 06 Sep 2026 08:34 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.5
TAGSaurastealercmstpelevationkatzetherhidinggoogle-chromelockapphostlumma-stealermicrosoft-defenderpromanagerrevstealerrussiasoftmanagervoidstealerwindows-updatewinupdate

In plain words

Researchers found four hidden programs linked to REVSTEALER, a Windows password-and-data thief spread through fake game cheats, pirated software, and a fake “Claude Opus 5 Free Desktop” app. People who install these lures can have passwords, browser sessions, cryptocurrency wallets, gaming accounts, messages, and files stolen, and one leftover program can turn off Windows Update and weaken Microsoft Defender so it can run a cryptocurrency miner. This matters because the main thief may delete itself and look gone while the leftover programs keep stealing, changing copied crypto addresses, routing attacker traffic through the victim’s internet connection, or using the computer to mine cryptocurrency.

Summary
Elastic researchers found four persistent REVSTEALER-linked Windows modules that remain after the main infostealer deletes itself, including LockAppHost, which disables Windows Update and weakens Microsoft Defender before running a crypto miner. Victims should not assume cleanup is complete after removing the stealer, because the modules can keep stealing wallets, hijacking crypto transactions, proxying traffic, or mining in the background.

Elastic Security Labs documented four previously unreported programs associated with REVSTEALER, an emerging Windows information stealer. The programs are named ProManager, WinUpdate, SoftManager, and LockAppHost, and they remain on an infected machine after the core stealer reports completion and deletes itself. Elastic said the programs share REVSTEALER’s build tradecraft, including the same packer, runtime function resolution, and use of Polygon smart contracts for backup configuration.

The modules have different roles that extend the impact of an infection beyond the initial theft of data. ProManager targets desktop cryptocurrency wallets, WinUpdate replaces copied cryptocurrency addresses and collects likely recovery phrases, SoftManager turns the machine into a reverse proxy, and LockAppHost runs a cryptocurrency miner. LockAppHost is the most disruptive because it gains administrator rights, disables Windows Update services and scheduled tasks, adds Microsoft Defender exclusions, and hides a miner within legitimate Windows processes.

REVSTEALER itself steals browser passwords and cookies, cryptocurrency wallets, gaming accounts, messaging data, VPN and FTP configuration, Windows Credential Manager data, password manager data, and selected documents. It has been spread mainly through game-cheat lures promoted on YouTube channels, and it has also been packaged as pirated or impersonated software, including a fake “Claude Opus 5 Free Desktop” application. Elastic warned that responders should look for surviving modules after the core stealer disappears, re-enable Windows Update components and remove Defender exclusions where LockAppHost has run, and affected users should change passwords and end active account sessions.

  • A REVSTEALER infection can appear resolved because the core stealer reports completion and deletes itself, but separate persistent modules may continue operating on the machine afterward.
  • Elastic did not observe the four modules being delivered onto a live REVSTEALER host, so their connection to REVSTEALER is based on shared build tradecraft and investigative context rather than a captured delivery chain.
  • Systems affected by LockAppHost require more than miner removal because the module leaves behind weakened defenses, including disabled Windows Update components and Microsoft Defender exclusions.
  • Password resets alone may be insufficient for affected users because REVSTEALER steals session cookies and the Chrome App-Bound Encryption key, so active account sessions should also be ended.
  • Elastic’s public YARA coverage includes the core stealer and three modules but does not include a rule for LockAppHost, the module that disables updates and runs the miner.
Key Points
  • 01Elastic Security Labs documented four previously unreported programs associated with REVSTEALER named ProManager, WinUpdate, SoftManager, and LockAppHost that install themselves in the user’s profile and remain after the core stealer deletes itself.
  • 02LockAppHost gains administrator rights, disables Windows Update services and scheduled update tasks, adds Microsoft Defender exclusions, and runs a cryptocurrency miner hidden within legitimate Windows processes.
  • 03ProManager targets desktop cryptocurrency wallets by overlaying attacker-controlled content on wallet windows and recording passwords or passphrases typed or pasted into identified fields.
  • 04WinUpdate watches the clipboard, replaces copied cryptocurrency addresses with the attacker’s addresses, and collects text that looks like a wallet recovery phrase.
  • 05REVSTEALER is distributed mainly through game-cheat lures on YouTube channels and has also been packaged as pirated or impersonated software, including a fake “Claude Opus 5 Free Desktop” application.
