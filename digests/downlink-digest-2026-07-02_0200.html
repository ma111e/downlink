In plain words

Medtronic, a medical device company, is telling affected customers that someone without permission accessed some of its company computer systems and may have seen their personal and health information. The information may include names, contact details, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, and health-related information. This matters because such details can be used for scams, identity theft, and misleading messages, although Medtronic says its medical devices are still safe to use and the stolen data was not posted online.

Summary
Medtronic is notifying customers after ShinyHunters accessed corporate IT systems and exposed personal and health-related data, potentially including names, contact details, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, and health information. The company says its medical devices were not affected and that the stolen data has not been posted online.

Medtronic is notifying affected customers about a data breach that exposed personal data to an unauthorized third party. The company said it became aware of unusual activity on certain corporate IT systems on April 15, 2026. An investigation with third-party cybersecurity experts found that an unauthorized actor accessed certain Medtronic corporate IT systems from April 13 to April 19, 2026.

The exposed data may include full names, contact information, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, and health-related information. The ShinyHunters data extortion group claimed responsibility and said it held 9 million Medtronic records containing personally identifiable information and internal corporate data. The group listed Medtronic on its dark web extortion portal on April 18 and threatened to release the data if a ransom was not paid by April 21.

The Medtronic entry was later removed from ShinyHunters’ listing, and Medtronic said the stolen data was not exposed online. Medtronic also said its devices remain safe to use and were not affected by the cybersecurity incident. Notification recipients are advised to enroll in the offered 24-month credit monitoring and identity theft protection services, watch for suspicious communications, and monitor account activity closely.

  • Affected customers have a concrete mitigation step available because Medtronic is offering 24 months of credit monitoring and identity theft protection services.
  • The article separates the corporate IT breach from device safety, with Medtronic saying its medical devices remain safe to use and were not affected by the incident.
  • Although ShinyHunters threatened to publish allegedly stolen records by April 21, Medtronic says the stolen data was not exposed online after the group’s listing was later removed.
  • The combination of personal, Social Security, and health-related information makes suspicious communications especially important to watch for, because the article warns that exposed data may be used in scams, social engineering, and phishing attempts.
