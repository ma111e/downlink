The exploit was published hours after Microsoft released its July 2026 Patch Tuesday updates and abuses a vulnerability in the Windows User Profile Service that had not yet received a CVE ID. Unlike some previous Nightmare Eclipse releases, the public LegacyHive proof of concept was modified to require additional credentials, making it harder for attackers to weaponize.

In plain words

The European Commission ordered Google to let rival AI assistants use the same deep Android phone abilities that Google’s Gemini can use, including the camera, microphone, screen contents, wake words, and control of other apps, with most changes due by August 2027. This affects Android phone users in Europe, Google, other AI assistant makers, app developers, and rival search services. It matters because it could give people more choice, but it also means outside apps may be able to handle very sensitive phone information like notifications, messages, screenshots, and what is on the screen, raising real privacy and security concerns.

Summary
The European Commission ordered Google to open powerful Android AI-assistant capabilities, including access to the microphone, camera, screen, wake words, and app control, to rival assistants by Android 18 or August 2027. The move aims to curb Google’s gatekeeping under the Digital Markets Act, but it raises significant security and privacy concerns because third-party assistants could gain deep access to sensitive device data.

The European Commission ordered Google to give rival AI assistants the same reach into Android that Gemini already has, including access to the camera, microphone, screen contents, wake word, and the ability to control other apps in the background. Google must ship the changes in the next major release, Android 18, and by 1 August 2027 at the latest. The decision is one of two binding specification decisions adopted under the Digital Markets Act, with the second requiring Google to provide anonymised Search query, click, and ranking data to rival search engines and AI chatbots that do search.

The Android decision covers 11 operating system features, with five placed behind a certification requirement and six opened without certification. The unrestricted features include ambient data, always-on hotword detection, long-press invocation, system-level on-device models, third-party model implementation, and background execution. Ambient data includes microphone input, system audio, camera, screen contents, location, and sensors, continuously and in the background, under the same consent prompts that Google’s own AI services get.

For the restricted features, Google must create a Qualified AI Assistant Programme and allow third-party Trusted Certification Authorities to certify assistants free of charge. The article notes that by August 2027, a certified assistant, or an uncertified one approved by the user, can open an app on a virtual display, read its screen, and click through it while the user does something else. Google objected that the Android decision threatens device security by granting external apps sensitive permissions, while the article highlights that third-party assistants could receive access to notifications, SMS, screen contents, and screenshots.

  • The final Android measures are significantly more favorable to Google than the April draft, adding restricted features, certification authorities, eligibility controls, and later deadlines after Google objected during consultation.
  • Although Google cannot revoke an individual assistant’s certification under the Qualified AI Assistant Programme, the article notes that Google can revoke the Trusted Certification Authority that issued it.
  • Users will be able to bypass the certification requirement for restricted features on a per-service, per-device basis, and Google cannot hide that option behind developer mode.
  • App developers may need to prepare before the Android 18 beta because certified or user-approved assistants will be able to open apps on a virtual display, read their screens, and click through them while the user does something else.
  • The article highlights notification content as a concrete risk channel because SafeBreach previously used notifications to hijack Gemini’s Android Utilities agent through indirect prompt injection.
Key Points
  • 01The European Commission ordered Google to give rival AI assistants the same Android access that Gemini has, including the camera, microphone, screen contents, always-on wake word, and background app control, in Android 18 or by 1 August 2027 at the latest.
  • 02The Android decision covers 11 operating system features, with Google allowed to require certification for five restricted features but barred from restricting access to six others, including ambient data, always-on hotword detection, long-press invocation, on-device models, third-party model implementation, and background execution.
  • 03For the restricted features, Google must create a Qualified AI Assistant Programme, accept certifications from third-party Trusted Certification Authorities, and apply the same conditions to Gemini.
  • 04The Commission also ordered Google to share anonymised Search query, click, and ranking data with rival search engines and AI chatbots that perform search, subject to eligibility rules, contracts, audits, and a cost-based fee.
