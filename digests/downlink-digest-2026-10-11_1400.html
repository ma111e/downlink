Emerging Threat: (CVE-2026-107406) NetScaler ADC and Gateway Remote Code Execution via SAML
cycognito.com · 11 Oct 2026 17:49 UTC openai-codex / gpt-5.6-terra
TAGSadaptive-authenticationcisacitrixcloud-software-groupcve-2026-107406cycognitonetscaler-adcnetscaler-gatewaysamlsecure-private-access

In plain words

The article warns about a serious flaw in NetScaler devices, which many organizations use as internet-facing entry points for employee and application access. It affects devices set up to handle a common sign-in method called SAML, and an outsider could potentially take control of the device or cause it to stop working. Because these devices sit in front of many internal services, a successful break-in could disrupt access or expose systems across a large organization. Organizations need to check their device setup and install Citrix’s updated software, since there is no simpler fix.

Summary
CVE-2026-107406 is a critical NetScaler ADC and Gateway memory-overflow flaw that can enable unauthenticated remote code execution or denial of service on appliances configured for SAML. Organizations should immediately identify SAML-enabled internet-facing NetScaler instances and upgrade to Citrix’s fixed builds, as no workaround is available.

CVE-2026-107406 is a critical memory-overflow vulnerability in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway that can lead to remote code execution or denial of service. It has a CVSS v4.0 score of 9.5 and can be reached over the network without privileges or user interaction, although its attack complexity is rated high. Citrix reported no known unmitigated exploitation at disclosure, and no public proof of concept had been identified when the article was published.

Affected appliances must be configured as a SAML service provider or identity provider, with the affected role depending on the installed build. Citrix has released fixes, including version 14.1-73.46 or later for the 14.1 line and 13.1-64.29 or later for the main 13.1 line, while end-of-life systems require migration because they will not receive patches. Organizations should inventory internet-facing NetScaler devices, confirm their exact builds and SAML settings, and prioritize systems with authentication login panels; external scans cannot reliably determine either the relevant build number or SAML configuration.

  • Whether a NetScaler appliance is affected depends on both its software build and its SAML role: older builds can be affected in either service-provider or identity-provider mode, while specified newer build ranges are affected only in identity-provider mode.
  • External discovery data cannot reliably separate vulnerable NetScaler devices from patched ones because neither exact build numbers nor SAML configuration are generally visible from outside the organization.
  • NetScaler appliances showing an AAA login panel are a practical priority for review because they run authentication virtual servers, where SAML configurations reside, although the panel alone does not prove SAML is enabled.
  • End-of-life appliances will not receive fixes, making migration to a supported version necessary rather than a routine patching task.
  • The 13.1 FIPS and NDcPP branch uses a different build-numbering scheme from the main 13.1 line, creating a risk that administrators could select the wrong upgrade target.
Key Points
  • 01CVE-2026-107406 is a critical memory-overflow flaw in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway that can cause remote code execution or denial of service.
  • 02Only NetScaler appliances configured as a SAML service provider or identity provider are affected, with the affected role varying by software build.
  • 03Citrix has released fixed builds, including 14.1-73.46 or later for the 14.1 line and 13.1-64.29 or later for the main 13.1 line.
  • 04Citrix has published no workaround, so organizations must upgrade affected appliances or remove SAML authentication where it is not required.
  • 05External scanning cannot reliably determine whether a NetScaler appliance is affected because its build number and SAML configuration are not externally visible.

Nippon Columbia malware incident exposes 8.6 million karaoke fan records

In plain words

A harmful computer program was found on an employee’s computer at Nippon Columbia, a company that handles customer information for Japanese karaoke operator Daiichi Kosho. The incident may involve personal details of about 8.63 million customers and 93,000 employees, including names, birthdays, email addresses, and phone numbers. Although no stolen or publicly released data has been confirmed and passwords were not included, affected people could face convincing scam emails, texts, or calls that use their personal information.

Summary
A malware infection at Daiichi Kosho contractor Nippon Columbia may have exposed personal data for 8.63 million karaoke customers and 93,000 employees in Japan. No theft or public leak has been confirmed, and the affected records reportedly did not include passwords or loyalty-point misuse evidence.

Daiichi Kosho, a major Japanese karaoke operator, disclosed that a malware infection at contractor Nippon Columbia Group may have exposed personal information it handles on Daiichi Kosho’s behalf. Nippon Columbia notified Daiichi Kosho on October 5 after finding malware on an employee computer and isolated the affected system the following day. Daiichi Kosho says its own systems were not breached.

The potentially affected data covers 8,631,000 customers and 93,000 employees, including names, genders, dates of birth, email addresses, and telephone numbers. Customers of BIG ECHO, MEGA BIG, Karaoke CLUB DAM, Banana Club, B-GARAGE, and DK Dining may be impacted. Daiichi Kosho has not confirmed that data was stolen or leaked, but advises people to be cautious about unsolicited emails, text messages, or calls seeking payments or sensitive information.

The company said the data does not include passwords and that it has found no evidence of unauthorized loyalty-point use. Nippon Columbia Group reset passwords and other authentication credentials while investigating the cause and scope of the incident and whether data was leaked online. A later Daiichi Kosho update did not add information about whether a leak had occurred.

  • The incident shows that Daiichi Kosho’s customers could be affected through a contractor even though Daiichi Kosho says its own systems were not breached.
  • Because the potentially exposed information includes names, birth dates, email addresses, and phone numbers, Daiichi Kosho specifically advises customers to be cautious about unsolicited emails, text messages, and calls requesting payments or sensitive information.
  • The company reported that passwords were not included and that there is no evidence of unauthorized loyalty-point use, limiting two specific forms of immediate account-related impact.
  • The possible exposure spans customers across multiple Daiichi Kosho brands, including BIG ECHO, MEGA BIG, Karaoke CLUB DAM, Banana Club, B-GARAGE, and DK Dining.
Key Points

  • 02The potentially exposed records include full names, genders, dates of birth, email addresses, and telephone numbers.
  • 03Daiichi Kosho said it has not confirmed data theft or leaks, and Nippon Columbia Group is investigating the incident’s cause, scope, and whether information was leaked online.
  • 04The company said passwords were not included in the exposed data and that it found no evidence of unauthorized loyalty-point use.
  • 05Customers of BIG ECHO, MEGA BIG, Karaoke CLUB DAM, Banana Club, B-GARAGE, and DK Dining may be affected.
