Docker fixed a critical Docker Hub flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-105445 and called GhostPat, that was present from August 26, 2024, until September 30. The issue arose when a personal Docker Hub account was converted into an organization and an existing user was named as its owner. During this process, the original account’s personal access tokens could be transferred to the named owner while retaining their existing secrets. Docker rated the flaw critical with a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.4.

A person could create a token on an account they controlled, convert that account into an organization, name another user as owner, and then use the token at Docker Hub’s registry as that user. Depending on the token’s permissions, this could allow pulling, pushing, or deleting images in the named owner’s repositories and organizations, including publishing images under a legitimate name.
