In plain words

Hackers linked to a Russian group broke into a small Polish heat-and-power plant through a poorly separated private mobile network and shut down equipment that ran a steam turbine and water treatment system. The plant serves heat to about 50,000 residents, but staff restored the systems quickly and the outage did not affect the population. The case matters because Poland’s emergency response team says this may be the first known real-world attack using this path into industrial equipment, and similar setups may be common in other countries.

Summary
Polish CERT says hackers linked to Russia’s Electrum group breached a small Polish heat-and-power plant by moving laterally through a misconfigured private APN, then shut down PLCs controlling a steam turbine and water treatment system. The incident highlights that private APNs should be treated as untrusted networks with client isolation, strict allowlists, and hardened device access.

Hackers compromised a small combined heat-and-power plant in Poland during the destructive cyberattacks that hit the country’s energy sector last year. The plant supplies heat to around 50,000 residents, and the incident shut down a steam turbine and the process-water treatment system. Staff restored the affected systems quickly, so the outage was short-lived and had no impact on the population.

Polish CERT said the attacker used a private Access Point Name to reach the plant’s operational technology network. Investigators determined that the attacker first compromised a FortiGate VPN/firewall at a wind farm and used a Teltonika cellular router to tunnel into a private APN managed by the distribution system operator. The APN lacked client isolation, allowing the attacker to scan for and communicate with devices at other facilities.

CERT Polska said the attacker found a WAGO PFC200 PLC with an exposed web interface and default administrator credentials, enabled SSH, and used it as a bridge into the plant’s operational network. On December 29, the attacker accessed the SCADA interface and Siemens PLCs, switched them into STOP mode, activated password protection, and shut down key plant systems. Polish CERT said this was the first known real-world cyberattack involving access to an OT network through a private APN, and recommended treating private APNs as untrusted external networks.

  • Polish CERT said this was the first known real-world cyberattack in which an attacker reached an operational technology network by moving laterally through a private APN.
  • The same private APN configuration that enabled the attack was found to be common in Poland at the time, and Polish CERT assessed that similar arrangements are likely used internationally.
  • The attacker was able to turn an exposed WAGO PFC200 controller with default administrator credentials into a bridge into the plant’s operational technology network.
  • Polish CERT recommended treating private APNs as untrusted external networks, enabling isolation between connected clients, and using allowlists for essential traffic between APN gateways and operational systems.
